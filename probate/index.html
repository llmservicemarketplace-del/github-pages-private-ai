Probate Records by State: Official Free Court Resources

Last reviewed November 12, 2026. Written by The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit educational resource. General information only, not legal advice. We sell no legal, search, background-check, or filing services.

Probate is handled county by county, and every state organizes its courts differently. Choose a state to see which court keeps estate files, how to search for a case, and how to request a copy of a will, inventory, or closing order.

Before you start: gather the person's full legal name, approximate date of death, county of residence, any case number, and the name of the executor or administrator.

Common questions

Are probate records free to search?

Often the index is free and copies cost money. Some courts charge for online access. Fees vary by county, so ask the clerk before ordering.

Can I find a will online?

Sometimes, but many wills are only in the court file. A filed will may be available from the probate clerk; a will kept at home or with a lawyer may never be filed.

Do I need a lawyer to open an estate?

Not always. Simple estates are often handled without one. Contested wills, minor heirs, businesses, or unclear home titles are good reasons to get advice.
