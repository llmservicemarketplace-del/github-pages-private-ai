How things work in Georgia

Small claims are in Magistrate Court

Georgia doesn't have a court called "small claims." Civil cases for $15,000 or less go to the Magistrate Court in your county. You can file without a lawyer, and the court's forms are usually on the county magistrate court website.

Evictions are "dispossessory" cases

A Georgia eviction case is called a dispossessory. If you're served, you generally have only 7 days to answer, in writing or in person at the court. Missing it can mean a quick judgment. See eviction notices.

Insurance: the 60-day demand

Before suing an insurer for bad-faith penalties under O.C.G.A. 33-4-6, a policyholder generally must send a written demand and wait 60 days. See the Georgia 60-day demand.

Insurance claim problems in Georgia

Georgia Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire
Website: oci.georgia.gov. Consumer line: (800) 656-2298.
The department takes complaints about denied, delayed, or underpaid claims for free.

Questions this guide answers

Who regulates insurance companies in Georgia?

The Georgia Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire. It licenses insurers and adjusters and takes consumer complaints for free.

How do I file an insurance complaint in Georgia?

Contact the Georgia Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire through its website or at (800) 656-2298. Have your policy number, claim number, the company's letters, and a short timeline of what happened.

Where can I find free legal aid in Georgia?

Use LawHelp.org or the Legal Services Corporation's locator and choose Georgia, or call 211.
