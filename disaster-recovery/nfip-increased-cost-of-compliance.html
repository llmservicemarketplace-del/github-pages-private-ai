Increased Cost of Compliance: Flood Insurance Help to Elevate Your Home

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Most National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) policies include Increased Cost of Compliance (ICC) coverage. It pays up to $30,000 to help bring a flood-damaged building up to local flood rules, on top of your regular flood claim.

What ICC can pay for

  • Elevating the home above the required flood height
  • Floodproofing (mainly for non-residential buildings)
  • Relocating the building to higher ground
  • Demolishing the building

Who qualifies

  • You had an NFIP flood policy when the flood happened.
  • Your community officially determines the building is substantially damaged by flood, or it meets the community's repetitive-loss rules.
  • The community requires you to bring the building into compliance.

How to file

  1. Get the written substantial damage determination from your community.
  2. Tell your flood insurer or adjuster you're filing an ICC claim.
  3. Choose your option (elevate, relocate, demolish, or floodproof) and get estimates.
  4. Send the paperwork your insurer asks for. Payments are often made as work progresses.

Deadlines and limits

  • ICC has time limits for finishing the work. Ask your insurer for the current deadline and request extensions in writing if needed.
  • The $30,000 counts toward your policy's total payout limit.
  • Elevating often costs more than $30,000. Ask about FEMA mitigation grants, state programs, and SBA loans to fill the gap.

Frequently asked questions

How much does ICC pay?

Up to $30,000 toward bringing a substantially damaged or repetitively damaged building into compliance with local flood rules.

Do I automatically get ICC coverage?

Most NFIP policies include it. You must have had an NFIP policy at the time of the flood, and your community must determine the building needs to be brought into compliance.

Can ICC be used to elevate my house?

Yes. Elevation is one of the four approved uses, along with floodproofing, relocation, and demolition.
