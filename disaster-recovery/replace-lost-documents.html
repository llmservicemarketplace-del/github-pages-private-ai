How to Replace Documents Lost in a Disaster

Last reviewed November 5, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Floods and fires destroy wallets, file boxes, and safes. You need ID to apply for almost everything else, so replace identity documents first. Here is where to go for each document.

Start with identity documents

Driver's license or state ID

Contact your state's driver's license agency (DMV). Many states let you request a replacement online, and some waive or reduce fees after a declared disaster.

Social Security card

Many people can request a replacement online through a my Social Security account at SSA.gov. You can also call 1-800-772-1213. You usually do not need the card itself to apply for FEMA help; your number is enough.

Birth, death, and marriage certificates

These come from the vital records office in the state where the event happened, not where you live now. The CDC keeps a list of every state office: Where to Write for Vital Records.

U.S. passport

Report a lost or destroyed passport to the State Department, then apply for a replacement in person. Information: travel.state.gov or 1-877-487-2778.

Green card

Apply to USCIS for a replacement (Form I-90). Information: USCIS.gov or 1-800-375-5283.

Health and benefits

  • Medicare card: print or order one at Medicare.gov or call 1-800-633-4227 (1-800-MEDICARE).
  • Medicaid or SNAP cards: contact your state agency. After disasters, some states offer replacement SNAP benefits for food lost in power outages if you apply within a short window.
  • Prescriptions: call your pharmacy. Your prescription records are electronic, and many states allow emergency refills after a disaster.

Money and property

  • Tax returns: free transcripts at IRS.gov/Get Transcript. For full copies, use Form 4506. The IRS waives the usual fee for people in federally declared disaster areas; write the disaster designation at the top of the form. IRS help line: 1-800-829-1040.
  • Savings bonds: paper bonds can be replaced through TreasuryDirect. Information: TreasuryDirect.gov or 1-844-284-2676.
  • Deeds and mortgage papers: your county recorder or clerk of court keeps recorded deeds. Your mortgage company can send your loan documents.
  • Insurance policies: call your agent or the company. Ask for a full copy of the policy, including the declarations page and every endorsement, since you will need it for your claim.
  • Bank and credit cards: call the bank or card company to replace cards and get statements.

Military records

Veterans can request a DD-214 and other records from the National Archives at archives.gov/veterans or 1-866-272-6272.

Protect the replacements

  • Photograph or scan each document and store copies somewhere off-site.
  • Keep originals in a waterproof, fire-resistant container, up off the floor.
  • Make a one-page list of policy numbers, account numbers, and phone numbers to keep with your go-bag.

Next step: apply for FEMA assistance.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need my Social Security card to apply for FEMA?

Usually not. FEMA needs the Social Security number of an adult in the household, not the physical card.

Where do I get a new birth certificate after a disaster?

From the vital records office in the state where you were born. The CDC's Where to Write for Vital Records page lists each state office.

Can I get free copies of my tax returns after a disaster?

Tax transcripts are always free online. The IRS also waives the fee for full copies (Form 4506) for people in federally declared disaster areas.
