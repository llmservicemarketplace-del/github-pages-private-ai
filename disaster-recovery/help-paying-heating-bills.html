Help Paying Heating and Utility Bills (LIHEAP)

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

The Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) is a federal program, run by states and local agencies, that helps households pay heating and cooling bills. It can also help if your power or gas has been shut off or is about to be. LIHEAP is funded for this winter, but money is limited and many areas give priority to older adults, people with disabilities, and families with young children, so apply early.

What LIHEAP can help with

  • Heating bills in winter and cooling bills in summer
  • Energy crisis help to stop a shutoff or reconnect service after one
  • In many states, repairing or replacing an unsafe heating system, and weatherization

Payments usually go directly to your utility or fuel company, not to you.

Who qualifies

Each state sets its own income limits and rules. In general you may qualify if your household income is low compared to your state's limit, or if someone in your household already gets SNAP, SSI, or certain other benefits. You can rent or own, and you don't need a past-due bill to apply in most places.

How to apply

  1. Find your local office. Call the National Energy Assistance Referral line at 1-866-674-6327 (weekdays 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. Eastern) or search EnergyHelp.us. You can also call 211.
  2. Check your state's dates. Each state opens on its own schedule. In Georgia, the heating program typically opens first for residents 65 and older or medically homebound, then for everyone else. In Florida, apply through your county's local provider.
  3. Gather documents: photo ID, Social Security numbers for household members, proof of income for the last month or so, and your most recent utility or fuel bill.
  4. Apply as early as possible. Many programs are first-come, first-served until the money runs out.

If you've gotten a shutoff notice

  • Call your utility right away and ask about payment plans, budget billing, and medical or winter shutoff protections. Rules vary by state.
  • Tell the LIHEAP office it's an emergency. Crisis help is often handled faster.
  • If someone in your home uses powered medical equipment, read medical equipment during a power outage.

Stay safe while you wait

If your heat is off, never use a gas oven, grill, or generator indoors for heat. See how to stay warm without power and carbon monoxide warning signs. Free home weatherization can lower future bills; see free weatherization help.

Avoid LIHEAP scams

LIHEAP never charges a fee to apply and doesn't give cash grants by phone or text. If someone asks you for a fee or your bank information to "release" a LIHEAP grant, hang up and report it to the HHS fraud hotline at 1-800-447-8477.

Frequently asked questions

How do I apply for LIHEAP?

Call the National Energy Assistance Referral line at 1-866-674-6327, visit EnergyHelp.us, or call 211 to find your local LIHEAP office. Each state runs its own application.

Can LIHEAP stop my power from being shut off?

LIHEAP crisis assistance can help pay a past-due bill to prevent a shutoff or restore service. Tell the office it's an emergency and call your utility about payment plans.

Does LIHEAP charge a fee?

No. Applying is free. Anyone asking for a fee or bank information to release a LIHEAP grant is running a scam.
