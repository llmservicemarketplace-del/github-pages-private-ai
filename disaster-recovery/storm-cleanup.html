Find Storm Cleanup Help

Clearing storm debris, downed limbs, and waterlogged drywall is physically demanding and hazardous. Whether managing yard wreckage or interior water damage, understanding volunteer relief avenues, municipal curbside guidelines, and safety gear requirements helps you make steady progress while protecting your health.

Volunteer Disaster Cleanup Networks

  • Crisis Cleanup Hotline: During activated disasters, Crisis Cleanup (call 844-965-1386 or visit crisiscleanup.org when operational) connects property owners with vetted volunteer relief organizations. Services may include debris removal, interior muck-out, and tree cutting based on volunteer capacity. Assistance is provided free of charge, though response depends on volunteer numbers and priority needs.
  • 211 Directory: Dial 211 or visit 211.org to find local charitable and faith-based disaster response teams operating in your community.
  • Response Times: Volunteer organizations prioritize older adults, individuals with disabilities, and low-income survivors. Registering with a hotline places your home in the shared queue, but response cannot be guaranteed.

Municipal Curbside Debris Sorting Rules

Common Curbside Separation Categories

  • Vegetative Debris: Tree limbs, branches, logs, and brush. Avoid plastic bags unless explicitly allowed by your local government.
  • Construction & Demolition (C&D): Drywall, lumber, carpet, insulation, and roofing shingles.
  • White Goods & Large Appliances: Refrigerators, freezers, stoves, and water heaters. Empty all contents and tape doors securely shut to protect neighborhood children.
  • Household Hazardous Waste (HHW): Paints, motor oils, household cleaners, lawn chemicals, and batteries.
  • Electronics: Televisions, computers, and monitors.

Place debris at the curb edge, keeping it clear of fire hydrants, water meters, mailboxes, and low-hanging utility lines.

Flood Cleanup & Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Respirators: Wear a NIOSH-approved N95 or P100 respirator to reduce inhalation of mold spores and airborne dust. Standard cloth or paper masks provide minimal protection against spores.
  • Protective Gloves & Footwear: Wear heavy-duty waterproof gloves and sturdy, steel-shank rubber boots to guard against puncture wounds from hidden nails and debris.
  • Muck-Out Considerations: Remove porous materials such as water-soaked carpet, padding, and saturated drywall promptly to mitigate mold proliferation. Clean hard, non-porous surfaces with appropriate disinfectants.

Avoiding Contractor Fraud After Disasters

  • Always verify that the contractor holds a valid local license and carries both general liability and workers' compensation insurance. Request a certificate of insurance and verify coverage with the issuer.
  • Obtain written, itemized estimates outlining labor, materials, and disposal responsibilities before authorizing work.
  • Deposit requirements vary; check state contractor licensing rules. Avoid paying full contract amounts or excessive cash deposits before work commences.
  • Be cautious before signing any Assignment of Benefits (AOB) agreement that surrenders direct control of your insurance claim to a third party.
