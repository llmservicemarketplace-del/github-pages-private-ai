Septic System After a Flood

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

When the ground floods, your septic drainfield can't absorb water, and wastewater can back up into your home or yard. Acting too fast, especially pumping the tank too early, can make things worse.

While the ground is still flooded

  • Use as little water as possible. Every flush and load of laundry adds to the system. Hold off on laundry and long showers.
  • Don't pump the tank yet. Pumping while the soil is saturated can make an empty tank float up out of the ground or collapse.
  • Don't dig or drive over the tank or drainfield while the soil is wet.
  • If your system has electrical parts, such as a pump or aerator, turn off power to them before going near them.

Signs your septic system is in trouble

  • Toilets and drains slow down or gurgle
  • Sewage backs up into tubs, sinks, or floor drains
  • Soggy ground or sewage smell over the drainfield after floodwater recedes

If sewage backed up into your home, see sewage backup cleanup.

After the water goes down

  1. Wait until the groundwater level drops below the drainfield before using the system normally.
  2. Have a licensed septic professional inspect the tank, pipes, and drainfield. Silt and debris may need to be pumped out once the ground is drier.
  3. Have any pumps or electrical parts checked by a professional before turning them back on.
  4. Keep cutting water use for a while so the drainfield can recover.

Protect your drinking water

A flooded septic system can contaminate nearby private wells. If you have a well, read well water after a flood before drinking it.

Paying for repairs

Damage from rising floodwater is usually covered by flood insurance, not homeowners insurance. See flood insurance basics. After a declared disaster, FEMA may help repair a septic system needed to make your home livable; see the FEMA application guide.

Frequently asked questions

Should I pump my septic tank after a flood?

Not while the ground is still saturated. Pumping too early can make the tank float out of the ground. Wait for the water table to drop, then have a professional inspect and pump it.

Can I use my toilet if my septic system flooded?

Use as little water as possible until floodwater recedes and the drainfield drains. If drains back up, stop using them and call a septic professional.

Does FEMA pay for septic repair?

After a declared disaster, FEMA may help repair a septic system needed to make your home safe to live in. Apply and list the damage.
