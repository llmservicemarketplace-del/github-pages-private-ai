Replacement Cost vs. Actual Cash Value (and Recoverable Depreciation)

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Many homeowners are surprised when the first insurance check is far below the repair estimate. Usually, that's depreciation. Whether you can get that money back depends on whether your policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value.

The two ways policies pay

Replacement cost (RCV)Actual cash value (ACV)
PaysWhat it costs to repair or replace with similar new materialsReplacement cost minus depreciation (age and wear)
DepreciationUsually held back at first, then paid once you repairNot paid back
Common forMany homeowners policies on the house itselfOlder roofs, some contents, lower-cost policies

Check your declarations page and any roof endorsement. Some policies pay replacement cost on the house but actual cash value on the roof.

How replacement cost claims are usually paid

  1. First payment: the actual cash value, meaning replacement cost minus depreciation, minus your deductible.
  2. Do the repair and keep the invoice and proof of payment.
  3. Second payment: send the final invoice to the insurer to claim the recoverable depreciation that was held back.

Example

A roof costs $15,000 to replace. The insurer depreciates it by $5,000 for age, and your deductible is $1,000. The first check is about $9,000. After the roof is replaced and you send the invoice, you can claim the $5,000 held back.

Don't miss the deadline

Most policies give you a set time to finish repairs and claim the held-back depreciation, often 180 days to a year or more, and some states set minimums. Read your policy. If repairs are delayed by contractor backlogs, ask the insurer in writing for an extension.

If the depreciation seems too high

  • Ask for the line-item estimate showing how depreciation was calculated.
  • Challenge wrong ages or conditions with receipts or photos.
  • Labor depreciation is treated differently in different states. Ask your state insurance department.

If you disagree on the amount, see the appraisal clause or what to do when a claim is denied or underpaid.

Frequently asked questions

What is recoverable depreciation?

It's the part of a replacement cost claim the insurer holds back at first. You get it after you finish the repair and send proof of the cost.

Why is my insurance check less than the estimate?

Usually because the first payment is actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation and your deductible. With a replacement cost policy, you can claim the held-back depreciation after repairs.

Does my mortgage company get the depreciation check?

If your lender is named on the policy, insurance checks are often made out to both you and the lender. See our guide to insurance checks and mortgage companies.
