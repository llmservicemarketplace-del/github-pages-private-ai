Sump Pump Failure: Prevention and What to Do

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

A sump pump moves water out of your basement or crawl space before it can flood. They tend to fail at the worst time: during heavy rain, often when the power goes out. A little maintenance and a backup can prevent a flooded basement.

Why sump pumps fail

  • Power outage, the most common cause during storms
  • Stuck or tangled float switch
  • Clogged inlet screen or frozen or blocked discharge line
  • Pump too small for the amount of water
  • Old age; many pumps last only several years to about 10

Test and maintain it

  1. A few times a year, and before storm season, slowly pour a bucket of water into the pit. The pump should turn on, drain the pit, and shut off.
  2. Check that the float moves freely.
  3. Clean debris from the pit and inlet screen.
  4. Make sure the discharge pipe drains well away from the house and isn't frozen or clogged.
  5. Make sure the pump is plugged into a working GFCI outlet.

Add a backup

  • Battery backup pump: takes over when power fails. Test the battery regularly and replace it as recommended.
  • Water-powered backup: uses city water pressure, no electricity needed. Check local codes.
  • Generator: keep it outside and use a transfer switch. See generator safety.
  • High-water alarm: a cheap alarm warns you when water rises in the pit.

If your basement floods

  • Don't walk into standing water if the power is on. See electrical safety after a flood.
  • Photograph everything before cleanup.
  • Remove water fast, then dry the space with fans and dehumidifiers within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. See mold after a flood.

Does insurance cover it?

Standard homeowners policies often exclude water that backs up or overflows from a sump pump unless you added a water backup endorsement. Flood insurance covers surface flooding but has limits on basements. Check your policy. See sewage backup and flood insurance basics.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my sump pump fail during a storm?

The most common cause is a power outage. Stuck floats, clogged lines, undersized pumps, and old age are other common causes.

How do I test a sump pump?

Slowly pour a bucket of water into the pit. The pump should start, drain the pit, and shut off on its own.

Does homeowners insurance cover sump pump failure?

Often only if you have a water backup endorsement. Check your policy, and ask your agent about adding it.
