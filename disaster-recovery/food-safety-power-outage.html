Food Safety After a Power Outage: What to Keep and What to Throw Out

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal, medical, or professional advice. Rules can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

When the electricity goes out, keeping your refrigerator and freezer doors closed is your primary defense against food spoilage. Perishable foods stored above 40 degrees Fahrenheit for more than 2 hours must be thrown away to prevent serious foodborne illness. By checking food temperatures and following established time limits, you can protect your household and avoid food waste.

How Long Will Food Stay Safe Without Power?

According to federal food safety experts at the USDA, modern insulated appliances can keep cold air inside for extended periods if doors remain shut. During an unexpected power outage, every time you open the appliance door, cold air rushes out and accelerates bacterial growth.

  • Refrigerator: A tightly closed refrigerator will keep food cold and safe for about 4 hours.
  • Full freezer: A freezer packed full with frozen goods will keep a safe temperature for approximately 48 hours.
  • Half-full freezer: A freezer filled halfway will hold its safe temperature for about 24 hours.

The 2-Hour Temperature Rule for Refrigerated Items

Bacteria multiply fastest in warm conditions. Any perishable food that has spent more than 2 hours above 40°F must be discarded. Never taste a food item to check if it has gone bad.

Federal health agencies urge families to remember one golden rule: "When in doubt, throw it out."

Perishable Foods to Throw Away

Throw out these items if your refrigerator has been without power for over 4 hours or if their temperature rose above 40°F for more than 2 hours:

  • Raw, cooked, or leftover red meat, poultry, seafood, or lunch meats
  • Milk, cream, yogurt, sour cream, and soft cheeses (such as mozzarella, brie, or cottage cheese)
  • Opened cans or cartons of baby formula
  • Fresh eggs, egg substitutes, and egg-rich dishes like custard or quiches
  • Cooked pasta, cooked rice, potatoes, and casseroles
  • Cut fresh melons, leafy greens, tomatoes, and open jars of mayonnaise or creamy dressings

Foods That Are Usually Safe to Keep

Certain items resist bacterial growth longer and remain safe even after temporary temperature increases:

  • Hard cheeses (such as cheddar, parmesan, and swiss in block form)
  • Butter and margarine
  • Uncut, raw fresh vegetables and whole fresh fruits
  • Opened fruit juices and dried fruits
  • High-acid condiments like mustard, ketchup, pickles, relish, and soy sauce
  • Breads, rolls, bagels, cakes, and fruit pies without dairy or custard toppings

Freezer Food: When You Can Refreeze

Once power is restored, check your freezer right away before turning dial settings:

  1. Check for ice crystals: Food that still contains visible ice crystals throughout can be safely refrozen or cooked.
  2. Check the thermometer: If the freezer thermometer reads 40°F or below, the items are safe to refreeze, though there may be a slight loss in texture or flavor.
  3. Discard warm items: If frozen poultry, fish, or meat have completely thawed and remained above 40°F for more than 2 hours, throw them into an outdoor trash bin immediately.

Refrigerated Medicines and Baby Formula

Power cuts create special risks for medical supplies and infants. Plan ahead with these safety steps:

  • Prescriptions: Drugs like insulin, biologics, and liquid antibiotics often require refrigeration. If electricity is out for an extended period, contact a licensed pharmacist right away. Do not discontinue vital medication until you speak to a medical professional.
  • Infant feedings: Use single-serve ready-to-feed formula cans whenever clean water or refrigeration is unavailable. Throw out prepared formula that has sat at room temperature for more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour after a feeding starts.

Frequently asked questions

Can I smell or taste food to see if it is still good?

Never taste food to determine if it is safe. Bacteria that cause severe food poisoning can multiply rapidly without changing the smell, appearance, or taste of food.

Can I refreeze partially thawed food from the freezer?

Yes, you can safely refreeze food if it still contains visible ice crystals or if an appliance thermometer confirms the food stayed at 40 degrees Fahrenheit or lower.

What should I do with refrigerated insulin or other prescription medications?

Do not throw them out immediately, but do not risk using expired or warm medication. Contact your local pharmacist or doctor right away for guidance on specific drug stability.

Can I claim spoiled food on my homeowners or renters insurance?

Some policies provide limited food spoilage coverage, so check yours. Take clear photos of your full refrigerator and freezer contents before throwing anything away to help support a claim.
