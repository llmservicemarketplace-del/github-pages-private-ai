Disaster Unemployment Assistance (DUA)

Last reviewed November 5, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

If a declared disaster cost you your job, your hours, or your self-employment income, Disaster Unemployment Assistance (DUA) may pay weekly benefits. It covers many people regular unemployment does not, including self-employed workers, gig workers, and farmers.

Who can qualify

DUA is only available after a federal major disaster declaration that includes it. You may qualify if, because of the disaster:

  • Your workplace was damaged, destroyed, or closed
  • You cannot get to work because of the disaster
  • You were about to start a job and could not
  • You were injured in the disaster and cannot work
  • You became the main earner because the head of household died in the disaster
  • Your self-employment income or business was lost or disrupted

You generally must not qualify for regular state unemployment. If you are not sure, apply anyway; the state will check regular unemployment first and then consider you for DUA.

The deadline: 30 days

You usually must apply within 30 days after your state announces that DUA is available. That is often sooner than FEMA's deadline, so do not wait. Late applications are accepted only with good cause.

How to apply

Apply through your state unemployment agency, not through FEMA:

  • Georgia: Georgia Department of Labor
  • Florida: FloridaCommerce Reemployment Assistance
  • Other states: find your state agency through the U.S. Department of Labor's unemployment benefits finder

Your state's announcement will say which counties are covered and how to file.

Proof you will need

Have your Social Security number and your employer's name and address ready. You will also need proof that you were working, self-employed, or about to start work when the disaster hit, along with proof of income from the most recent tax year. Examples include tax returns, W-2s or 1099s, pay stubs, bank statements, and business records. Federal rules generally give you 21 days after filing to submit proof of employment, or your benefits can be stopped and recovered.

If your records were destroyed, see how to replace lost documents. Free IRS transcripts can serve as proof of income.

Keep certifying every week

Like regular unemployment, DUA is paid week by week. File your weekly claims on time and report any work or income. Benefits end when you are back at work or when the disaster assistance period ends.

Other help to apply for at the same time

  • FEMA Individual Assistance for housing and other needs
  • SBA disaster loans for homeowners, renters, and small businesses
  • Local disaster help from charities and agencies

Frequently asked questions

Can self-employed people get Disaster Unemployment Assistance?

Yes. DUA covers self-employed workers, gig workers, and farmers who lost income because of a declared disaster and do not qualify for regular unemployment.

What is the deadline to apply for DUA?

Usually 30 days after your state announces that DUA is available for your disaster. Check your state unemployment agency's announcement.

Where do I apply for DUA?

Through your state unemployment agency, not FEMA. For example, the Georgia Department of Labor or FloridaCommerce.
