Ice Dams: Why Your Roof Leaks in Winter

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

An ice dam is a ridge of ice along the edge of the roof that traps melting snow behind it. The trapped water backs up under shingles and leaks into ceilings and walls. Ice dams are a symptom of heat escaping into the attic, which is the part you can fix.

What causes an ice dam

Warm air leaking from the living space heats the roof deck. Snow on the upper roof melts and runs down to the colder eaves, where it refreezes. Each melt-and-freeze cycle builds the dam higher, and water pools behind it.

Warning signs

  • Thick ice along the gutters and roof edge, with large icicles
  • Water stains or peeling paint on ceilings near outside walls
  • Water dripping inside exterior walls or around window frames

If an ice dam is leaking now

  1. Catch the water inside with buckets and towels. Move belongings. See roof leak first steps.
  2. Clear snow from the lower roof from the ground with a long-handled roof rake. Stay off ladders on ice and never climb onto an icy roof.
  3. Don't chip at the ice with hammers, axes, or shovels. You can easily damage shingles and gutters.
  4. Make a melt channel. A common method is to fill a nylon stocking with calcium chloride ice melt and lay it across the dam so it melts a path for water to drain. Avoid rock salt, which can damage roofing and plants.
  5. Call a professional for steam removal of large dams. Use a licensed, insured contractor.

Document the damage

Photograph the ice dam from outside and every interior stain and drip. Save receipts for buckets, fans, and professional ice removal. See our damage documentation guide.

Prevent ice dams next winter

  • Seal air leaks from the living space into the attic, around light fixtures, vents, chimneys, and attic hatches.
  • Add attic insulation so heat stays in the house instead of warming the roof.
  • Improve attic ventilation so the roof deck stays cold.
  • Keep gutters clear before winter, and rake snow off the lower roof after big storms.

Does insurance cover ice dam leaks?

Many homeowners policies cover interior water damage caused by an ice dam, but often not the cost of removing the ice itself or fixing the attic insulation. Report the leak promptly and keep records showing you acted to limit the damage. Our guide to temporary repairs insurance usually pays for explains what to save.

Frequently asked questions

What causes ice dams on a roof?

Heat escaping into the attic melts snow on the upper roof. The water refreezes at the colder roof edge, forming a dam that traps more water behind it.

Should I chip ice dams off my roof?

No. Chipping can damage shingles and gutters, and climbing on an icy roof is dangerous. Rake snow from the ground and call a professional for steam removal.

Does homeowners insurance cover ice dam damage?

Interior water damage from an ice dam is often covered, but removing the ice and fixing insulation usually is not. Check your policy and report the leak quickly.
