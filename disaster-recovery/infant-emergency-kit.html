Emergency Kit for Babies

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal, medical, tax, insurance, or professional advice. Rules and programs can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

What caregivers should prepare for infants and young children during evacuations and outages: feeding, safe water, diapers, medicine, comfort, identification, and safe sleep.

Immediate danger: Call 911 and follow local emergency instructions. This page does not dispatch responders or replace individualized professional advice.

Pack several days of essentials

Include diapers, wipes, clothing, blankets, feeding supplies, prescribed medicine, copies of records, and familiar comfort items.

Plan safe feeding

Follow public-health instructions for water safety. Ready-to-feed formula may be useful when safe water is limited; never dilute formula.

Protect safe sleep

Use an age-appropriate firm, flat sleep surface without loose bedding. Do not improvise unsafe sleep spaces during sheltering.

Keep identification together

Carry caregiver contacts, recent photos, medical information, and authorized pickup details in a waterproof pouch.

Make a simple action plan

Write down the first safe action, who to call, and which records or supplies may be needed. Keep the plan short enough to use under stress. Share it with everyone in the household, including caregivers and trusted neighbors. Store one copy on a phone and another copy somewhere that can be reached if power or internet service fails.

Document what happens

When it is safe, take clear photographs and write the date, time, location, and names of people contacted. Save receipts, estimates, notices, reference numbers, and written instructions. Do not enter a dangerous area or disturb damaged property only to take pictures. Good records can make later conversations with emergency officials, insurers, contractors, or assistance programs easier.

Confirm local instructions

Disaster conditions and rules can change quickly. Check county emergency management, public safety, public health, utility, insurer, manufacturer, or program guidance that applies to the situation. Use official phone numbers and web addresses. If two instructions conflict, ask the responsible agency or a qualified professional to clarify before acting.

Help household members use the plan

Use plain language and divide tasks into small steps. Consider children, older adults, disabilities, medical equipment, service animals, pets, transportation limits, and language access. Never delay an evacuation or emergency call while gathering documents. Safety comes first; paperwork and replacement supplies can be addressed after immediate danger has passed.

Frequently asked questions

How often should supplies be rotated?

Check sizes, food, medicine, batteries, and expiration dates every few months.

Can homemade infant formula be used in an emergency?

Do not use homemade formula. Contact a pediatric clinician, WIC, health department, shelter, or emergency resource for safe feeding help.
