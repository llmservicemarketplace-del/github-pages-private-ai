Electrical Safety After a Flood

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Water and electricity are a deadly mix. After a flood, the wiring, outlets, breaker panel, and appliances in your home may be damaged even after they look dry. Getting power back on safely is about patience and the right inspection.

Before you go in

  • Never step into standing water if the power might be on. Water can be electrically charged from outlets, cords, or appliances.
  • If you can reach the main breaker without standing in water or on a wet floor, turn it off. Otherwise, call your utility to disconnect power at the meter.
  • Stay far away from downed power lines and anything touching them. Assume every downed line is live and call 911 or your utility.
  • Watch for sparks, burning smells, or buzzing sounds. If you notice them, leave and call for help.

Get it inspected before power comes back

Have a licensed electrician check your home before turning the power back on. Many local building departments require an inspection after flooding. Electrical equipment that was underwater often needs to be replaced, not just dried, because water leaves behind mud and corrosion that can cause shocks or fires later. That often includes:

  • The breaker panel, breakers, and fuses
  • Outlets, switches, and GFCI devices
  • Wiring and cables that were submerged
  • Water heaters, furnaces, AC units, and well pumps

Appliances and electronics

Don't plug in or turn on any appliance that got wet until a qualified technician checks it. Throw out flooded extension cords and power strips. Photograph everything before discarding it.

Generators

Never plug a generator into a wall outlet; it can backfeed the power lines and electrocute line workers or neighbors. Use a transfer switch installed by an electrician, and keep the generator outside and far from windows. See generator safety.

Paying for repairs

Flood insurance can cover electrical systems and appliances damaged by flooding. Keep the electrician's report and receipts. See flood insurance basics, flood cleanup, and hiring a contractor after a storm.

Frequently asked questions

When is it safe to turn the power on after a flood?

After a licensed electrician inspects the wiring, panel, and outlets. Many areas require an inspection before power is restored.

Do outlets need to be replaced after a flood?

Electrical devices that were underwater, including outlets, switches, breakers, and panels, usually need to be replaced rather than dried and reused.

Can I walk in floodwater if the power is out?

Avoid it. Power may come back on or be fed from another source, and floodwater can hide debris and sewage.
