Mobile Home Storm Safety

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Mobile and manufactured homes are much more likely to be damaged or destroyed by high winds than site-built homes. Even newer, properly anchored homes are not safe places to ride out a tornado or a hurricane. The key is having a plan to get somewhere sturdier before the storm hits.

Tornadoes: leave

When a tornado watch is issued, get ready to leave. When a tornado warning is issued, go immediately to a sturdy building or storm shelter you picked ahead of time. Don't wait to see or hear the tornado. If there's truly no shelter nearby, lie flat in a ditch or low spot away from the home and trees, and cover your head. See tornado safety.

Hurricanes: evacuate

People in mobile homes are often told to evacuate even if they live outside storm surge zones. Follow local orders and leave early. See the hurricane prep checklist and how to find a shelter.

Make a plan now

  • Find the nearest sturdy building or community storm shelter. Many mobile home parks have one; ask the manager.
  • Know how long it takes to get there, and plan to leave early.
  • Sign up for weather alerts and keep a NOAA weather radio.
  • Keep a go bag ready. See our go bag checklist.

Tie-downs and anchors

Tie-downs and anchors help keep a home from sliding, rolling, or lifting off its foundation in wind, and many states require them. They reduce damage, but they don't make a mobile home safe to stay in during a tornado or hurricane.

  • Have a licensed installer check anchors and straps, especially on older homes.
  • Replace rusted or loose straps and tighten them as needed.
  • Secure skirting, sheds, porches, and steps, which can blow loose.
  • Bring in or tie down outdoor furniture and grills before storms.

After a storm

  • Check for gas leaks, downed lines, and shifted or damaged supports before going inside. See what to do if you smell gas.
  • Photograph and video all damage, inside and out, before cleanup.
  • Cover openings to prevent more damage. See the roof tarp guide.
  • Report the claim to your insurer. After a declared disaster, apply for FEMA assistance.

Frequently asked questions

Is a mobile home safe in a tornado?

No. Even anchored mobile homes can be destroyed by tornadoes. Go to a sturdy building or storm shelter you picked ahead of time.

Do tie-downs make a mobile home safe in a hurricane?

Tie-downs reduce damage but don't make a mobile home safe to stay in. Follow evacuation orders and leave early.

Where should I go if I live in a mobile home and a tornado warning is issued?

Go immediately to the nearest sturdy building or community storm shelter. If there's none, lie flat in a low spot away from the home and trees.
