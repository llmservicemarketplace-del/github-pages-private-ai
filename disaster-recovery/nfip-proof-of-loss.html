NFIP Flood Insurance Proof of Loss

Last reviewed November 9, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

If you have flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), you must send a signed, sworn proof of loss to get paid. Missing the deadline can cost you your claim, even if the damage is real, so it's one of the most important dates in your recovery.

What a proof of loss is

A proof of loss is your signed and sworn statement of how much you're claiming for building and contents damage. It's separate from reporting your claim. Your adjuster usually gives you a form along with their damage estimate, but it's your responsibility to make sure it's submitted on time.

The deadline: 60 days

  • Under the standard flood policy, the proof of loss is due within 60 days after the date of loss.
  • After major disasters, FEMA sometimes extends this deadline. Never assume it has. Confirm in writing with your insurer.
  • If you don't agree with the adjuster's numbers, you can still submit your own signed proof of loss with your own amount and supporting documents before the deadline.

What to include

  • The amount you're claiming for the building and for contents
  • Photos and video of damage and the water line
  • Contractor estimates, receipts, and an itemized list of damaged belongings. See home inventory checklist.

Keep a copy of everything, and proof of when and how you sent it (certified mail receipt or email confirmation).

Found more damage later?

You can file a supplemental claim with a new proof of loss for additional damage you discover. Do it as soon as you find it.

If your claim is denied or underpaid

  1. Appeal to FEMA within 60 days of the date on your denial letter. FEMA typically decides within about 90 days after it has everything it needs.
  2. Lawsuit deadline: you have 1 year from the insurer's first written denial to sue in federal court. Filing an appeal does not extend this 1-year deadline.

Free legal help may be available. See free legal help after a disaster.

Frequently asked questions

What is the deadline for an NFIP proof of loss?

Within 60 days after the date of loss, unless FEMA announces an extension. Confirm any extension in writing with your insurer.

Can I submit a proof of loss if I disagree with the adjuster?

Yes. You can submit your own signed proof of loss with your own amount and supporting documents before the deadline.

How long do I have to sue over a denied flood claim?

One year from the insurer's first written denial. A FEMA appeal does not extend that deadline.
