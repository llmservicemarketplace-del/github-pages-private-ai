Tsunami Safety: Warning Signs and What to Do

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

A tsunami is a series of powerful ocean waves, usually caused by an undersea earthquake. Waves can arrive within minutes of a nearby earthquake, and the danger can last for hours. Knowing the natural warning signs can save your life.

Natural warning signs: act immediately

If you're near the coast and notice any of these, don't wait for an official warning. Move to high ground or inland right away:

  • Strong earthquake shaking that makes it hard to stand, or shaking that lasts a long time
  • The ocean suddenly pulling back and exposing the sea floor
  • A loud roar from the ocean, like a train or jet

If you feel strong shaking, first Drop, Cover, and Hold On until it stops, then go. See earthquake safety.

Official alerts

  • Tsunami warning: a dangerous tsunami is possible or expected. Move to high ground or inland now.
  • Tsunami advisory: strong currents and waves are expected. Stay out of the water and away from beaches and harbors.
  • Tsunami watch: a tsunami may be coming. Stay tuned and be ready to act.

Where to go

  • Go to high ground or as far inland as you can, on foot if possible. Roads may be jammed or damaged.
  • Follow tsunami evacuation route signs if your area has them.
  • If you can't get to high ground, go to an upper floor of a sturdy, tall building as a last resort.
  • If you're on a boat, officials may advise heading to deep water; follow harbor and Coast Guard instructions.

Stay away until it's safe

A tsunami is a series of waves, and the first wave is often not the biggest. Danger can last for hours. Don't go to the shore to watch. Return only when officials say it's safe.

Prepare now if you live or visit the coast

  • Find out if your home, work, or school is in a tsunami hazard zone.
  • Learn the evacuation routes and practice walking them.
  • Keep a go bag ready. See our emergency go bag checklist.

After a tsunami, see returning home safely and flood insurance basics.

Frequently asked questions

What are the natural warning signs of a tsunami?

Strong or long earthquake shaking near the coast, the ocean suddenly pulling back, or a loud roar from the ocean. Move to high ground immediately.

How long does tsunami danger last?

Hours. A tsunami is a series of waves, and the first wave is often not the largest. Wait for officials to say it's safe.

What's the difference between a tsunami warning and advisory?

A warning means a dangerous tsunami is possible or expected, so move to high ground. An advisory means strong currents and waves, so stay out of the water.
