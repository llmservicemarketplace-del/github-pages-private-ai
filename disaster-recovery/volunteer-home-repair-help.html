Free Volunteer Help With Cleanup and Home Repairs

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

After many disasters, volunteer and faith-based groups provide free help with cleanup, tarping, tree removal, mold prevention, and rebuilding. Help is limited and usually goes to people who can't do the work or pay for it, like older adults, people with disabilities, and uninsured homeowners.

Cleanup help: start here

  • Crisis Cleanup: after many disasters, a free hotline is set up that connects survivors with volunteer cleanup groups. Find the current hotline at crisiscleanup.org.
  • 211: call or text to ask what cleanup help is available. See how 211 works.
  • Local churches and community groups often organize volunteer teams.

Groups that often help

Type of helpExamples of groups
Muck-outs, debris, tarps, treesSamaritan's Purse, Team Rubicon, Southern Baptist Disaster Relief, local church teams
Rebuilding homesSBP, Habitat for Humanity, Mennonite Disaster Service, local long-term recovery groups
Repairs for older adults or people with disabilitiesLocal Area Agency on Aging, home repair ministries, Rebuilding Together affiliates

Which groups respond depends on the disaster and the area.

What to have ready

  • Your address, phone number, and a description of the work needed
  • Whether you own or rent (renters should ask the landlord first)
  • Your FEMA registration number and insurance status, if you have them
  • Photos of the damage. See how to document damage.

Stay safe from fake "volunteers"

Real volunteer groups don't ask for upfront payment or your bank information. Ask which organization they're with and check it. See disaster scams.

For larger needs, ask about long-term recovery groups and home repair grants for seniors.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get free help cleaning up after a storm?

Look up the current Crisis Cleanup hotline for your disaster at crisiscleanup.org, call 211, or ask local churches and community groups.

Do volunteer groups charge for cleanup?

Real volunteer groups don't charge or ask for upfront payment. Be cautious of anyone who does.

Can renters get volunteer help?

Often yes, especially for personal belongings, but repairs to the building usually need the landlord's permission.
