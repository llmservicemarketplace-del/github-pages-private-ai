FEMA Rental Assistance After a Disaster: Who Qualifies and How to Apply

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal, medical, or professional advice. Rules can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

FEMA Rental Assistance provides direct financial support to families whose homes are damaged and uninhabitable following a federally declared disaster. Available to both renters and homeowners, this grant money helps you pay for an apartment, house, or hotel while repairs are underway. You can submit your application online or over the phone as soon as your county is approved for Individual Assistance.

What Is FEMA Rental Assistance?

Rental Assistance is a direct grant offered through FEMA’s Individuals and Households Program (IHP) under Housing Assistance. Unlike government loans, grant funds for temporary rent do not need to be repaid as long as you spend the money on legitimate temporary housing costs.

This grant helps bridge the gap when a storm, wildfire, or flood forces you out of your regular living quarters. For a full walkthrough of initial registrations, consult our FEMA application guide.

Who Qualifies for Rental Help?

To receive rental support, you must satisfy basic federal eligibility rules:

  • Declared county: Your home must be located in a county approved for FEMA Individual Assistance.
  • Uninhabitable home: An inspection or official review must show that your home is unsafe, unlivable, or inaccessible because of the storm.
  • Primary residence: The property must be where you live for the majority of the year (vacation cabins or secondary properties do not qualify).
  • Both homeowners and renters: Renters qualify if their apartment or leased house was damaged or their landlord terminated occupancy to make repairs. Homeowners qualify if structural damage makes sleeping there unsafe.
  • Insurance rules: You must file an insurance claim first. By federal law, FEMA cannot duplicate benefits provided by private homeowners, renters, or flood insurance.

How to Apply for Rental Assistance

You can begin your application immediately after the disaster declaration is announced. Use any of these official contact channels:

  1. Online: Visit DisasterAssistance.gov and click "Apply Online."
  2. Mobile app: Download the official FEMA App onto your smartphone.
  3. Toll-free telephone: Call 1-800-621-3362. Help is available in multiple languages.

Have your Social Security Number, your pre-disaster address, current contact phone number, a list of damages, and your banking information ready to set up direct deposit.

How to Keep Receipts and Request Continued Assistance

Initial rental assistance covers a set period (often two months at first). If your primary home is still not fixed when this initial grant runs out, you can request Continued Temporary Housing Assistance:

  • Keep every receipt: Keep detailed records, written leases, and rent receipts proving that every dollar was spent on housing or utility bills.
  • Submit a renewal form: Complete the Application for Continued Temporary Housing Assistance form mailed or messaged to your account.
  • Demonstrate ongoing progress: Provide evidence of repair delays, contractor estimates, or lease agreements showing your housing need continues.

If you are exploring long-term alternatives or relocation programs, review our resources on replacement housing options.

What to Do If Your Application Is Denied (Appeals)

Many applicants receive an initial letter stating they are "ineligible." This often means a document was missing, such as proof of occupancy, an insurance denial letter, or a lease agreement. You have legal appeal rights:

  1. Read the reason code: Review your letter carefully to identify the specific document or clarification requested.
  2. Note the deadline: You must postmark or upload your written appeal letter within 60 days of the date printed on your decision letter.
  3. Include your identifiers: Write your full legal name, the disaster number, and your nine-digit FEMA application number on every single page you submit.
  4. Add missing paperwork: Attach supporting utility bills, contractor estimates, or signed lease agreements.

Frequently asked questions

Can renters apply for FEMA Rental Assistance?

Yes. FEMA Rental Assistance is available to both displaced homeowners and displaced renters whose primary residence became unlivable or unreachable due to a declared disaster.

Can I receive FEMA rental money if insurance pays for my temporary housing?

By law, FEMA cannot duplicate benefits. If your insurance policy provides Additional Living Expenses that cover your temporary rent, FEMA cannot pay for those same costs.

How long do I have to appeal a FEMA decision letter?

You must postmark or submit your written appeal letter within 60 days of the date printed on your FEMA eligibility or determination letter.

What can I spend FEMA Rental Assistance funds on?

Rental assistance funds are strictly intended to pay for temporary housing, including monthly rent, security deposits, or essential utility hookups like electricity and water.
