Home Inventory Checklist for Insurance

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

After a fire, flood, or storm, insurance companies usually ask you to list everything you lost, with prices and ages. Trying to remember it all while you're in shock is very hard. A home inventory made ahead of time can speed up your claim and help you get paid for what you actually owned.

The fastest way: video

  1. Walk through each room slowly with your phone recording.
  2. Open closets, drawers, and cabinets.
  3. Say what things are out loud as you go: brand, about when you bought it, and what it cost.
  4. Get close-ups of expensive items and their brand names.
  5. Don't forget the garage, attic, basement, shed, and yard.

It takes about an hour for most homes. A video is far better than nothing.

What to record for valuable items

  • Description, brand, and model
  • Serial numbers (photograph the label)
  • When and where you bought it, and the price
  • Receipts, appraisals, or manuals if you have them

Pay extra attention to electronics, appliances, jewelry, tools, furniture, collectibles, and clothing. Clothing adds up fast and is often underestimated.

Store it safely

  • Save copies somewhere other than your home: a cloud account, email to yourself, or a trusted relative.
  • Keep your insurance policy and agent's contact information with it.
  • Update it once a year and after big purchases.

Check your coverage while you're at it

  • Is your personal property covered at replacement cost or actual cash value? Actual cash value pays less for older items.
  • Are there limits on jewelry, firearms, collectibles, or electronics? You may need extra coverage.
  • Do you have flood insurance? Homeowners and renters policies usually don't cover floods. See flood insurance basics.
  • Renters: your landlord's insurance doesn't cover your belongings. See renters' rights after a disaster.

If disaster already hit

Search your phone photos, social media, email receipts, and online order histories to rebuild your list. Photograph all damaged items before throwing them away. See how to document damage.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a home inventory quickly?

Walk through each room recording video on your phone, opening closets and drawers and describing items out loud.

Where should I keep my home inventory?

Somewhere outside your home, like a cloud account, an email to yourself, or with a trusted relative.

What if I didn't make an inventory before the disaster?

Rebuild it from phone photos, social media, email receipts, and online order histories, and photograph damaged items before discarding them.
