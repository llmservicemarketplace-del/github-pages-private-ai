Pets in Disasters: Plan Ahead So You Don't Leave Them Behind

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

If it isn't safe for you to stay, it isn't safe for your pets. Many people put themselves at risk by refusing to evacuate without their animals, so the best plan is to know ahead of time where you and your pets can go.

Pack a pet go kit

  • Food and water for several days, plus bowls
  • Medicines and copies of vaccination and medical records
  • Leash, harness, and a sturdy carrier or crate labeled with your name and phone number
  • Litter box and litter, or waste bags
  • A recent photo of you with your pet, to prove ownership if you get separated
  • Comfort items like a blanket or toy

Add your pet's kit to your own emergency go bag.

Microchip and ID

Make sure your pet wears a collar with an ID tag and is microchipped. Register the microchip and keep your contact information up to date. Add an out-of-town contact in case your phone is down.

Where to go with pets

  • Plan ahead: make a list of pet-friendly hotels, friends, or family outside your area.
  • Shelters: many general shelters accept only service animals, but more communities now open pet-friendly shelters or co-located animal shelters. Call your local emergency management office or 211 to ask.
  • Boarding: ask veterinarians and boarding kennels outside the danger area about emergency boarding.

Leave early. Waiting for a mandatory order can mean you're told to leave pets behind.

If you can't take your pet

This should be the last resort. Never tie up or cage a pet outside. Leave them indoors in a safe room with plenty of water and food, and put a sign on the door telling rescuers what animals are inside and how to reach you.

After the disaster

  • Keep pets leashed or crated. Familiar landmarks may be gone and pets can get lost or hurt.
  • Watch for broken glass, downed power lines, and contaminated floodwater.
  • If your pet is lost, contact local animal shelters and animal control right away, and post on local lost-pet groups with a photo.

Frequently asked questions

Can I bring my pet to an emergency shelter?

Many general shelters accept only service animals, but many communities now open pet-friendly shelters. Call local emergency management or 211 before you leave.

What should I pack for my pet in an evacuation?

Food, water, medicine, records, a leash, a labeled carrier, litter or waste bags, and a recent photo of you with your pet.

What if I can't take my pet with me?

It should be a last resort. Never leave a pet tied or caged outside. Leave them indoors with food and water and post a sign for rescuers.
