Storm Surge: The Deadliest Part of a Hurricane

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Storm surge is the abnormal rise of seawater pushed onto land by a hurricane's winds. It can flood homes with several feet of fast-moving water in minutes and has historically caused many of the deaths in U.S. hurricanes. You can't ride it out, and you can't outrun it once it arrives.

Why storm surge is so dangerous

  • Water weighs about 1,700 pounds per cubic yard. Moving surge can knock down buildings and sweep away cars and people.
  • It can reach many miles inland along bays, rivers, and low-lying areas.
  • It often arrives before the strongest winds, cutting off escape routes.
  • The height of the surge depends on the storm's size, speed, angle, and the shape of the coastline, not just its category. See hurricane categories explained.

Storm surge watch vs. warning

  • Storm surge watch: life-threatening flooding from rising water is possible in the area, usually within 48 hours.
  • Storm surge warning: life-threatening flooding is expected, usually within 36 hours. Leave if officials tell you to.

The National Hurricane Center also publishes maps showing how high water could get above ground in your area.

Run from water, hide from wind

If you live in a storm surge evacuation zone, leave when told. You don't need to go far, just out of the surge zone, to a friend's house, a hotel, or a shelter on higher ground. Don't shelter in an attic during a surge; you could be trapped. See find your evacuation zone and find a shelter.

After the surge

  • Don't return until officials say it's safe. Roads and bridges may be washed out.
  • Stay out of floodwater. It can be contaminated or electrically charged.
  • Photograph the water line and all damage before cleanup. See returning home after a hurricane.

Homeowners insurance usually doesn't cover storm surge flooding; flood insurance does. See flood insurance basics.

Frequently asked questions

What is storm surge?

Storm surge is the abnormal rise of seawater pushed onto land by a hurricane's winds. It can flood coastal and inland areas with several feet of water.

Does homeowners insurance cover storm surge?

Usually not. Storm surge is flooding, which is covered by flood insurance, not a standard homeowners policy.

Should I evacuate for storm surge?

Yes, if you live in an evacuation zone and officials tell you to leave. Storm surge is often the deadliest part of a hurricane.
