Temporary Repairs Your Insurance Pays For After a Storm

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal, medical, or professional advice. Rules can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

After a storm damages your home, your homeowners insurance policy generally requires you to make temporary repairs to stop further destruction from rain, wind, or wildlife. Insurance policies typically pay for these emergency measures under your reasonable repairs coverage. Knowing what qualifies, taking photos first, and saving every receipt will help ensure your insurer reimburses you without delay.

Your Duty to Protect the Property

Almost every standard homeowners insurance contract includes a clause called the "duty to mitigate damages." This means you must take practical, safe steps to protect your house from getting worse after an initial incident.

For example, if high winds rip shingles off your roof, you cannot simply let rainwater pour through the ceiling for days while waiting for an adjuster. If you fail to cover the hole and rainwater destroys your hardwood floors or creates mold, the insurance company may refuse to pay for that extra secondary damage. Before you touch anything, make sure you take time to document damage thoroughly with photos and video.

Common Temporary Repairs That Are Covered

Insurers expect you to carry out basic, reasonable emergency measures. Covered temporary repairs usually include:

  • Roof tarping: Buying plastic tarps, furring strips, and nails, or hiring a service to cover holes and missing shingles.
  • Boarding broken windows: Placing exterior plywood over shattered glass to keep out wind-driven rain, looters, and stray animals.
  • Covering damaged doors: Securing broken exterior doors or installing temporary locks so the home can be secured.
  • Water extraction: Renting commercial fans, dehumidifiers, or wet vacuums to pull pooled water out of carpeting before mold starts growing. When dealing with standing water, follow proper steps for flood cleanup to stay safe from contaminants.
  • Shutting off damaged utilities: Turning off the main water valve to prevent interior flooding from broken pipes.

Temporary vs. Permanent Repairs: What Not to Do Yet

Understanding the difference between an emergency patch and a permanent fix is critical for your claim payout:

Important Warning: Do not begin permanent reconstruction until your insurance adjuster has had a fair chance to inspect the damage.

If you replace an entire ceiling or install a brand-new roof before the insurance company's representative inspects the damage, the company cannot confirm what caused the initial failure. They may deny or reduce your claim. Keep your work strictly temporary: only do what is necessary to seal the house from the outside elements.

Step-by-Step: How to Get Reimbursed

Follow these steps to make sure your emergency out-of-pocket costs are reimbursed by your insurer:

  1. Take photos before touching anything: Take wide and close-up photos of broken windows, fallen branches, and roof punctures before you put up tarps or boards.
  2. Keep all receipts: Save every single paper receipt from the hardware store for tarps, screws, plywood, sandbags, and plastic sheeting.
  3. Save equipment rental invoices: If you rent blowers, shop vacuums, or pumps, keep the contracts and payment receipts.
  4. Log contractor labor: If an emergency mitigation crew installs the tarp, verify they are licensed and ask for an itemized invoice. Review guidance on how to hire a contractor after a storm to avoid unlicensed storm chasers.
  5. Submit receipts promptly: Send clear copies of all emergency receipts to your claim adjuster with your policy and claim numbers clearly written at the top.

What If Insurance Does Not Cover the Damage?

If your policy carries a high storm deductible or your carrier denies part of your loss, you may qualify for federal emergency grants if your county receives a major disaster declaration. You can check our FEMA application guide to see what Individual Assistance can cover.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to wait for the adjuster before making temporary repairs?

No. You should make reasonable emergency repairs immediately to prevent more damage, such as tarping a roof or boarding broken windows. Take photos of the damage first and save all receipts.

Can I begin permanent rebuilding before the insurance company approves it?

No. Do not begin permanent repairs until the adjuster has inspected the damage or your insurer has had a fair chance to. Ask for the inspection date in writing.

What happens if I fail to protect my property from further rain or weather?

Most insurance policies contain a duty to mitigate damages clause. If you leave a roof open and rain causes mold or rot days later, the insurer may deny coverage for that secondary damage.

Does insurance pay for the tarps and plywood I buy at the hardware store?

Usually. Most homeowners policies cover reasonable out-of-pocket costs for emergency supplies used to secure the structure. Keep every itemized store receipt.
