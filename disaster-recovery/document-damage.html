How to Document Property Damage After a Disaster

Following hurricanes, tornadoes, hail, or severe flooding, thorough property damage documentation is vital to communicating your losses clearly to insurance adjusters and disaster aid organizations. Before discarding damaged items or initiating major repairs, take time to record comprehensive, time-stamped visual and written evidence.

1. Wide-Angle Context Shots vs. Close-Up Detail Photos

  • Wide-Angle Establishing Shots: Stand back in every room and at each exterior corner to photograph the entire space, including ceilings, walls, and flooring, establishing context.
  • Close-Up Detailed Evidence: Move in within 12 to 24 inches to photograph specific damage: water staining lines on baseboards, hail impact marks on metal roof vents, or cracked siding panels.
  • Include a Reference Scale: Place a common object like a ruler, coin, or tape measure next to cracks, hail dents, or flood lines to document scale objectively.
  • Capture Serial Numbers and Manufacturer Labels: Photograph manufacturer nameplates on major appliances, water heaters, HVAC compressors, electronics, and power tools showing brand, model, and serial numbers.

2. Exterior Inspection Checklist

  • Roofing & Shingles: Document torn, lifted, creased, or missing shingles from the ground. Note damage to ridge vents, chimney flashing, and soffits.
  • Gutters & Downspouts: Check for dented, detached, or debris-clogged gutters.
  • Siding, Brickwork, & Stucco: Photograph holes, impact punctures from flying debris, cracks, or wind peeling.
  • Windows, Screens, & Doors: Look for cracked glass, dented frames, torn insect screens, and warped door jambs.
  • Outbuildings & Exterior Equipment: Photograph detached sheds, perimeter fences, outdoor HVAC condenser units, and swimming pool pumps.

3. Interior Room-by-Room Inspection

  • Ceilings & Attics: Document water stains, sagging drywall, damp insulation, or daylight showing through roof decking.
  • Flooring: Measure water lines on baseboards and photograph warped hardwood, buckling laminate, or saturated carpeting.
  • Cabinetry & Built-Ins: Check inside vanity and kitchen cabinets for swelling particleboard or early mold growth.
  • Continuous Video Walkthrough: Record a steady video walkthrough of the entire property while speaking aloud to note the date, room location, and specific damage.

4. Compiling the Damaged Belongings Inventory

Recommended Personal Property Schedule Columns

  • Item Description (Brand, Make, Model, Dimensions)
  • Room / Location where the item was kept
  • Approximate Age / Date of Purchase
  • Original Purchase Price (supported by receipts, bank records, or online order histories where available)
  • Estimated Replacement Cost in current retail prices
  • Photo Reference linking to your image archive

Do not discard high-value damaged items (such as electronics or major appliances) until your insurance adjuster has inspected them or given written permission.

5. Tracking Mitigation Receipts & Contractor Estimates

Emergency mitigation expenses may be reimbursable depending on the policy, cause of loss, coverage, limits, documentation, and circumstances. Keep all receipts and records:

  • Retain physical and digital receipts for tarps, plywood, screws, fans, dehumidifiers, and cleaning supplies.
  • Keep hotel invoices, dining receipts, and fuel purchases if your home is rendered uninhabitable, as these may apply to Additional Living Expense (ALE) provisions.
  • Maintain a communication log noting dates, names, and summaries of every phone call, text, or email with adjusters, contractors, and inspectors.
