Coping After a Disaster

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Losing your home, belongings, or sense of safety is hard, and recovery can take months. Feeling overwhelmed, anxious, angry, or numb after a disaster is common. These feelings usually ease over time, and free support is available any time you need it.

Common reactions

  • Trouble sleeping or bad dreams
  • Feeling anxious, irritable, sad, or numb
  • Trouble concentrating or making decisions
  • Headaches, stomach problems, or feeling tired all the time
  • Worry when it rains or storms again
  • Using more alcohol, tobacco, or other substances

Free support, 24/7

The Disaster Distress Helpline offers free, confidential crisis counseling to anyone affected by a disaster. Call or text 1-800-985-5990, any time, in English, Spanish, and other languages.

If you or someone you know is thinking about suicide or is in crisis, call or text 988. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

Things that help

  • Take care of the basics: eat, drink water, and rest when you can.
  • Stay connected. Talk with people you trust. Faith communities, neighbors, and support groups can help.
  • Limit disaster news and social media, especially before bed.
  • Break big tasks into small steps. Recovery paperwork is easier one piece at a time. Our FEMA guide and replacing lost documents guide can help.
  • Get back to routines like meals, bedtime, and work when you can.
  • Be patient with yourself. Recovery isn't a straight line.

Helping children

Kids may become clingy, act younger than their age, have trouble sleeping, or act out. Keep routines as normal as possible, answer their questions honestly in simple words, limit their exposure to news coverage, and let them know they're safe and it's okay to feel upset. Ask their school about counseling if problems continue.

When to get more help

Reach out to a doctor or counselor if strong feelings last more than a few weeks, get in the way of daily life, or get worse. Many communities offer free crisis counseling after a declared disaster. Call 211 to find local services, or ask NFCcop for help with recovery paperwork.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal to feel anxious after a disaster?

Yes. Anxiety, trouble sleeping, sadness, and irritability are common after a disaster and usually ease with time and support.

Is there a free hotline for disaster stress?

Yes. The Disaster Distress Helpline offers free, confidential support 24/7. Call or text 1-800-985-5990. For a crisis or suicidal thoughts, call or text 988.

How can I help my child after a disaster?

Keep routines, answer questions simply and honestly, limit news exposure, and reassure them they're safe. Ask about school counseling if problems continue.

Need help with your claim or paperwork?

NFCcop offers free guidance to storm survivors. Ask for free help or browse all disaster recovery resources.
