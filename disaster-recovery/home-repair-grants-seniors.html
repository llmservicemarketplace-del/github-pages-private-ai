Home Repair Loans and Grants for Low-Income and Older Homeowners

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

If your home needs repairs you can't afford, especially repairs that affect health and safety, there are programs that may help. One of the best known is the U.S. Department of Agriculture's Section 504 Home Repair program for rural homeowners.

USDA Section 504 Home Repair loans and grants

  • Loans up to $40,000 at a 1% fixed interest rate, repaid over up to 20 years, to repair, improve, or modernize a home or remove health and safety hazards.
  • Grants up to $10,000 (a lifetime limit) for homeowners 62 and older who can't repay a loan, to remove health and safety hazards.
  • Loans and grants can be combined for up to $50,000 in help.
  • Grants may have to be repaid if the home is sold within 3 years.

Figures are from USDA Rural Development; confirm current amounts on the USDA repair loans and grants page.

Who qualifies

  • You own and live in the home.
  • The home is in an eligible rural area. USDA has a map tool to check your address.
  • Your household income is below the "very low" limit for your county.
  • For a loan, you can't get affordable credit elsewhere. For a grant, you're 62 or older.

How to apply

  1. Check your address and income on USDA's eligibility tools.
  2. Contact your local USDA Rural Development office. Applications are accepted year-round, though funding is limited.
  3. Gather proof of income, ID, proof of ownership, and repair estimates if you have them.

Use licensed, insured contractors for the work. See how to hire a contractor.

Other repair help to check

  • Free weatherization for insulation, air sealing, and heating repairs. See free home weatherization.
  • LIHEAP, which in many states can repair or replace unsafe heating systems. See help paying heating bills.
  • After a declared disaster: FEMA assistance and SBA disaster loans.
  • Local programs: many cities, counties, and charities run home repair programs for seniors and people with disabilities. Call 211.

Be wary of anyone who says you're "pre-approved" for a home repair grant and asks for a fee.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a grant for seniors to repair their homes?

USDA's Section 504 program offers grants up to $10,000 for very low-income homeowners 62 and older in eligible rural areas to remove health and safety hazards.

What is the USDA home repair loan interest rate?

USDA Section 504 repair loans have a 1% fixed rate, up to $40,000, repaid over up to 20 years. Confirm current terms with USDA Rural Development.

Do I have to live in a rural area for USDA repair help?

Yes. The home must be in an eligible rural area. USDA has a map tool to check your address.
