Disaster Food Assistance (D-SNAP) and Replacing Lost SNAP Food

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

After a major disaster, two kinds of food help may be available. Disaster SNAP (D-SNAP) can give short-term food benefits to people who don't normally get SNAP. If you already get SNAP and lost food in a disaster or a power outage, you may be able to get replacement benefits.

Disaster SNAP (D-SNAP)

D-SNAP opens only after a federal major disaster declaration that includes Individual Assistance, and only when your state asks for it and the U.S. Department of Agriculture approves it.

  • Short application window. D-SNAP sign-ups usually run for only about a week in the affected counties. Watch your state's announcements closely.
  • Who may qualify: people who lived or worked in the disaster area and had disaster costs, like home repairs, temporary housing, evacuation costs, or lost income, under special disaster income limits.
  • How benefits arrive: on an EBT card, usually within a few days of approval.

If you already get SNAP

  • Replacement benefits: If you lost food you bought with SNAP because of a disaster, a house fire, or a power outage, report it to your state SNAP office within 10 days. Your state may replace the value of the lost food, up to one month's benefit.
  • Disaster supplements: When D-SNAP opens, current SNAP households in the area may also get extra benefits.

For food that spoiled in an outage, follow food safety after a power outage and photograph what you throw away.

How to find out if D-SNAP is open

  1. Check your state SNAP agency's website and local news.
  2. Call 211.
  3. Check DisasterAssistance.gov to see if your county has a declaration.

What to bring to apply

  • Photo ID, if you have one
  • Proof of address, if you have it
  • Information about income and money on hand
  • Information about disaster costs, like repair bills or hotel receipts

Lost documents? See how to replace lost documents. Many states will help you apply without full paperwork.

Other help to apply for at the same time

  • FEMA Individual Assistance
  • Disaster Unemployment Assistance
  • Local food banks and pantries. Call 211 to find them.

Frequently asked questions

Who can get Disaster SNAP?

People who lived or worked in an approved disaster area and had disaster-related costs or lost income, and who meet special disaster income limits. It opens only when USDA approves it for your state.

Can I get my SNAP food replaced after a power outage?

Often yes. Report the loss to your state SNAP office within 10 days. The state may replace the value of the lost food, up to one month's benefit.

How long is the D-SNAP application window?

Usually short, often about a week. Watch your state's announcements and apply as soon as it opens.
