How to Stay Warm When the Power Goes Out in Winter

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

A winter outage can turn a house cold in hours. The goal is to keep people warm without creating a fire or carbon monoxide danger. Most cold-weather deaths at home during outages come from hypothermia or from unsafe heating, so both matter.

Warm one room, not the whole house

  • Pick one interior room with few windows and gather everyone there.
  • Close doors to the rest of the house. Put towels or blankets along the bottom of doors.
  • Cover windows with blankets, quilts, or heavy curtains at night. Open south-facing curtains in daytime sunlight.
  • Use a small tent or blanket fort indoors for children; body heat warms a small space quickly.

Keep your body warm

  • Wear loose layers, a hat, gloves, and warm socks, even indoors and in bed.
  • Eat regular meals and drink warm, non-alcoholic fluids. Your body makes heat from food.
  • Stay dry. Change out of damp clothes right away.
  • Know the warning signs of hypothermia and frostbite, especially for babies and older adults.

Never heat with these indoors

These cause carbon monoxide poisoning, an invisible gas that can kill quickly:

  • Gas ovens or stovetops used for heat
  • Charcoal or gas grills, camp stoves, and propane heaters not rated for indoor use
  • Generators, even in a garage with the door open. See generator safety.

Use battery-powered or battery-backup carbon monoxide alarms. If anyone gets a headache, dizziness, nausea, or confusion, get everyone into fresh air and call 911.

Safer heat sources

  • Fireplace or wood stove only if the chimney is clean and working. Keep a screen in place and never leave the fire unattended.
  • Indoor-rated fuel heaters only with ventilation and a working carbon monoxide alarm, following the maker's instructions.
  • Electric space heaters once power returns or on a properly connected generator. Read space heater safety.

Protect your home while the power is out

  • If the house is getting very cold, let faucets drip and open sink cabinets. See frozen pipes.
  • Keep the refrigerator and freezer closed. See food safety after an outage.
  • Unplug sensitive electronics to protect them from a surge when power returns.

When to leave

If you can't keep your home warm, or if anyone is elderly, very young, ill, or uses powered medical equipment, go to a warming center, shelter, or a friend's home with heat. Call 211 or your local emergency management office to find warming centers. Check on neighbors who live alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to use a gas oven for heat during a power outage?

No. Using a gas oven or stove for heat can cause carbon monoxide poisoning. Never use ovens, grills, camp stoves, or generators to heat your home.

How do I keep one room warm without power?

Gather everyone in one small interior room, close the doors, block drafts with towels, cover windows with blankets, and wear layers, hats, and gloves.

Where can I find a warming center?

Call 211 or check your county emergency management office or local news. Many communities open warming centers during long cold outages.
