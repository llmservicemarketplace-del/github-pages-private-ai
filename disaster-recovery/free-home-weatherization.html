Free Home Weatherization Help

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

The Weatherization Assistance Program is a federal program, run through state and local agencies, that makes homes more energy efficient at no cost to eligible households. It can lower heating and cooling bills for years and make a cold, drafty home safer. Renters and homeowners can both apply.

What weatherization can include

A trained energy auditor inspects your home and decides what work makes sense. Common work includes:

  • Sealing air leaks around doors, windows, and the attic
  • Adding insulation to attics, walls, and floors
  • Tuning, repairing, or sometimes replacing unsafe heating and cooling equipment
  • Health and safety checks, such as carbon monoxide testing and ventilation

Who qualifies

Income limits are set by each state. In many states, households that receive SSI or certain other benefits automatically qualify. Most programs give priority to:

  • Older adults
  • People with disabilities
  • Families with children
  • Households with high energy bills compared to income

Renters can qualify, but the landlord usually has to agree to the work.

How to apply

  1. Find your state weatherization program and local provider through the U.S. Department of Energy's how to apply page, or call 211.
  2. Have proof of income, ID, recent utility bills, and proof you own or rent the home.
  3. Expect a waiting list in many areas. Apply early, and ask whether you can be prioritized if you're older, disabled, or have young children.

Weatherization and LIHEAP often work together

Many local agencies handle both weatherization and bill-paying help. If you're struggling with a bill now, also apply for LIHEAP heating assistance.

Things you can do right now

  • Block drafts under doors with rolled towels.
  • Close curtains at night and open sunny ones by day.
  • Protect exposed pipes. See frozen pipes.
  • Use space heaters safely. See space heater safety.

If your home needs major repairs, also look at USDA home repair loans and grants.

Frequently asked questions

Is weatherization really free?

For eligible households, yes. The Weatherization Assistance Program pays for the work through state and local agencies.

Can renters get free weatherization?

Yes, renters can qualify, but the landlord usually has to agree to the work.

How do I apply for weatherization assistance?

Find your state program through the Department of Energy's how-to-apply page or call 211, then contact your local provider.
