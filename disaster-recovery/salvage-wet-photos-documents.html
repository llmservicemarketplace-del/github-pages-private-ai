How to Save Wet Photos and Important Papers After a Flood

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Family photos, letters, and important papers soaked in a flood can often be saved if you act within a couple of days. Mold can start growing within about 48 hours, so focus first on the items that matter most.

Safety first

Floodwater can contain sewage and chemicals. Wear gloves and a mask, and work in a well-ventilated area. Don't spend time on keepsakes until your home is safe. See returning home safely.

Photos

  1. Carefully separate photos that are stuck together. If they won't come apart easily, leave them and freeze the stack (see below).
  2. Gently rinse off dirt with clean, cool water. Don't rub the image surface.
  3. Lay photos face up on clean paper towels or a screen, or hang them by a corner with clothespins.
  4. Let them air dry. Don't use a hair dryer, heater, or direct sun.
  5. Scan or photograph them once they're dry to make digital copies.

Papers and books

  • Rinse muddy pages gently with clean water if needed.
  • Spread out papers to air dry with fans moving air in the room, but not blowing directly on them.
  • For wet books, stand them on end with pages fanned open, and put paper towels between pages, changing them often.
  • Keep a dehumidifier running if you can.

Can't dry it right away? Freeze it

If you can't start drying within about 48 hours, put wet photos and papers in plastic zip-top bags, separated with wax paper if possible, and freeze them. Freezing stops mold and gives you time to thaw and dry items later, a few at a time.

When to call a professional

For irreplaceable items like old photos, family Bibles, or historic documents, a professional conservator may be able to save what you can't. Ask your local library, museum, or historical society for a referral.

Replacing official documents

Birth certificates, Social Security cards, IDs, titles, and insurance papers can usually be replaced. See how to replace lost documents and getting free copies of tax returns.

Frequently asked questions

Can water-damaged photos be saved?

Often, yes. Gently rinse them in clean water and air dry them face up. Act within about 48 hours, before mold starts.

Should I freeze wet documents?

If you can't dry them within about 48 hours, freezing them in plastic bags stops mold and buys you time.

Can I use a hair dryer on wet photos?

No. Heat can damage photos. Let them air dry face up, away from direct sun.
