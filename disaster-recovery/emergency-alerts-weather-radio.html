How to Get Emergency Alerts You Won't Miss

Last reviewed November 9, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Warnings save lives only if they reach you, including at 3 a.m. with your phone on silent. The best setup uses at least two different ways to get alerts, so if one fails (dead phone, cell tower down, power out), another still works.

1. Turn on phone emergency alerts

Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA) are free text-like messages sent to phones in the danger area for tornado warnings, flash flood warnings, evacuation orders, and AMBER alerts. You don't need to sign up or have an app.

  • iPhone: Settings → Notifications → scroll to the bottom → turn on Emergency Alerts and Public Safety Alerts. Turning on "Always Deliver" lets alerts sound even when your phone is on silent or Focus mode.
  • Android: Settings → Safety & emergency (or Notifications) → Wireless emergency alerts → turn on Extreme and Severe threats.

2. Get a NOAA Weather Radio

NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts National Weather Service warnings 24 hours a day on seven frequencies between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz. A weather radio with an alarm will wake you up for a warning even when the power and cell service are out.

  • Buy one with SAME technology. You program your county's code so it only sounds for warnings near you.
  • Look for battery backup, and a hand-crank or solar option for long outages.
  • Find your county code and local transmitter on the NOAA Weather Radio site.
  • Test it monthly and replace the batteries twice a year.

3. Sign up for local alerts

Most counties have their own alert system (often called Alert + county name) that sends evacuation orders, boil water notices, and shelter openings by text, call, or email. Search your county emergency management website, or call 211.

Know what the alerts mean

  • Watch: conditions are right for the hazard. Get ready.
  • Warning: the hazard is happening or about to. Act now.

See tornado safety, flash flood safety, and storm surge warnings.

Don't rely on sirens

Outdoor sirens are meant for people outside. You may not hear them indoors, during a storm, or while asleep.

If cell service fails, see staying in touch when cell service is down.

Frequently asked questions

How do I turn on emergency alerts on my phone?

On iPhone go to Settings, Notifications, Emergency Alerts. On Android go to Settings, Safety and emergency, Wireless emergency alerts. Turn on extreme and severe alerts.

Do I need a weather radio if I have phone alerts?

It's a good backup. A weather radio with battery power and an alarm still works and wakes you up when the power and cell service are out.

What is a SAME weather radio?

A NOAA Weather Radio you program with your county's code so it only sounds an alarm for warnings that affect your area.
