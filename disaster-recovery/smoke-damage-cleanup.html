Smoke and Soot Damage Cleanup

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Smoke and soot from a house fire or nearby wildfire can spread through a whole home, even rooms the fire never reached. Soot is acidic and can stain and damage surfaces over time, and it can irritate your lungs. Cleaning it up the right way matters.

Before you start

  • Get the all-clear from the fire department before going in.
  • Call your insurer first. Smoke damage is usually covered under homeowners and renters policies, and they may want to send a professional cleaner. Photograph everything before you clean.
  • Wear an N95 mask, gloves, eye protection, and old clothes.
  • Keep children, pregnant people, and anyone with asthma or heart disease out until cleanup is done.

Air it out

  • Open windows and use box fans pointing out to move smoky air outside.
  • Replace your HVAC filter, and don't run the system until it's been checked. Ducts can spread soot.
  • Run a HEPA air cleaner if you have one. See wildfire smoke safety.

Cleaning surfaces

  1. Vacuum loose soot first with a HEPA vacuum, holding the nozzle just above the surface. Don't wipe first; wiping smears soot into surfaces.
  2. Use a dry chemical soot sponge on walls and ceilings, working top to bottom, before using any water.
  3. Then wash with a mild detergent and water, rinse, and dry. Do small sections at a time.
  4. Greasy soot from oil or plastic fires is harder to clean and often needs a professional.

Belongings

  • Clothing and linens: wash several times, or take to a dry cleaner who handles smoke damage.
  • Hard items: wash with detergent and water.
  • Upholstery, mattresses, and carpet often need professional cleaning or replacement.
  • Food and medicine exposed to smoke should be thrown out.
  • Electronics exposed to soot should be checked by a professional before use.

Odor

Smoke odor gets into drywall, insulation, and ducts. Scented sprays only cover it up. Professionals use methods like thermal fogging and sealing primers. Don't run ozone generators while people or pets are inside; ozone irritates the lungs.

Frequently asked questions

How do you clean soot off walls?

Vacuum loose soot with a HEPA vacuum, use a dry chemical soot sponge from top to bottom, then wash with mild detergent and water.

Does homeowners insurance cover smoke damage?

Usually, yes. Call your insurer before cleaning and photograph everything first.

Is it safe to stay in a house with smoke damage?

Heavy soot and smoke can irritate the lungs. Keep sensitive people out until the home is cleaned and aired out.
