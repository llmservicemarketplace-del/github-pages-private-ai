Find Gas After a Disaster

Following severe storms, tornadoes, and hurricanes, retail service stations may experience prolonged electrical blackouts, internet disruptions, or temporary fuel depletion. Before burning limited vehicle fuel searching for open pumps, verify current operating status and review critical safety protocols for transporting and handling motor fuels.

Public Notice: Y PrivateAI does not monitor live fuel inventories or distribute petroleum products. The steps below outline standard emergency management methods to locate open pumps safely.

Locating Open Stations After a Storm

Standard navigation apps may show normal operating hours rather than real-time power or fuel status. Consider these verification options:

  • Crowdsourced Fuel Availability Apps: During major declared disasters, crowdsourced trackers such as GasBuddy frequently activate community outage trackers where motorists flag whether a station has power, regular fuel, or diesel.
  • State 511 & Highway Traveler Portals: State Departments of Transportation often maintain 511 service maps showing open commercial rest stops along evacuation corridors.
  • Major Retail Chain Apps: Regional travel plazas and fuel chains (such as Love's, Pilot Flying J, Buc-ee's, or Wawa) often post generator-equipped station updates directly to their digital apps.
  • Check Timestamps Carefully: On crowdsourced portals, prioritize reports posted within the last 30 to 60 minutes. Stations with fuel can exhaust supplies rapidly during regional shortages.

Safe Fuel Storage and Handling

  • Use Only Certified Fuel Containers: Transport and store fuel exclusively in approved, red portable containers certified by the Department of Transportation (DOT), Underwriters Laboratories (UL), or ASTM. Never use water jugs or unapproved plastic containers.
  • Allow Expansion Room: Fill portable gas cans to no more than 95% capacity to allow for fuel expansion in warm weather.
  • Store in Well-Ventilated, Detached Areas: Keep fuel cans in a locked, well-ventilated outbuilding or shed, away from living quarters, direct sunlight, and heat sources such as water heater pilot lights.
  • Fuel Freshness: Modern ethanol-blended gasoline can degrade within 30 to 90 days. If storing fuel for emergency generators, consider adding an appropriate fuel stabilizer.

Critical Generator Carbon Monoxide (CO) Safety

LIFE SAFETY WARNING: Portable generator exhaust contains high levels of carbon monoxide (CO)—an invisible, odorless, and deadly gas. Running a generator indoors can cause death within minutes.
  • Keep at Least 20 Feet Away: Position portable generators outdoors at least 20 feet away from any door, window, or building vent.
  • Direct Exhaust Away: Always aim the muffler exhaust away from your home and neighboring structures.
  • Never Run Indoors or in Enclosed Spaces: Never operate a generator inside a home, garage, basement, crawlspace, or screened porch, even if doors and windows are open.
  • Install Working Battery-Powered CO Alarms: Ensure functional battery-operated or battery-backed carbon monoxide alarms are installed on each level of the home.
  • Allow Engine to Cool Before Refueling: Turn off the generator and let it cool down before pouring fuel to prevent accidental vapor ignition on hot engine components.
