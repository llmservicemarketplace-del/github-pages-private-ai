Building Permits After a Storm: What Needs One

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Emergency steps to protect your home, like tarping a roof or removing wet drywall, usually don't need a permit. Most permanent repairs do. Getting permits protects your safety and your insurance claim, and it helps you spot unlicensed contractors.

What usually needs a permit

Usually needs a permitUsually doesn't (check locally)
Roof replacement or major roof repairTemporary tarps and board-up
Electrical work, including rewiring after a floodRemoving wet carpet, drywall, and debris
Plumbing and water heater replacementMinor cosmetic repairs like painting
HVAC replacementCleaning and drying out
Structural repairs, additions, elevation

Rules vary by city and county. Always check with your local building department.

After a disaster, permitting often changes

  • Many communities open walk-in permit centers and speed up review.
  • Some waive or reduce permit fees for storm repairs.
  • In flood zones, the building department may need to make a substantial damage determination before major repairs.

Why permits matter

  • Safety: inspections catch dangerous electrical and structural work. See electrical safety after a flood.
  • Insurance: insurers may question unpermitted work on future claims.
  • Selling your home: unpermitted work can delay a sale.
  • Scam protection: a contractor who won't pull permits may not be licensed.

Who pulls the permit

Usually your licensed contractor pulls the permit. Ask for the permit number and check it on your local building department's website. In many places homeowners can pull permits for their own home, but they take responsibility for the work meeting code.

See how to hire a contractor after a storm and disaster scams.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit to replace my roof after a storm?

In most places, yes. A temporary tarp usually doesn't need one, but a full replacement or major repair usually does.

Do I need a permit to remove wet drywall after a flood?

Usually not. Removing damaged materials is typically allowed without a permit, but rebuilding walls, electrical, and plumbing work usually needs one.

Should my contractor pull the permit?

Usually yes. A licensed contractor should pull it and give you the permit number. Be cautious of anyone who tells you not to bother.
