Mudflow, Landslides, and Debris Flows: Safety and Insurance

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules differ by state and change over time, so confirm with the official sources below.

Heavy rain, especially on hillsides burned by wildfire, can send fast-moving rivers of mud, rock, and debris downhill with little warning. How insurance treats the damage depends on whether it's considered mudflow (which flood insurance can cover) or landslide or earth movement (usually excluded).

Insurance: mudflow vs. landslide

EventHomeowners policyFlood insurance (NFIP)
Mudflow: a river of liquid mud carried by water over normally dry landUsually excludedCan be covered as flood
Landslide, mudslide, or earth movement: ground sliding or settlingUsually excludedUsually excluded

The wording in your policy decides it. After a loss, describe what happened accurately, with photos showing water carrying the mud, and ask your insurer to explain any denial in writing. Separate earth movement coverage may be available in some areas.

Burn scars raise the risk

Burned soil can't soak up water. For several years after a wildfire, even moderate rain can trigger debris flows in and below burned areas. If you live downhill or downstream from a burn scar, talk to your insurer about flood coverage now, because NFIP policies usually have a 30-day waiting period. Ask whether any post-wildfire exception applies.

Warning signs

  • Rumbling that grows louder, or cracking trees and boulders
  • A sudden rise or drop in creek levels, or water turning muddy
  • New cracks in the ground, pavement, or foundation, or tilting trees, poles, or fences
  • Doors or windows suddenly sticking

What to do

  • Sign up for alerts and follow evacuation orders right away. See emergency alerts.
  • If you can't leave, move to an upper floor on the side away from the slope.
  • Never drive or walk through debris flows or flooded roads. See flash flood safety.
  • After it passes, stay away until officials say the area is safe; more flows can follow.

Frequently asked questions

Does flood insurance cover mudslides?

NFIP flood insurance can cover mudflow, meaning liquid mud carried by water. Landslides and other earth movement are generally excluded.

Does homeowners insurance cover landslides?

Usually not. Most homeowners policies exclude earth movement. Separate coverage may be available in some areas.

How long does debris flow risk last after a wildfire?

Risk is highest in the first few years after a fire, until vegetation grows back. Heavy rain is the main trigger.
