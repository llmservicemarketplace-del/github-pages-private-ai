Free Legal Help After a Disaster

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Disasters create legal problems fast: insurance claim disputes, FEMA denials, landlord issues, contractor fraud, and lost wills and deeds. Low-income survivors can often get free help from volunteer lawyers and legal aid offices.

Disaster Legal Services

After a federally declared disaster, FEMA can activate Disaster Legal Services, run with the American Bar Association's Young Lawyers Division. Volunteer lawyers give free legal help to low-income disaster survivors.

  • A toll-free hotline is set up for each disaster and promoted by FEMA in the affected area.
  • An intake operator takes your information and passes it to a volunteer lawyer, who usually contacts you within a day or two.
  • Hotlines are only active for specific disasters, so check the ABA Disaster Legal Services page for current numbers.

What they can help with

  • FEMA and other government assistance appeals
  • Insurance claims and denials
  • Landlord and tenant problems
  • Mortgage and foreclosure issues
  • Consumer problems, like contractor fraud
  • Replacing wills, deeds, and other legal documents

Legal aid offices

Even without an active disaster hotline, you can contact your local legal aid office. Find one through LawHelp.org or by calling 211. Legal aid is free for people who meet income limits. Our guide to legal aid and finding a lawyer explains your options.

Get your papers ready

  • Your insurance policy, including the declarations page
  • Letters from FEMA, your insurer, your landlord, or your lender
  • Photos of damage, receipts, estimates, and contracts
  • A short written timeline of what happened and when

Our damage documentation guide shows how to organize it.

Watch the deadlines

  • FEMA appeals are due within 60 days of the decision letter. See how to appeal FEMA.
  • Insurance policies and state laws set deadlines for claims and lawsuits. Don't wait to ask for help.

Be wary of anyone who charges upfront to "guarantee" a FEMA or insurance outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Is there free legal help for disaster survivors?

Yes. After many federally declared disasters, FEMA activates Disaster Legal Services, where volunteer lawyers help low-income survivors for free. Local legal aid offices also help.

How do I find the disaster legal hotline?

FEMA promotes a toll-free number for each disaster in the affected area. The American Bar Association's Disaster Legal Services page lists current hotlines.

What can disaster lawyers help with?

FEMA appeals, insurance claims, landlord and tenant problems, mortgages, contractor fraud, and replacing legal documents like wills and deeds.
