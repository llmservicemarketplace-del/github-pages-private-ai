How to Stay in Touch When Cell Service Fails

Last reviewed November 9, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

After a hurricane, tornado, or earthquake, cell networks often get overloaded or knocked out. Calls fail long before texts do. A few simple habits can help your family find each other and get help.

Text, don't call

Text messages use far less network capacity than calls and can go through when calls can't. Keep messages short: where you are, whether you're OK, and what you need. Leave voice lines open for emergencies.

Use an out-of-area contact

When local lines are jammed, it's often easier to reach someone far away. Pick one relative or friend in another state that everyone in the family checks in with. Write that number on paper in every wallet and go bag, because a dead phone means no contact list. See our family reunification plan.

Save your battery

  • Turn on low power or battery saver mode and lower screen brightness.
  • Close apps you aren't using, and turn off Bluetooth.
  • Keep charged power banks, and a car charger. Charge your car's phone only while the engine is running, and never run a car in a garage. See carbon monoxide safety.
  • If there is no signal at all, use airplane mode to stop your phone from draining its battery searching for towers.

Other ways to connect

  • Wi-Fi calling: if you can reach Wi-Fi (a shelter, library, or neighbor with a generator), turn on Wi-Fi calling to call and text without a cell signal.
  • Social media check-in: a short post that you're safe can reach many people at once.
  • Landlines: an old-style corded phone on a copper line may work when the power is out. Cordless phones need power.
  • Some newer phones can send emergency texts by satellite when there's no cell service. Learn your phone's feature before you need it.

Reaching 911

Always try calling 911 first in an emergency. In many areas you can also text 911 if you can't make a call; if text isn't supported where you are, you'll get a bounce-back message telling you to call. Charge your phone and know your location to give to dispatchers.

Stay informed without your phone

A battery or hand-crank AM/FM or NOAA Weather Radio gets official updates when phones and internet don't work. See how to get emergency alerts.

Frequently asked questions

Why do texts work when calls don't after a disaster?

Texts use much less network capacity than voice calls, so they often get through when networks are overloaded or damaged.

Can I text 911?

In many areas, yes. Calling is still best. If text-to-911 isn't available where you are, you'll get a message telling you to call instead.

How can I make my phone battery last during an outage?

Use low power mode, dim the screen, close apps, turn off Bluetooth, use airplane mode when there's no signal, and keep charged power banks.
