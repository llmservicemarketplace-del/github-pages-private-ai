How to Stop a Power or Gas Shutoff

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

If you're behind on your electric or gas bill, you have more options than you may think. Most states have rules that delay disconnection for serious illness, during extreme weather, or while you set up a payment plan. Act before the shutoff date if you can.

Call the utility first

  • Ask for a payment plan (often called a payment arrangement) for the past-due amount.
  • Ask to be put on their medical or life-support list if anyone depends on powered equipment. See medical equipment in a power outage.
  • Ask what assistance programs they partner with.

Medical certificates

Many states let a doctor or clinic certify that a shutoff would seriously harm someone's health, which delays disconnection. In Georgia, under Public Service Commission rules for electric service:

  • Notify the utility in writing that someone in the home has a serious illness the shutoff would make worse (a phone call is allowed, but written notice must follow within 10 days).
  • Within 10 days, the utility must receive a written statement from a physician, county board of health, hospital, or clinic naming the illness and its expected duration.

Rules and time limits differ by state and utility. Ask your utility and your state's utility commission.

Weather protections in Georgia

SeasonRule for electric service
Winter (Nov. 15 – Mar. 15)No disconnection when the forecast is below 32°F for 24 hours starting at 8 a.m. on the shutoff date, if you agree in writing to a payment plan
SummerNo disconnection when a National Weather Service heat advisory or warning is in effect or forecast for your county

Notice you should get (Georgia)

Written notice at least 5 days before disconnection, separate from your bill, plus a good-faith attempt to contact you at least 2 days before. If you didn't get proper notice, contact the Georgia Public Service Commission.

Money help

  • LIHEAP crisis assistance for shutoff notices. See help paying heating bills.
  • Utility hardship funds, local charities, and churches. Call 211.

If you lose power, don't use grills or generators indoors. See carbon monoxide safety and staying warm without power.

Frequently asked questions

Can a doctor's note stop a power shutoff?

In many states, a medical certificate from a doctor or clinic can delay disconnection when someone in the home has a serious illness. Rules and time limits vary by state.

Can Georgia Power shut off my power in winter?

Under Georgia PSC rules, electric service can't be disconnected from Nov. 15 to Mar. 15 when the forecast is below freezing for 24 hours, if you agree in writing to a payment plan.

How much notice do I get before a shutoff in Georgia?

At least 5 days' written notice, separate from the bill, plus a good-faith attempt to contact you at least 2 days before.
