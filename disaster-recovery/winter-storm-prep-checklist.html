Winter Storm Preparation Checklist

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Winter storms can knock out power, close roads, and freeze pipes for days. Getting ready before the first storm, while stores are stocked and the weather is calm, keeps your family safe and your home protected.

Know the alerts

  • Winter Storm Watch: a winter storm is possible. Get ready now.
  • Winter Storm Warning: a dangerous winter storm is happening or about to. Stay off the roads if you can.
  • Ice Storm Warning: significant freezing rain is expected. Expect power outages and falling limbs. See ice storm safety.

Sign up for local emergency alerts and keep a battery or hand-crank weather radio.

Home emergency kit

  • Water: at least 1 gallon per person per day for several days
  • Food that needs no cooking, and a manual can opener
  • Medications, first-aid kit, and supplies for babies, older adults, and pets
  • Flashlights, extra batteries, and charged phone power banks
  • Extra blankets, sleeping bags, hats, gloves, and warm clothes
  • Important documents in a waterproof bag

Get the house ready

  • Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and replace batteries.
  • Have the furnace, chimney, and vents checked. Read heating safety.
  • Insulate exposed pipes and learn where your main water shutoff is. See frozen pipes.
  • Clean gutters, and trim dead branches that could fall on the house or power lines.
  • If you have a generator, test it outdoors and stock fuel safely. See generator safety.

Car winter kit

  • Blankets, warm clothes, water, and snacks
  • Ice scraper, small shovel, and sand or cat litter for traction
  • Jumper cables, flashlight, first-aid kit, and phone charger
  • Keep the gas tank at least half full in winter

Read what to do if you're stranded in your car.

Insurance steps before the storm

  • Photograph or video every room and your valuables. It makes any claim easier.
  • Read your policy for freeze damage, ice dams, and tree damage, and keep your agent's phone number handy.

Plan for people who need extra help

Check on older neighbors and anyone who lives alone. If someone uses powered medical equipment, plan a backup power source or a place to go. Know where local warming centers are; call 211 to find them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a winter storm watch and warning?

A watch means a winter storm is possible, so prepare. A warning means a dangerous storm is happening or about to, so take action and avoid travel.

What should be in a winter emergency kit?

Water, food that needs no cooking, medications, flashlights, batteries, phone power banks, blankets, warm clothes, a first-aid kit, and important documents.

What should I keep in my car in winter?

Blankets, warm clothes, water, snacks, an ice scraper, a small shovel, sand or cat litter, jumper cables, a flashlight, and a phone charger. Keep the tank at least half full.
