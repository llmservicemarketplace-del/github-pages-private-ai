How to Use Sandbags to Protect Your Home From Flooding

Last reviewed November 7, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Sandbags can help redirect shallow water and mud away from doors, garages, and low spots. They don't make a home waterproof, and they won't stop deep flooding, but placed correctly they can reduce damage.

Where to get sandbags

Many cities and counties give out free sand and bags before storms. Check local news, your county website, or call 211. Bring a shovel and expect limits per household. Hardware stores also sell bags and sand.

How to fill them

  • Fill each bag about half to two-thirds full. Overfilled bags don't lay flat and leave gaps.
  • Use sand if you can. Soil works in a pinch but is heavier and leaks more.
  • Fold the open end under the bag instead of tying it, unless the instructions say otherwise.
  • Wear gloves, and work in pairs: one holds the bag, one shovels.

How to place them

  1. Clear debris from the area where bags will go.
  2. Lay a sheet of heavy plastic on the ground first, extending toward the water side, to help seal the bottom.
  3. Lay bags lengthwise and parallel to the flow of water, with the folded end facing upstream.
  4. Stack them in a brick pattern, overlapping each layer by half a bag, and press each bag down firmly.
  5. For walls more than one or two bags high, make the base wider than the top, like a pyramid.
  6. Don't stack sandbags against the walls of a home in deep water. Water pressure can damage the foundation. Use them to steer water away instead.

Other ways to reduce flooding

  • Clean gutters and storm drains near your home.
  • Move valuables, documents, and electronics to upper floors.
  • Test your sump pump. See sump pump failure.

Sandbags are not a substitute for evacuating when officials tell you to. See flash flood safety and flood insurance basics.

After the flood

Treat used sandbags as contaminated if they touched floodwater. Wear gloves and follow your local government's disposal instructions. Don't dump sand into wetlands or waterways.

Frequently asked questions

How full should a sandbag be?

About half to two-thirds full, so it lies flat and fits tightly against the others.

Do sandbags stop flooding?

They can redirect shallow water and mud, but they don't make a home waterproof and won't stop deep flooding.

Where can I get free sandbags?

Many cities and counties hand out free sand and bags before storms. Check your county website or call 211.
