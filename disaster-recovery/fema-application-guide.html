How to Apply for FEMA Disaster Assistance

Last reviewed November 5, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

FEMA Individual Assistance can help pay for a safe place to stay, basic home repairs, and other disaster costs your insurance does not cover. Applying is free. This guide walks through each step, what to have ready, and what to do if FEMA says no.

Step 1: Check that FEMA help is open for your county

FEMA Individual Assistance is only available after a major disaster declaration that includes Individual Assistance for your county. Enter your address at DisasterAssistance.gov to see whether help is open where you live. If it is not open yet, keep your photos and receipts; counties are sometimes added later.

Step 2: File with your insurance company first

FEMA cannot pay for losses your insurance already covers, but it can help with what insurance does not pay. File your insurance claim right away, then apply to FEMA without waiting for the insurance decision. When your insurer sends a settlement or denial letter, upload it to your FEMA account. Many FEMA denials happen simply because FEMA never received the insurance decision.

Step 3: Gather what you need

  • Identity: Social Security number for an adult in the household.
  • Address: the damaged home's address and a phone number where FEMA can reach you.
  • Household: names and ages of everyone living there, and the household's yearly income before the disaster.
  • Insurance: company name, policy number, and claim number.
  • Banking: routing and account number if you want direct deposit.
  • Damage: photos, video, and a list of what was damaged. Our damage documentation guide shows how to record it.

You can apply even if some documents were lost. See how to replace lost documents.

Step 4: Apply

  • Online: DisasterAssistance.gov
  • Phone app: the free FEMA App
  • By phone: 1-800-621-3362. If you use a relay service such as video relay or captioned telephone, give FEMA that number.
  • In person: a Disaster Recovery Center, when one is open in your area

Write down your FEMA registration number. Put it on every document you send.

The deadline

The deadline is usually 60 days after the disaster declaration, and FEMA sometimes extends it. Your exact deadline is shown on DisasterAssistance.gov. Apply early: inspections are scheduled in the order applications come in.

Step 5: The inspection

FEMA may call to schedule an inspection, in person or by phone or video. The inspector records damage; they do not decide your award. Answer calls from unknown numbers during this time, since FEMA often calls from numbers you will not recognize. A real FEMA inspector will already have your registration number and will never ask for money.

Step 6: Read the decision letter carefully

FEMA mails a decision letter and posts it to your online account. The letter states the reason for any denial. Common reasons are fixable: missing insurance decision, identity not verified, proof of occupancy or ownership not received, or "insufficient damage" when the inspector missed something.

How to appeal a FEMA decision

You have 60 days from the date on the decision letter to appeal.

  1. Write a short, signed letter explaining what FEMA got wrong and what you are asking for.
  2. Include your registration number, the disaster number, and your name and address on every page.
  3. Attach proof: contractor estimates, receipts, photos, your insurance letter, a utility bill or lease for occupancy, or a deed or tax bill for ownership.
  4. Upload it to your DisasterAssistance.gov account, mail or fax it as the letter instructs, or bring it to a Disaster Recovery Center.

What FEMA can help pay for

  • Housing: rent while you are displaced, or repairs that make your home safe, sanitary, and livable. FEMA repair help is meant for basic needs, not a full restoration.
  • Other needs: such as essential personal property, a damaged vehicle, disaster-related medical or funeral costs, childcare, and moving or storage.
  • Immediate needs: in some disasters, a one-time payment for water, food, and first aid.

FEMA grants do not have to be repaid. For costs above FEMA's limits, look at SBA disaster loans, which are open to homeowners and renters, not only businesses.

Watch for scams: FEMA never charges to apply or to "speed up" a payment. Report anyone who asks to ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Frequently asked questions

Does it cost money to apply for FEMA assistance?

No. Applying is free. Anyone who charges a fee to apply or promises to speed up your payment is not from FEMA.

Can I apply to FEMA if I have insurance?

Yes. File your insurance claim first, then apply to FEMA. FEMA cannot pay for what insurance covers, but it can help with uninsured losses. Send FEMA your insurance decision letter when you receive it.

How long do I have to appeal a FEMA denial?

You have 60 days from the date on your FEMA decision letter. Send a signed letter explaining why the decision is wrong, with supporting documents and your registration number on every page.

Do renters qualify for FEMA help?

Yes. Renters can be eligible for rental assistance and for help replacing essential personal property, even though they do not own the home.
