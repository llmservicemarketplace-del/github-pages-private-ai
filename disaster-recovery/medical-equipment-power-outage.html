Medical Equipment During a Power Outage

Last reviewed November 6, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

If you or someone at home depends on electricity for oxygen, breathing machines, dialysis, a power wheelchair, or refrigerated medicine, a power outage can quickly become an emergency. A written plan made before storm season gives you time to act calmly.

Make a plan with your care team

  • Ask your doctor and equipment supplier how long each device's battery lasts and what to do if power is out longer.
  • Ask about backup batteries, oxygen tanks that don't need power, or other alternatives.
  • Keep a list of your equipment, settings, medications, doses, and your providers' phone numbers. Keep a copy in your go-bag.
  • Ask your pharmacist how to store refrigerated medicines like insulin if power is lost.

Tell your utility

Many utilities have a medical or life-support program. Some states give extra protection from shutoffs when a doctor certifies that someone in the home needs powered medical equipment. These programs usually do not guarantee your power will be restored first in an outage, so you still need a backup plan. Call your utility to ask what they offer.

Backup power options

  • Extra batteries for each device, kept charged.
  • Battery power stations can run some small devices for a while. Check your device's power needs first.
  • Generators must run outdoors, far from windows and doors. Read generator safety; a generator indoors can cause deadly carbon monoxide poisoning.
  • A car charger or inverter can charge some devices in an emergency, but never run a car in a closed garage.

Know where you'll go

  • Identify a place with power you can go to: a friend or relative's home, a hotel outside the outage area, or a shelter that can support medical equipment.
  • Call 211 or your local emergency management office before storm season to ask about shelters and transportation.
  • If you're in danger or your equipment fails, call 911.

During a long outage

  • Keep refrigerated medicines cold with a cooler and ice, and follow your pharmacist's advice. See food safety after an outage for keeping a fridge cold.
  • Stay warm safely. See staying warm without power.
  • Check on neighbors who use medical devices.

Frequently asked questions

Will my utility restore power first if I use medical equipment?

Usually not guaranteed. Many utilities have medical programs and some states add shutoff protections, but you still need your own backup plan.

Can I run oxygen or a CPAP on a generator?

Some devices can run on a properly sized generator, but the generator must be outdoors and far from windows. Ask your supplier about your device's power needs.

Where can I go if my medical equipment needs power?

A friend's or relative's home with power, a hotel outside the outage, or a shelter that supports medical equipment. Call 211 or local emergency management to plan ahead.
