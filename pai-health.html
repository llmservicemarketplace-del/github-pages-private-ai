Medication context by name

See what the FDA labels say—and where they say it.

PAi Health connects the medication names and symptoms in the record to public RxNorm and FDA-label sources. It quotes the label instead of silently turning a match into a medical conclusion.

1

Name to ingredient

A logged or imported brand name, generic name, strength, form or combination product is matched through RxNorm to its active ingredients.

2

Drug-to-drug checks

Every medication pair is checked against each medicine’s FDA label for interactions, contraindications, boxed warnings, duplicate ingredients and same drug classes.

3

Possible side-effect context

Symptoms already in the person’s record are compared with adverse-reaction and warning sections. The app shows the label’s own wording and source link; a match does not prove the medicine caused the symptom.

Medication safety: PAi Health provides label-based context, not individualized prescribing advice. Before starting, stopping, combining or changing a medicine, ask a pharmacist or prescriber.

Procedure history from billing records

Build a timeline of services reported by claims and bills.

PAi Health can extract and organize procedure or service information from supported healthcare billing documents while preserving what the source actually says.

1

Recognize supported billing records

Work with CMS-1500 and UB-04 claim forms, explanations of benefits, Medicare Summary Notices, patient bills and supported FHIR financial records. For paper forms, the person confirms the detected form type.

2

Preserve the printed codes

Keep service dates, descriptions, modifiers, claim-line amounts and printed ICD-10-PCS, HCPCS Level II, CPT or revenue codes connected to the source record. Licensed CPT and revenue-code descriptions are not supplied unless the document itself includes them.

3

Keep the evidence status honest

Items remain labeled as submitted, billed, adjudicated or paid-as-reported. PAi Health does not silently turn a claim line into proof that care occurred or a reported payment into a bank transaction.

Review required: Billing layouts vary. Current extraction materials were tested with synthetic fixtures and have not been verified against every real-world form. Compare every extracted code, date, description and amount with the original document.

Questions before you start

PAi Health FAQ

Who is PAi Health for?

It is designed for adults who need to organize a chronic, complicated or long-running health history across records, medications, labs and daily readings. It can also help a caregiver organize records with the person’s permission.

Is PAi Health a medical device or diagnostic service?

No. It is an information-organization, tracking and research tool. It does not diagnose, treat, prescribe or replace a qualified healthcare professional.

Does every feature require AI?

No. The application can organize records and support tracking without AI. Optional private AI can run locally on compatible hardware; optional external AI uses the user’s own provider key and explicit consent.

Does PAi Health contain nearly 40 million medical articles?

Its literature index was derived from 39,994,988 PubMed 2026 baseline article records. The application does not bundle the full articles. It stores condensed medical concept relationships, counts, study-design signals and selected PubMed identifiers that point users to citations.

How does the medication interaction and side-effect check work?

PAi Health uses RxNorm to resolve medication names to ingredients, then checks medication pairs against public FDA-label sections from openFDA/DailyMed. It can also show when symptoms in the record are mentioned in a medication’s adverse-reaction or warning sections. It quotes and links to the label; a match is not proof of causation and is not a recommendation to change treatment.

Does a billing code prove that a procedure was performed?

No. PAi Health organizes procedures and services as they are reported in supported billing records and preserves whether an item was submitted, billed, adjudicated or paid-as-reported. A claim form is not proof that a service occurred, and an explanation of benefits is not a bank record. Confirm important details against the clinical record, provider and original billing document.

What happens after the free 30 days?

The trial lasts 30 days. Microsoft Store shows the current purchase price and licensing terms that apply after the trial.

Are all device integrations validated?

No. Support varies by format and device. Some importers and Bluetooth paths in the current software materials are marked as tested with synthetic fixtures but not yet verified against every real export or hardware device. Review imported information against its source.
