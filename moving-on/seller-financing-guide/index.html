Seller Financing and Owner Financing After Displacement

Understand seller carryback, owner financing, land contracts, balloon payments, title, existing mortgages, servicing, default terms, and fraud risks.

The short answer

Seller financing can describe very different arrangements. The seller may transfer the deed and take a mortgage, retain title under a contract for deed, or propose a transaction involving an existing mortgage. Before signing, identify who holds title, what lien secures the obligation, how payments are credited, whether a balloon is due, and what happens after default.

Terms to put in writing

Verify identity and ownership

Confirm the seller, deed, legal description, taxes, liens, association status, and authority to sell through independent records.

Identify the structure

Determine when title transfers, what document secures payment, who records it, and whether another mortgage remains.

Calculate the real payment

Review price, down payment, interest rate, amortization, balloon date, late charges, taxes, insurance, fees, and prepayment terms.

Plan servicing and records

Use clear statements, receipts, escrow terms where applicable, addresses for notices, and a process for correcting payment errors.

Review default and exit terms

Understand cure periods, acceleration, forfeiture or foreclosure language, sale or refinance rights, and dispute provisions before signing.

Seller-financing comparison

  • Independent title and lien search
  • Purchase agreement and financing documents reviewed together
  • Annual rate, amortization, payment, and balloon date
  • Taxes, insurance, repairs, assessments, and recording responsibility
  • Existing-loan and due-on-sale questions identified
  • Default, notice, cure, payoff, prepayment, and transfer provisions

Print or save this list. Add dates and source information so you can tell what was known at each decision point.

Watch for scams and unsafe shortcuts

  • A seller who will not provide proof of ownership
  • A large nonrefundable payment before title and documents are reviewed
  • A hidden balloon payment or payment schedule that does not reduce principal as expected
  • Instructions to conceal the transfer from a lender, insurer, association, or public record

Safety rule: Verify the person, organization, property, ownership, and payment instructions through an independently obtained source before sending documents or money.
