Unpermitted Repairs After a Storm: Risks for Buyers and Sellers

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or real estate advice. Rules differ by state and change over time, so confirm with the official sources below.

After big storms, contractor shortages lead many homeowners to repair without permits. That work can cause problems later when the house is sold: delayed closings, lower offers, lender or insurer questions, and disputes over what the seller knew.

For sellers

  • Disclosure: many states require sellers to disclose known material defects or answer a disclosure form honestly. Florida courts require sellers to disclose known defects that materially affect value and aren't easily seen. Georgia leans more on buyer inspection, but sellers can't hide or lie about known problems. Ask a real estate lawyer about your state.
  • Unpermitted electrical, structural, or roof work is the biggest concern for buyers and inspectors.
  • Fix it before listing: ask your building department about a retroactive or "as-built" permit. You may need to open walls for inspection.
  • Keep every contractor invoice, warranty, and photo of the work.

For buyers

  • Check permit history at the city or county building department, often online. Compare it with recent repairs and the seller's disclosures.
  • Hire a licensed home inspector, and a licensed electrician or roofer for flood- or storm-repaired homes.
  • Ask the seller for contractor names, licenses, invoices, and warranties.
  • Get homeowners insurance quotes early. Some insurers ask about roof age and recent work.
  • If problems turn up, negotiate a price reduction, repairs before closing, or an escrow holdback.

Flood-zone homes

In a flood zone, major unpermitted repairs may skip the required substantial damage review. That can cause compliance problems and affect flood insurance rates. Ask the building department whether a determination was made, and get an elevation certificate. See hazard and flood research.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to disclose unpermitted work when I sell?

In many states, you must disclose known material defects or answer your state's disclosure form honestly. Ask a real estate lawyer in your state.

How do I find out if repairs were permitted?

Search permit records at the city or county building department, often online, by address.

Can I get a permit after the work is done?

Many building departments offer retroactive or as-built permits, which may require inspections and opening walls.
