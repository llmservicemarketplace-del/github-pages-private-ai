Mortgage Forbearance: Meaning, Options, and What Happens Next

Understand mortgage forbearance, questions to ask a servicer, records to keep, post-forbearance possibilities, foreclosure risk, and relief scams.

The short answer

Mortgage forbearance generally means a servicer permits a temporary pause or reduction in required payments. It usually does not erase the amount that was not paid. The exact agreement, repayment path, interest, escrow treatment, credit reporting, and available next steps depend on the loan, investor, program, and written terms.

Before agreeing to forbearance

Verify the servicer

Use the number on a recent statement or the servicer’s independently located official website—not a number in an unsolicited message.

Describe the hardship

Explain the disaster, income interruption, displacement, repair obligations, and the time frame you can document.

Ask for written terms

Request the start and end dates, payment amount, interest and fee treatment, escrow handling, credit-reporting information, and exit options.

Calendar follow-ups

Record confirmation numbers and schedule contact well before the forbearance period ends.

Review the exit

Ask what evaluation or documents are required for repayment, deferral, modification, reinstatement, sale, or another available option.

Servicer conversation log

  • Date, time, telephone number, and representative name or ID
  • Hardship description and documents requested
  • Quoted payment, dates, fees, interest, and escrow terms
  • Confirmation or case number
  • Written agreement received and saved
  • Next deadline and next scheduled contact

Print or save this list. Add dates and source information so you can tell what was known at each decision point.

Watch for scams and unsafe shortcuts

  • Advance fees for a guaranteed modification or foreclosure stop
  • Instructions to stop communicating with the real servicer
  • Demands to send the mortgage payment to a new third party
  • Pressure to sign a deed, power of attorney, or unfamiliar financing document

Safety rule: Verify the person, organization, property, ownership, and payment instructions through an independently obtained source before sending documents or money.
