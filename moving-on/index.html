Recovery-to-relocation guide

Moving On: Disaster Recovery and Safer Relocation Resource Center

A guided disaster-recovery pathway for mortgage hardship, rebuilding decisions, safer relocation, property research, and scam prevention.

The short answer

When rebuilding is not the right choice, moving forward can require several decisions at once: protecting shelter, understanding the mortgage, preserving insurance and expense records, comparing relocation options, and screening the next property for hazards and fraud. Start with the stage that matches your immediate need and return here as the situation changes.

Choose your next step

Stabilize today

Secure safe lodging, medicine, transportation, mail, records, and reliable contact information before making long-term commitments.

Protect the mortgage and paperwork

Contact the verified mortgage servicer, ask about hardship options, and keep a dated log of notices, calls, payments, and documents.

Compare rebuilding with relocating

Put estimates, policy information, timelines, future hazard exposure, insurance availability, and family needs side by side.

Research the next home safely

Use listing sites as leads, then independently verify ownership, title, condition, hazards, insurance, financing, and payment instructions.

First-file checklist

  • Disaster declaration and assistance numbers
  • Insurance policy, claim number, adjuster contacts, and written coverage positions
  • Mortgage statements and verified servicer contact information
  • Temporary-housing receipts and a housing-search log
  • Repair, demolition, mitigation, and relocation estimates
  • Photos, inventories, notices, emails, and a dated event timeline

Print or save this list. Add dates and source information so you can tell what was known at each decision point.

Watch for scams and unsafe shortcuts

  • Anyone demanding an advance fee to “guarantee” mortgage relief
  • Copied property listings or requests to pay before viewing and verification
  • Changed wiring instructions delivered only by email or text
  • Pressure to sign over a deed or hide the transaction from a lender or insurer

Safety rule: Verify the person, organization, property, ownership, and payment instructions through an independently obtained source before sending documents or money.
