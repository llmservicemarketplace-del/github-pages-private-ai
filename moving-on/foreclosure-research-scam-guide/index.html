Time-sensitive homeowner research

Foreclosure Research and Scam-Warning Guide

Understand common foreclosure stages, find official records and help, preserve deadlines, and recognize foreclosure-rescue and deed scams.

Time-sensitive: If you received a summons, complaint, notice of default, notice of sale, eviction paper, or scheduled auction date, treat it as time-sensitive. This page is educational and cannot calculate your deadline.

The short answer

Foreclosure rules and deadlines vary by state, property, loan, and court or sale process. Do not rely on a generic internet timeline. Read every notice, verify the sender, record the stated deadline, find the official court or county source, contact the verified servicer, and seek qualified help promptly.

Build a reliable foreclosure record

Collect every notice

Keep envelopes, certified-mail information, postings, emails, account statements, court papers, and prior assistance applications.

Identify the process

Determine whether the notice references a court case, power-of-sale process, tax proceeding, association lien, sheriff sale, or another procedure.

Verify official records

Use the court clerk, county recorder, tax office, or sheriff website reached independently—not a link supplied by a stranger.

Contact verified help

Ask the servicer about available loss-mitigation review and use official counselor or legal-aid directories.

Track every deadline

Maintain a written calendar, document what was submitted, and preserve proof of delivery.

Foreclosure research file

  • Loan and servicer information
  • Complete payment and communication history
  • All notices with envelopes and dates received
  • Court, county, or sale record identifiers
  • Assistance applications and proof of submission
  • A deadline calendar and list of professional referrals contacted

Print or save this list. Add dates and source information so you can tell what was known at each decision point.

Watch for scams and unsafe shortcuts

  • A promise to stop foreclosure regardless of your facts
  • A demand for money before any mortgage-relief result
  • A deed transfer described as a temporary rescue
  • A person claiming to be the court, lender, or government while using unofficial contact details

Safety rule: Verify the person, organization, property, ownership, and payment instructions through an independently obtained source before sending documents or money.
