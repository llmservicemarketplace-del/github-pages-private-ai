Sea-Level Rise, Flood, Storm-Surge, and Erosion Research

Learn how to compare official flood maps, sea-level scenarios, storm surge, coastal change, historical events, and insurance information for a property.

The short answer

No single map answers every property-risk question. FEMA maps primarily support floodplain management and insurance administration; NOAA and USGS tools can add sea-level, high-tide, surge, shoreline, and coastal-change context. Local drainage, elevation, building condition, and insurance underwriting still require property-specific research.

Address-level research method

Confirm the exact location

Record the street address, parcel number, coordinates if available, jurisdiction, watershed, and coastal or river setting.

Open the effective flood map

Save the map panel, zone, effective date, and any letters or amendments relevant to the parcel.

Add scenario and event context

Review sea-level, storm-surge, erosion, rainfall, and historical-event sources without treating scenarios as exact predictions.

Ask local questions

Contact floodplain, planning, building, public works, emergency management, and utility offices about drainage, access, projects, and rules.

Test financial resilience

Ask insurers and qualified professionals how the exact location and structure affect coverage, deductibles, mitigation, and repair feasibility.

Map-reading record

  • Tool, agency, URL, layer, scenario, and date viewed
  • Address or parcel searched and any geocoding correction
  • Map limitations and resolution
  • Current regulation versus future scenario clearly labeled
  • Screenshots or exported reports with source information
  • Questions requiring local or professional verification

Print or save this list. Add dates and source information so you can tell what was known at each decision point.

Watch for scams and unsafe shortcuts

  • A map screenshot with no source, date, legend, or scenario
  • A claim that one layer proves a property is safe or doomed
  • Confusing storm surge, rainfall flooding, river flooding, and long-term sea-level scenarios
  • Making a purchase or insurance decision without checking the exact structure and access route

Safety rule: Verify the person, organization, property, ownership, and payment instructions through an independently obtained source before sending documents or money.
