Software that runs on your computer, not someone else's

Four Windows applications published on the Microsoft Store, in two lines. PAi products are private: they load their AI on your own computer and work with the network off. PSi products do their core work locally too, and can reach a larger model through an API when you want one.

You choose which trade you want. Neither line asks you to hand over your files as the price of using it.

PAi Legal

A private case workspace for people handling their own legal matter

Legal trouble arrives as a pile of paper: denial letters, contracts, emails, photographs, court forms. PAi Legal takes that pile and turns it into an organized case file on your own hard drive. It reads PDFs, Word files, spreadsheets, Outlook messages, and scanned paper — optical character recognition is built in, so a photographed document becomes searchable text without a subscription to anything.

One event often produces more than one legal matter. A denied insurance claim and the lawyer who mishandled the appeal are two different disputes with two different defendants. PAi Legal keeps them separated inside a single case folder so the record stays straight.

  • Document import with built-in OCR for scans and photographs
  • Separate legal matters tracked inside one case
  • Encrypted backups and an append-only audit record
  • Jurisdiction rules and deterministic filing checks
  • Optional private AI that runs on your own hardware
  • Plain-English guidance for self-represented litigants

PSi Legal

A case law library on your own machine, searchable without a connection

Commercial legal research runs on a meter. PSi Legal takes a different approach: it builds and indexes a case law corpus that lives on your computer, so searching costs nothing, works with the network unplugged, and leaves no record of what you looked up. No AI is required — the index does the work.

If you want a model to read the results for you, PSi Legal can reach one through an API. That part is optional and off unless you turn it on. It also pairs with PAi Legal, which can pull authorities from the corpus while your case files stay where they are.

PAi LFM

A local AI assistant with no account and no cloud

PAi LFM installs a language model on your own computer and gives you a chat window to it. Once the model is downloaded, it answers with the network off. Nothing you type is transmitted, logged on a server, or used to train anything, because there is no server involved.

The installer handles the parts that usually stop people: choosing a model that fits the machine, downloading it, and starting it. Free to install; activation uses a purchase code.

Libby's Mental Metals

Alloy screening that tells you what composition can and cannot determine

Enter an alloy composition and get back the properties that genuinely follow from it: density, atomic-size mismatch, valence-electron concentration, configurational entropy, elastic-modulus and conductivity envelopes, thermal expansion, and a phase-family screening hint. Useful for high-entropy alloys and other multi-component systems where hand calculation gets unwieldy.

What separates it from a calculator is what it refuses to do. Strength, hardness, toughness, creep, corrosion, and weldability depend on microstructure, process history, and test data — so instead of returning a number that looks like a measurement, the report names exactly which state information is missing. Manufacturing routes get the same treatment: melt and cast, wrought, powder-HIP, LPBF, DED, mechanical alloying, and joining are each marked available, conditional, or requires-data.

No output is certified material. Certification needs a controlled specification, a qualified process, a traceable lot, and acceptance testing, and every report states that boundary rather than blurring it.

The calculation engine runs on your machine. You can optionally connect an AI model of your choice using your own API key, which adds a reasoning layer over the results — when no key is set, the engine runs its solvers locally and the app works without it.
