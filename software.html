Three products, and the add-ons that extend them

Two complete Windows applications and a line of specialist assistants. All of them run the AI on your own hardware, with no account and nothing uploaded. Denied claim, contested matter, or a subject you need an expert answer on — the record stays yours.

PAi Claims

Property insurance claim software with a deterministic repair estimator built in.

Photographs, carrier estimates, repair costs, correspondence and payment records go into one claim file you control, and the software builds the relationships between them: facts to the documents that support them, damage to the photographs and measurements that document it, pricing to quantities and cited sources. That structure exists independently of any AI model.

The estimator derives quantities from measurements and shows the formula on every line. Materials price from your own store-price import with SKU, store and ZIP recorded. Contractor cost above materials is a median of at least three independent observations. An estimate reports as supported only when all of that is present — otherwise it lists exactly what is missing rather than producing a number anyway.

PSi Estimators is included. There is nothing extra to buy for it.

PAi Legal

A case workspace that builds a factual narrative one unit at a time.

Each fact, statement and allegation is entered as a discrete unit tied to what supports it — a document, a page, a photograph, a dated communication — and the narrative is assembled from those units, so every sentence in the finished account can be traced to the record behind it. Documents import with OCR built in, so scanned and photographed paper joins the searchable file.

The links between facts and documents form a map, weighted by how much of the record connects to each point. The least-supported part is surfaced explicitly as the first structural gap to inspect, which is generally the sentence an opponent will find first.

Each matter is stored in a location the attorney chooses — a removable drive, a volume, a designated path — so separation between matters is physical rather than a permission setting. At first run you choose whether you are practicing or handling your own matter; the self-represented mode uses plain language and explains every term as it appears.

PAi Assistants

Specialist local chatbots, each tuned for one subject — the questions it asks you, the way it reasons, and where it tells you to check with a professional.

Setup checks your computer's memory, disk space and graphics, recommends a model that suits the hardware, downloads it from its publisher, and configures it. Two dozen specialisms are available, including mechanical diagnosis, engineering calculation, metallurgy, coding, home repair, photography, and everyday writing and planning.

PSi Legal — build your own caselaw corpus

Commercial legal research runs on a meter. PSi Legal is an indexer: it builds and indexes a caselaw corpus that lives on your own machine, so searching costs nothing, works with the network unplugged, and leaves no record of what you looked up. No AI is required, because the index does the work.

For a firm, this means an in-house corpus assembled from sources you choose, on hardware you control, without a per-search charge or a log of your research sitting on a vendor's server. PAi Legal can pull authorities from that corpus while your case files stay where they are.

PAi Legal ships with a sample index of roughly five million cases. Be clear about what that is: the sample preserves only the first 600 characters of each description, so it is a finding aid rather than a research library. It will tell you a case exists and roughly what it concerns. Reading the case is a separate step. PSi Legal is how you build a corpus with the full text you need.

If you want a model to read results for you, PSi Legal can reach one through an API. That path is off until you enable it and supply your own credentials.

How the two lines differ

PAi products load an AI model onto your own computer during setup and run it there. Once installed they work with no network connection, no account, and no data leaving the machine. PAi Claims, PAi Legal and the assistants are all PAi products.

PSi add-ons do their core work locally as well. Where they differ is that they can optionally reach a hosted model through an API when a job needs one larger than a desktop can hold. That path is off until you enable it and supply your own credentials.

The products connect. PAi Claims and PAi Legal share a case, so claim evidence and its structure carry into the legal workspace when a denial turns into a dispute. PAi Legal can pull authorities from a PSi Legal corpus without your case files leaving the machine.
