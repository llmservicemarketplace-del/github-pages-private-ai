How do I get paid for damage the insurer missed?

Leer en español

It's common to find more damage after the first payment: rotted decking under a roof, wet insulation, a damaged subfloor, or items the adjuster left off the estimate. You can ask the insurer to pay for it through a supplement.

Some states set deadlines. In Florida, a supplemental claim must be reported within 18 months of the date of loss.

The short answer. Stop and document hidden damage when you find it, before it's covered up. Tell the insurer in writing right away, send photos and a line-item estimate, and ask for a re-inspection if needed. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • Whether the new damage comes from the same covered event.
  • Whether the insurer had a chance to see it before repairs covered it.
  • State and policy deadlines for supplements.

How to submit a supplement

  1. Pause the repair at that spot if you can, and photograph the damage with something for scale.
  2. Notify the insurer in writing the same day or next day, with the claim number.
  3. Send a line-item estimate for the added work, with photos.
  4. Offer a re-inspection before the area is closed up.
  5. Track it in your claim log until it's paid or answered in writing.

Common supplement items

  • Roof decking, drip edge, flashing, and code-required items. See ordinance or law coverage.
  • Drywall, insulation, and flooring found wet after removal.
  • Items left off the estimate, or wrong quantities or measurements.
  • Recoverable depreciation after repairs are done. See ACV vs. replacement cost.

What your file needs

  • Photos of the hidden damage before it's covered.
  • Line-item estimate for added work.
  • Date found and date reported.

Quick answers

Can I file for more money after my claim is paid?

Often yes, through a supplemental claim for damage from the same event that was missed or found later. Report it in writing promptly.

Is there a deadline for supplemental claims?

It depends on your state and policy. In Florida, supplemental claims must be reported within 18 months of the date of loss.
