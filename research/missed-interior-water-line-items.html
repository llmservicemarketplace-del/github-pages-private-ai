What should an interior water damage estimate include?

Water damage repairs follow a sequence: dry the structure, remove what can't be saved, rebuild, and finish. An estimate that only pays to "patch drywall" skips most of that sequence.

The short answer. Check that the estimate covers drying, removal, rebuilding and finishing for every affected room, plus moving contents and resetting fixtures. Then check whether the cause of the leak is covered at all; see sudden vs. gradual leaks.

Checklist by stage

Mitigation (drying out)

  • Water extraction, air movers and dehumidifiers, with equipment days.
  • Moisture readings and drying logs from the mitigation company.
  • Antimicrobial treatment where needed.

Removal

  • Flood cut of wet drywall, usually above the wet line. See flood cuts.
  • Wet insulation removal.
  • Baseboard, casing and trim removal.
  • Wet carpet and pad; cabinets that took water at the base.
  • Debris hauling and disposal.

Rebuild

  • New insulation, drywall hung, taped, mudded and textured to match.
  • New baseboard and trim, caulked and painted.
  • Flooring for the whole continuous area, not just the wet square feet, where it can't be matched.
  • Cabinets, countertops and plumbing reset if cabinets were removed.

Finish

  • Primer and paint for the whole wall, and often the whole room, so it matches.
  • Ceiling paint where the ceiling was stained or replaced.
  • Final cleaning.

Items people forget

  • Contents manipulation: moving furniture out and back.
  • Detach and reset: outlets, switch plates, toilets, vanities, appliances, door casings.
  • Additional living expense if the home isn't livable during repairs. See ALE.
  • Mold limits may apply separately. See mold sublimits.

Photos to take

  • The wet line on every wall, before removal.
  • Moisture meter readings with the location visible.
  • The opened wall cavity, insulation and subfloor.
  • The source of the water, before the plumber repairs it.

Quick answers

Should insurance pay to paint the whole room?

Often the whole wall or room has to be painted so the repair matches. Ask for it in writing with photos showing the repaired area won't blend.

Does insurance pay for drying equipment?

Reasonable water mitigation after a covered loss is commonly paid, and most policies require you to protect the property from further damage. Keep the drying logs and invoices.

Will insurance pay for new flooring in rooms that didn't get wet?

It depends on the policy and state matching rules. If continuous flooring can't be matched, raise matching in writing.
