How do I get the insurance adjuster to come back out?

If the adjuster missed damage, measured wrong, or inspected before hidden damage was found, you can ask for a re-inspection. Insurers often agree when you show them specifically what was missed.

The short answer. Ask in writing, list exactly what was missed with photos, and ask to have your contractor present. If the insurer refuses, ask for the refusal and its reasons in writing.

What to send

  1. A short letter or email with your claim number asking for a re-inspection.
  2. A numbered list of what was missed or measured wrong, by area.
  3. Photos with dates, and a contractor's itemized estimate if you have one.
  4. Any new damage found during repairs, reported right away as a supplement.
  5. Days and times you and your contractor are available.

Preparing for the visit

  • Have your contractor or roofer there to point out the damage and agree on measurements.
  • Walk the adjuster through your numbered list in order.
  • Take your own photos of what the adjuster photographs.
  • Write down the adjuster's name and, in Florida, license number; ask when the revised estimate will arrive.
  • Don't agree to anything on the spot that you haven't seen in writing. See meeting the adjuster.

Timing rules (Florida example)

Florida requires an insurer to conduct any physical inspection within 30 days after receiving proof-of-loss statements, and to pay or deny an initial, reopened or supplemental claim within 60 days of notice, absent factors beyond its control (Fla. Stat. 627.70131). Other states have their own timelines; see insurer deadlines by state.

If the insurer says no

  • Ask for the refusal and reasons in writing.
  • Send your contractor's estimate and photos and ask for a revised estimate without an inspection.
  • Consider appraisal if the dispute is about the amount of loss.
  • File a complaint with your state insurance department if the insurer stops responding.

Quick answers

Can I ask for a second adjuster?

You can ask for a re-inspection, and you can ask that a different adjuster or a field supervisor attend. The insurer decides who it sends.

Should my contractor be at the re-inspection?

Usually yes. A contractor can show damage and agree on measurements on the spot, which avoids back-and-forth later.

What if the damage is already repaired?

Send the photos, invoices and any removed materials you kept. Report hidden damage before covering it up whenever you can.
