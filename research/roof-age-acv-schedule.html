Why is my insurer paying only part of my roof because of its age?

Many newer policies don't pay full replacement cost for an older roof. Instead, an endorsement pays actual cash value, or a percentage based on the roof's age, for the roof covering. The rest of your home may still have replacement cost coverage.

The short answer. Look for a roof endorsement in your policy and its schedule. Then check the age the insurer used: a permit, invoice or inspection showing a newer roof can change the payment.

Common roof provisions

  • ACV roof endorsement. The roof covering is paid at actual cash value (replacement cost minus depreciation) instead of replacement cost.
  • Roof surfacing payment schedule. A table pays a set percentage of the roof's replacement cost based on its age and material, for example less each year after a certain age.
  • Separate roof or wind/hail deductible. A percentage deductible that applies only to roof or wind and hail losses.
  • Cosmetic damage exclusion. Excludes dents and marks that don't affect function. See cosmetic damage.

Check the roof age

The payment often turns on how old the insurer thinks the roof is. Gather the building permit, the roofer's invoice, a manufacturer warranty registration, or a home inspection report. If the insurer used the house's age instead of the roof's, say so in writing with your proof.

Florida's roof rules

  • An insurer may not refuse to issue or renew a homeowners policy solely because the roof is less than 15 years old (Fla. Stat. 627.7011(5)(b)).
  • For a roof at least 15 years old, the insurer must allow a roof inspection at the homeowner's expense before requiring replacement, and may not refuse to issue or renew solely because of roof age if the inspection shows at least 5 years of useful life remaining (627.7011(5)(c)).
  • If a separate roof deductible applies, the insurer may limit the roof payment to actual cash value until it receives reasonable proof that you paid the roof deductible (627.7011(3)(a)).

Other states regulate roof endorsements differently. Your state insurance department's consumer pages are the best place to check.

What to ask the insurer

  1. Which endorsement and schedule did you apply, by form number?
  2. What roof age did you use, and what is the source?
  3. Show the calculation from replacement cost to payment.

Quick answers

Can my insurer pay less because my roof is old?

If your policy has an actual cash value roof endorsement or a roof payment schedule, yes. Without one, a replacement cost policy generally pays replacement cost after you repair, subject to depreciation holdback.

How do I prove my roof's age?

A building permit, roofer's invoice, warranty registration or inspection report showing the installation date.

Can a Florida insurer drop me because my roof is old?

Not solely because the roof is under 15 years old. For older roofs, an inspection showing at least 5 years of remaining life prevents refusal based only on age.
