Can an insurer depreciate labor when paying actual cash value?

Depreciation reduces some estimates from replacement cost to an actual-cash-value amount. Whether labor may be depreciated is a separate question that depends on policy wording and applicable law. Ask how the estimate was calculated before assuming a deduction is permitted or prohibited.

States are split. Some hold labor is not depreciable as a matter of law, some permit it when the policy says so clearly, and some have resolved it by statute.

The difference is often thousands of dollars on the first check, and it compounds if the depreciation is never recovered because repairs are not completed in time.

Rules differ by state and by policy wording. Use the searches below to read what courts in your own state have said, then check that a case is still good law before relying on it.

Labor depreciation rules by state: a research matrix

There is no single national rule answering whether an insurance company may depreciate labor when calculating actual cash value. The result can depend on the jurisdiction, the policy’s definitions, the type of property coverage and the date of the loss. This matrix summarizes identified authorities as research starting points; it is not a legal conclusion about a particular claim.

What to gather

  • The estimate showing replacement cost, depreciation, and the initial payment calculation.
  • Any line-item split between material, labor, equipment, and other costs.
  • The policy’s valuation provisions and relevant endorsements.
  • The written explanation of age, condition, expected useful life, and depreciation method.

A practical example

If one line combines material and installation, a single depreciation amount may not show which cost components were reduced. Request the breakdown. A calculation that is hard to follow should be clarified before you decide what to challenge.

How to review the issue

Check the arithmetic separately from the legal question. Record the amount deducted and the stated basis. If replacement-cost benefits may be recoverable after repair, check the conditions, required documents, and deadlines in the policy.
