How do I read the insurance adjuster's estimate?

Leer en español

Most property estimates, whatever software produced them, follow the same layout: a list of areas (rooms, roof slopes, elevations), the line items for each area, and a summary page that turns replacement cost into what you're actually paid. Once you know where each number comes from, you can check it.

The short answer. Read the summary first to see how the payment was calculated, then go area by area: is every damaged area listed, are the quantities right, and is every step of the repair there? Most underpayments are missing items or short quantities, not wrong prices.

The parts of an estimate

PartWhat it tells youWhat to check
HeaderClaim number, date of loss, insured, adjuster, price list or pricing dateRight address and date of loss; pricing dated near the repair, not years earlier
AreasEach room or roof section with its measurementsEvery damaged area is listed; room sizes and roof squares match reality
Line itemsDescription, quantity, unit (SF, LF, SQ, EA), unit priceTear-out and replacement both listed; quantities match the measurements
RCVReplacement cost value for each lineQuantity × unit price, plus tax where shown
DepreciationAmount withheld for age and conditionWhether it is marked recoverable; whether labor was depreciated
ACVRCV minus depreciationThis is usually the first check you receive
SummaryTotals, O&P, tax, deductible, prior payments, net claimDeductible applied once; prior payments correct; O&P included when a general contractor is needed

Units you'll see

  • SF: square feet (drywall, flooring, paint).
  • LF: linear feet (baseboard, gutters, drip edge).
  • SQ: a roofing square, 100 square feet of roof.
  • EA: each (a fixture, a vent, a light).
  • HR: hours of labor for a trade.

How to check it, step by step

  1. Start at the summary. Write down RCV, depreciation, ACV, deductible and net payment. Make sure the math adds up.
  2. List the damaged areas yourself before reading theirs. Then mark any area missing from the estimate.
  3. Measure. Compare room dimensions and roof squares with your own measurements or a contractor's.
  4. Follow each repair start to finish. A wall repair should include removal, disposal, new material, finishing, primer and paint. Missing steps are common.
  5. Check depreciation. See whether it's recoverable and whether labor was depreciated; rules differ by state. See labor depreciation.
  6. Look for O&P. If the job needs several trades coordinated, ask why overhead and profit is missing. See overhead and profit.
  7. Write your questions down line by line and send them in writing.

Florida: you're entitled to the estimate

In Florida, the insurer must send the policyholder a copy of any detailed estimate of the loss within 7 days after its adjuster generates it (Fla. Stat. 627.70131(3)(e)). Other states may not have a rule this specific; ask for the estimate in writing anyway.

Quick answers

Why is my insurance check less than the estimate total?

The first check is usually actual cash value: replacement cost minus depreciation and your deductible. Recoverable depreciation may be paid later, after repairs.

Can I ask the adjuster to explain a line item?

Yes. Send your questions in writing, list each line by number, and ask for the measurement or reason behind it.

Is the insurance estimate final?

No. Estimates are often revised through supplements, re-inspections, appraisal or negotiation when items are missing or wrong.
