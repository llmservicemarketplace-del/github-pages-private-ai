How should I keep track of calls and letters with my insurance company?

Leer en español

Claims take months, and adjusters change. A simple log of every contact protects you: it shows what was asked, what was promised and when, and it's the backbone of any complaint, demand or lawsuit.

The short answer. After every call, write down the date, time, person, what was said and what happens next. Then send a short email confirming the call. Keep the log and every document in one place.

Confirm calls in writing

A quick email after each call turns a phone conversation into a record:

Thank you for speaking with me today, [date], about Claim No. [number]. My understanding is that [what was agreed], and that you will [next step] by [date]. Please let me know in writing if I have misunderstood anything.

Why dates matter

  • Many states set deadlines for insurers to acknowledge, investigate and pay. Your log shows whether they were met. See insurer deadlines by state.
  • Your own deadlines (proof of loss, replacement cost, lawsuit) run from specific dates. See lawsuit deadlines.
  • Pre-suit demands such as Georgia's 60-day demand depend on provable dates.

Keep it simple

A notebook, spreadsheet or document works. Save every letter and email as a PDF in one folder, named by date (2026-11-03 adjuster email.pdf). Keep the originals of anything the insurer mails you.

Quick answers

Should I record calls with my insurance adjuster?

Recording laws differ by state; some require everyone's consent. A safer habit is to take notes and confirm each call by email the same day.

What should I write down after a call with my insurer?

Date, time, who you spoke with, what was said, what documents were exchanged, and the promised next step and date.

Why keep a claim log?

It shows what was promised and when, and supports complaints, demands and lawsuits if the claim is delayed or underpaid.
