What can I do if my insurer underpaid my claim?

Many claims aren't denied outright. Instead, the insurer pays less than the repairs will cost. Common reasons: missing items, low quantities, low unit prices, depreciation, or a partial denial of some damage.

You don't have to accept the first number.

The short answer. Compare the insurer's estimate line by line with a contractor's estimate. List each difference with proof. Send a written response asking for the difference. If that fails, consider appraisal, a complaint, mediation, or a public adjuster or attorney. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • Whether the gap is scope (what's included), quantity, price, or coverage.
  • Whether depreciation will be paid back after repairs.
  • Your deadlines.

Find the gap

  • Scope: rooms or items missing entirely.
  • Quantity: square footage or counts too low.
  • Price: unit prices below local cost.
  • Depreciation: held back until repairs are done. See ACV vs. replacement cost.
  • O&P, matching, code items: see O&P, matching, ordinance or law.

Your options

  1. Written response with your estimate and proof.
  2. Request a re-inspection.
  3. Appraisal, if your policy has it and the dispute is about amount.
  4. Mediation or a state complaint.
  5. A public adjuster or attorney.

What your file needs

  • Insurer's estimate and payment letter.
  • Contractor's line-item estimate.
  • Photos and measurements.
  • Your claim log.

Quick answers

What do I do if my insurance payout is too low?

Compare the insurer's estimate line by line with a contractor's estimate, send a written response with proof, and request a re-inspection. Appraisal, mediation, complaints, or professional help are next steps.

Will my insurer pay back depreciation?

If your policy pays replacement cost, the insurer usually pays withheld depreciation after repairs are completed and documented, within the policy's time limits.
