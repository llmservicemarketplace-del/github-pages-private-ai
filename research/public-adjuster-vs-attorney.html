Should I hire a public adjuster or an attorney for my insurance claim?

A public adjuster is a licensed claims professional you hire to prepare and negotiate your claim. An attorney can do that too, and can also sue, handle bad faith issues, and represent you in court.

Both are usually paid a percentage of what you recover. Many states cap public adjuster fees.

The short answer. For a valuation dispute on a covered loss, a public adjuster may be enough. For a denial, a coverage fight, an EUO, or a deadline to sue, talk to an attorney. Either way, read the contract, the fee, and the cancellation terms. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • Whether the dispute is about the amount or about coverage.
  • How close you are to a lawsuit deadline.
  • The size of the claim and the fee.

Fees

Public adjuster fees are often a percentage of the claim payments they help obtain. Some states cap them. In Florida, the cap is generally 20% of claim payments, and 10% for claims made in the first year after a governor-declared emergency. Fees can't be charged on money the insurer already paid or agreed to pay before you signed.

Questions to ask before hiring

  • Are you licensed in my state? (Check with the state insurance department.)
  • What is your fee, and is it charged on amounts already paid?
  • Who will handle my file day to day?
  • How do I cancel, and is there a cancellation period?
  • For an attorney: do you handle property insurance cases, and how are costs paid?
Check the license of any public adjuster with your state insurance department, and watch for door-to-door solicitation after a storm. Many states restrict when and how public adjusters may solicit.

What your file needs

  • The contract and fee terms.
  • Proof of license.
  • A summary of your claim and what's disputed.

Quick answers

How much do public adjusters charge in Florida?

Generally no more than 20% of claim payments, and no more than 10% for claims made in the first year after a governor-declared emergency.

Do I need an attorney or a public adjuster?

A public adjuster can help with valuation disputes. For denials, coverage disputes, examinations under oath, or lawsuit deadlines, talk to an attorney.
