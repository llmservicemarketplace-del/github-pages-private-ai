How is a hurricane or wind deductible applied to a claim?

A deductible may be a fixed dollar amount or a percentage calculated on a stated basis. Separate wind, hurricane, flood, or other deductibles may apply under different coverage terms. Read the declarations and endorsements together; the percentage alone is not enough to calculate the amount.

When the percentage applies, how it is calculated, and whether more than one can be applied in a single season are all litigated questions. Several coastal states regulate the trigger by statute.

Check which deductible the carrier applied, and to what base. A hurricane deductible applied to a wind loss that was not a named storm is a common and correctable error.

Rules differ by state and by policy wording. Use the searches below to read what courts in your own state have said, then check that a case is still good law before relying on it.

What to gather

  • The declarations page and deductible endorsements in effect on the loss date.
  • The carrier’s calculation, including the percentage and the amount it was applied to.
  • The loss date, location, and the carrier’s explanation of the applicable event category.
  • The estimate and payment history showing where the deductible was applied.

A practical example

For illustration only: 2% of a stated $250,000 insured value is $5,000. That is different from 2% of a $20,000 repair estimate, which is $400. Your policy identifies the relevant basis; this example does not identify your deductible.

How to review the issue

Check the arithmetic, the deductible basis, and which coverage the deduction affects. If multiple payments or coverages are involved, ask for a written reconciliation. Do not assume the same deductible applies to every cause of loss or every separate policy.
