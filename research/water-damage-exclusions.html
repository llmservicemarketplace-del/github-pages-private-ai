Why was my water damage claim denied as "long-term" or "repeated seepage"?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or failed supply line. Many standard forms exclude continuous or repeated seepage or leakage over a period of weeks, commonly 14 days or more.

Flooding from outside, surface water, and sewer backup are usually excluded too, unless you have flood insurance or a water backup endorsement.

The short answer. Find out which exclusion the insurer used and what evidence shows a long-term leak. Show when the leak started and when you found it: plumber's reports, the failed part, photos, and water bills. Check whether your policy has an exception for hidden leaks. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • When the leak started and when it was discoverable.
  • The exact water exclusion wording and any exceptions.
  • The source of the water: plumbing, roof, surface water, or sewer.

Evidence that helps

  • The failed part (supply line, valve, fitting). Keep it.
  • A plumber's written report on what failed and how.
  • Water bills showing when usage spiked.
  • Photos of staining, mold, or rot, which can help date the leak.

Quick answers

Does homeowners insurance cover slow leaks?

Often not. Many policies exclude continuous or repeated seepage or leakage over a period such as 14 days or more. Some have exceptions for hidden leaks.

Is flooding covered by homeowners insurance?

Usually not. Flood damage generally requires a separate flood policy, such as one through the NFIP.
