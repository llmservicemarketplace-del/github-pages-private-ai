How do I get a copy of my policy and the adjuster's estimate, photos and notes?

Leer en español

You can't check a decision without the documents it was based on. Start by asking for two things: the full policy, and the documents behind the claim decision.

The short answer. Ask in writing for a certified copy of the complete policy, including the declarations and every endorsement in effect on the date of loss, and for the estimate, photos, measurements and any expert reports used to decide your claim. Keep proof of when you asked.

What to ask for

The policy

  • A certified copy of the complete policy in effect on the date of loss.
  • The declarations page.
  • Every endorsement and amendment, with form numbers. The endorsements often hold the limits that matter: mold, roof payment schedules, cosmetic damage, hurricane deductibles.

The claim documents

  • Every estimate and revised estimate.
  • The adjuster's photos and measurements, including any aerial or drone measurements.
  • Engineer, inspector or lab reports. See engineer reports.
  • The written reason for any denial or partial denial, with the policy language relied on.
  • A payment history for the claim.

Sample request

Re: Claim No. [number], Policy No. [number], Date of loss [date]

Please send me: (1) a certified copy of my complete policy in effect on [date of loss], including the declarations and all endorsements; (2) all estimates prepared on this claim; (3) all photographs, measurements and diagrams; (4) any engineer, inspector or consultant reports; and (5) a written statement of the specific policy provisions relied on for any denial or reduction. Please send them to [email/address]. Thank you.

What the law requires

Rules differ by state. Florida, for example, requires an insurer to send the policyholder a copy of any detailed estimate within 7 days after the adjuster generates it (Fla. Stat. 627.70131(3)(e)). Many states' unfair claims practices rules require insurers to explain a denial with reference to the policy. Not every state gives you a right to the insurer's internal notes; ask anyway, and in a lawsuit those documents can be requested through discovery.

If the insurer doesn't answer, follow up in writing and consider a complaint to your state insurance department.

Quick answers

Can I get a copy of my insurance policy?

Yes. Ask your insurer or agent in writing for a certified copy of the complete policy in effect on the date of loss, including all endorsements.

Can I get the adjuster's photos?

Ask in writing. Many insurers will provide them. If they won't, the request can be made through discovery if the dispute goes to court.

Why does a certified copy matter?

It confirms which forms and endorsements were actually in effect, which can decide coverage questions.
