Business Interruption Claims for Small and Home-Based Businesses

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules differ by state and change over time, so confirm with the official sources below.

Business interruption (or business income) coverage can replace lost profit and pay continuing expenses while a business is shut down by covered damage. It's usually part of a commercial property or business owner's policy. A standard homeowners policy usually doesn't cover a home-based business's lost income.

What it typically covers

CoverageWhat it pays
Business incomeNet income you would have earned, plus continuing normal operating expenses like payroll, rent, and loan payments
Extra expenseReasonable costs to keep operating or reopen sooner, like a temporary location or rented equipment
Civil authorityLost income when officials bar access to your area because of nearby covered damage (often for a limited time)

Most policies require direct physical damage to the property from a covered cause, and many have a waiting period (often 72 hours) before coverage starts. Coverage lasts for the "period of restoration," the reasonable time to repair and reopen.

Home-based businesses

  • Homeowners policies usually offer little or no business coverage.
  • A home business endorsement or a separate business policy may be needed. Check now, before the next storm.

Records that prove your loss

  • Tax returns for the past 2 to 3 years
  • Monthly profit-and-loss statements and bank statements, including the same months last year
  • Payroll records, rent, and loan statements
  • Receipts for extra expenses
  • A timeline: when you closed, partially reopened, and fully reopened

Business income claims often turn on records. Keep backups off-site or in the cloud. See document damage and replacing lost documents.

If insurance doesn't cover it

  • SBA Economic Injury Disaster Loans may help small businesses and nonprofits with working capital after a declared disaster, even without physical damage. See SBA disaster loans.
  • Workers who lose income may qualify for Disaster Unemployment Assistance, including some self-employed people.

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover my home business income?

Usually not. You may need a home business endorsement or a separate business policy.

What proves a business interruption claim?

Tax returns, monthly profit-and-loss and bank statements, payroll and expense records, and a clear timeline of the shutdown.

Is there help if my business wasn't damaged but lost customers?

After declared disasters, SBA Economic Injury Disaster Loans may help small businesses with working capital even without physical damage.
