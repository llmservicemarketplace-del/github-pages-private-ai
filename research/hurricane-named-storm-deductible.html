How does a hurricane deductible work?

Leer en español

In hurricane-prone states, many policies have a separate deductible for hurricane or named-storm losses. It's usually a percentage of your dwelling coverage, not a flat dollar amount, so it can be much larger than your regular deductible.

The short answer. Find the hurricane or named-storm deductible on your declarations page and what triggers it. Multiply the percentage by your dwelling (Coverage A) limit, not by the damage. Then check that the storm actually met the trigger on the date of your loss.

The math

A 2% hurricane deductible on a home insured for $250,000 is $5,000. If the covered damage is $12,000, the payment before depreciation is $7,000. The deductible is based on the coverage limit, not the size of the loss.

What triggers it

Policies define the trigger differently: a hurricane watch or warning, a named storm by the National Hurricane Center, a declared hurricane making landfall in the state, or a set number of hours before and after. If damage happened outside the trigger window, your regular deductible should apply.

Florida's rules

  • Insurers must offer hurricane deductible options of $500, 2%, 5% or 10%, with some exceptions.
  • The hurricane deductible applies once per calendar year to covered hurricane losses under policies with the same insurer or insurer group.
  • It applies to losses from the time a hurricane watch or warning is issued for any part of Florida by the National Hurricane Center until 72 hours after the last watch or warning ends.
  • For a second hurricane in the same year with the same insurer or group, the deductible is the greater of the unused balance of the hurricane deductible or your all-other-perils deductible.

Source: Florida Department of Financial Services, citing Fla. Stat. 627.701.

Check the insurer's work

  1. Confirm the percentage and dwelling limit on the declarations page.
  2. Confirm the storm met your policy's trigger on the date of loss.
  3. In Florida, check whether a hurricane deductible was already partly or fully met earlier in the year.
  4. Make sure only one deductible was applied to the loss.

Quick answers

How is a hurricane deductible calculated?

Usually as a percentage of your dwelling coverage limit. A 2% deductible on $250,000 of coverage is $5,000, whatever the size of the loss.

Does a hurricane deductible apply to every storm?

Only to storms that meet the policy's trigger, such as a named storm or a National Hurricane Center watch or warning. Otherwise the regular deductible applies.

In Florida, do I pay the hurricane deductible twice in one year?

No, not with the same insurer or group. It applies once per calendar year; a later hurricane uses the greater of any unused balance or your all-other-perils deductible.
