The Coinsurance Clause and the 80% Rule, Explained

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules differ by state and change over time, so confirm with the official sources below.

Some property policies require you to insure a building for at least a set percentage of its value, often 80%. If you carry less, the insurer may pay only part of even a small claim. This is called a coinsurance penalty. It's most common on commercial and rental property policies, and many homeowners policies have a similar rule for getting replacement cost.

The formula

(Insurance you carry ÷ insurance required) × loss − deductible = payment

"Insurance required" is the coinsurance percentage times the property's value (replacement cost or actual cash value, depending on the policy).

Example

Fully insuredUnderinsured
Building value$500,000$500,000
Required at 80%$400,000$400,000
Coverage you carry$400,000$300,000
Loss$100,000$100,000
Payment (before deductible)$100,000$75,000 (300 ÷ 400 = 75%)

The underinsured owner gets $25,000 less on the same loss, even though the loss was far below the policy limit.

Homeowners policies: "insurance to value"

Many homeowners policies say you get full replacement cost only if your dwelling limit is at least 80% of the home's replacement cost. If it's lower, the insurer may pay the greater of actual cash value or a reduced share. See replacement cost vs. actual cash value.

How to protect yourself

  • Find the coinsurance or insurance-to-value wording in your policy and the percentage it uses.
  • Get a current replacement cost estimate. Construction costs rise, especially after big disasters.
  • Ask your agent about an inflation guard, an agreed value endorsement (which can waive coinsurance on commercial policies), or extended replacement cost.
  • Review limits every year and after renovations.

If a penalty was applied to your claim

  • Ask the insurer for the value they used and how they calculated it.
  • Challenge the value with your own estimate or appraisal.
  • Check whether your agent told you the limit was adequate. That may matter in a dispute.
  • File a complaint with your state insurance department if the math looks wrong.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 80% coinsurance rule?

A policy condition requiring you to insure property for at least 80% of its value. If you carry less, the insurer pays only a proportional share of covered losses.

Does coinsurance apply to homeowners insurance?

Many homeowners policies have a similar insurance-to-value rule for paying full replacement cost. Check your policy's loss settlement section.

How do I avoid a coinsurance penalty?

Keep your limit at or above the required percentage of current replacement cost, and ask about agreed value or inflation guard endorsements.
