What is an examination under oath, and can I refuse one?

Leer en español

An examination under oath (EUO) is a formal, sworn interview by the insurer's attorney, recorded by a court reporter. Many homeowners policies list it as a condition you must meet if the insurer asks.

Insurers often request an EUO in large claims, fire claims, or when something in the file raises questions.

The short answer. Take an EUO request seriously. Refusing or not showing up can give the insurer a reason to deny the claim. Ask for the request in writing, ask what documents they want, and strongly consider having an attorney with you. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • Whether your policy contains an EUO condition.
  • Whether the request and document demands are reasonable.
  • Whether you have legal representation.

What to expect

  • A court reporter records everything under oath.
  • The insurer's attorney asks about the loss, the property, your finances, prior claims, and your documents.
  • You may be asked to bring tax returns, bank records, receipts, and photos.
  • You can usually review and sign the transcript afterward.

How to prepare

  1. Get the request and document list in writing.
  2. Gather documents early and keep copies of what you give.
  3. Review your timeline and earlier statements so your answers are consistent.
  4. Answer truthfully and only what is asked. Never guess.
  5. Talk with an attorney before the EUO if you can.

What your file needs

  • The written EUO request and document list.
  • Copies of every document you produce.
  • Your claim log and earlier statements.

Quick answers

Can I refuse an examination under oath?

If your policy requires it, refusing can give the insurer grounds to deny the claim. Get legal advice before declining.

Can I bring a lawyer to an examination under oath?

Generally yes, and it is strongly recommended.
