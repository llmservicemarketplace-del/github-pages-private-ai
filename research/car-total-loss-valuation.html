Car Totaled? How to Check and Challenge the Insurance Valuation

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or professional advice. Rules differ by state and change over time, so confirm with the official sources below.

When a car is declared a total loss, the insurer usually pays its actual cash value: what a similar car would have sold for right before the loss. Most insurers get that number from a valuation report built by a vendor such as CCC, Mitchell, or Audatex. Those reports rely on comparable vehicles, and if the comparables don't match your car, the offer can be too low.

1. Ask for the full valuation report

Ask the adjuster for the complete report, not just the final number. It should list the comparable vehicles used, their prices, and any adjustments for mileage, options, and condition.

2. Check every comparable

CheckWhy it matters
Same year, make, model, and trimA base model priced against your higher trim lowers the value
Similar mileageAdjustments should be reasonable, not huge
Options and packagesMissing features (towing, leather, safety packages) should be added back
Condition rating"Below average" deductions need proof: ask for photos or notes
LocationComparables should come from your area
Real listingsAsk for the dealer name or listing for each comparable

3. Send your own evidence

  • Listings for truly similar cars for sale near you
  • Receipts for recent work: new tires, brakes, a new transmission, or aftermarket equipment
  • Photos showing your car's condition before the loss
  • The window sticker or build sheet showing your trim and options

Put your objections in writing: list each problem with the report and what you believe the value should be.

4. Ask about taxes and fees

In many states, a total loss payment must include sales tax and title or registration fees for a replacement car. Ask your insurer whether they're included and check with your state insurance department.

5. If you still disagree

  • Many auto policies include an appraisal clause for disputes about value. Each side hires an appraiser and a neutral umpire decides. See how appraisal works.
  • File a free complaint with your state insurance department.
  • If you owe more than the car is worth, check whether you have gap coverage.

Frequently asked questions

Can I negotiate a total loss offer?

Yes. Ask for the full valuation report, point out comparable vehicles that don't match your car, and send your own local listings and receipts in writing.

What is a CCC valuation report?

A report from a vendor many insurers use to estimate a totaled car's value from comparable vehicles. You can ask your insurer for a copy.

Does the insurer have to pay sales tax on a totaled car?

In many states the total loss payment must include sales tax and some fees. Check with your insurer and your state insurance department.
