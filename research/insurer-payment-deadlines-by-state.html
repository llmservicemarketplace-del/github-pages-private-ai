How long does my insurance company have to pay my claim?

Most states set deadlines for insurers to acknowledge a claim, start investigating, decide and pay. The clocks usually start from specific events, such as notice of the claim or receipt of a complete proof of loss, so dates matter. The table below covers four Southeastern states; for others, check your state insurance department.

The short answer. Find your state's rule, mark the date the clock started, and keep a claim log. Missed deadlines can support a complaint, interest or penalties, but the rules have exceptions and conditions.

Florida

  • Acknowledge a communication about a claim within 7 calendar days.
  • Begin the investigation within 7 days after receiving proof-of-loss statements; complete any physical inspection within 30 days of them.
  • Pay or deny an initial, reopened or supplemental residential claim within 60 days after notice, unless factors beyond the insurer's control prevent it. Late payments bear interest from the date of notice.

Fla. Stat. 627.70131 (2026).

Texas

  • Within 15 days after notice: acknowledge the claim, begin investigating, and request the items needed.
  • Accept or reject in writing within 15 business days after receiving everything requested. If it needs more time, it must say why, and decide within 45 days after that notice.
  • Pay within 5 business days after notifying you it will pay.
  • Late payment can carry statutory interest and attorney's fees; for most residential property claims, Chapter 542A changes the interest calculation. See Texas claim delay.

Tex. Ins. Code 542.055–.058, .060; ch. 542A.

Georgia

  • Acknowledge a first-party claim and send proof of loss forms within 15 days.
  • Affirm or deny liability within 15 days of receiving a completed proof of loss, or 30 days after the claim is reported if none is required. With notice and reasons, the total may not exceed 60 days.
  • Pay within 10 days after coverage is confirmed and the amount is settled.
  • Bad-faith penalty after a 60-day demand: up to 50% of the loss or $5,000, whichever is greater, plus attorney's fees. See Georgia's 60-day demand.

Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. 120-2-52-.03; O.C.G.A. 33-4-6.

Louisiana

  • Begin loss adjustment within 14 days after notice of loss, or 30 days for a catastrophic loss; the commissioner can extend this after a declared emergency.
  • Pay the amount due within 30 days after receiving satisfactory proof of loss, and make a written offer to settle within the period the statute sets.
  • Arbitrary or capricious late payment can bring a penalty of 50% of the amount found due.

La. R.S. 22:1892.

Other states

Most states have similar rules in their unfair claims settlement practices laws. Your state insurance department's consumer site usually lists them. See our state guides.

Quick answers

How long does an insurance company have to pay a homeowners claim?

It depends on the state. In Florida, insurers must generally pay or deny within 60 days of notice; Texas, Georgia and Louisiana use deadlines tied to proof of loss. See the table above.

When does the clock start?

Usually from notice of the claim or from receipt of a complete proof of loss, depending on the state and the step. Keep proof of every date.

What happens if the insurer misses the deadline?

Depending on the state, you may be owed interest or penalties, and you can file a complaint with the insurance department. Conditions apply, so check the statute.
