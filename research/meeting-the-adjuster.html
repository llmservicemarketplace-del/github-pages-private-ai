What happens when the insurance adjuster inspects my home?

After you report a claim, the insurer usually sends an adjuster to inspect the damage. The adjuster works for the insurance company (or an independent firm it hires). Their report and estimate largely decide your first payment.

You can't control the adjuster, but you can control what they see and what's in the file.

The short answer. Be there for the inspection, walk the adjuster through every damaged area, hand over a written list of damage with your photos, and ask for a copy of the estimate and any photos or reports they rely on. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • Whether all damaged areas, including attic, crawlspace, and roof, were inspected.
  • Whether the estimate includes every item you showed them.
  • Whether the adjuster brings in an engineer or other expert.

Before the visit

  • Make a room-by-room damage list and print photos taken right after the loss.
  • Have your contractor's estimate ready if you have one, or ask the contractor to attend.
  • Note hidden damage you suspect: wet insulation, swollen subfloor, soft decking.

During the visit

  • Walk every room and the exterior with the adjuster. Point to each item on your list.
  • Take your own notes and photos of what they look at.
  • Ask: "When will I get the estimate?" and "Is anyone else coming, like an engineer?"
  • You don't have to agree to anything or sign anything on the spot.

After the visit

  • Ask for the written estimate, and compare it line by line with the damage. See comparing insurance estimates.
  • Send a written list of anything missing. That starts a supplement.

What your file needs

  • A room-by-room list of damage.
  • Dated photos and video.
  • Your contractor's estimate, if any.
  • Notes from the inspection.

Quick answers

Does the insurance adjuster work for me?

No. The insurer's staff or independent adjuster works for the insurance company. A public adjuster is a licensed professional you can hire to represent you.

Do I have to accept the adjuster's estimate?

No. You can send missing items, ask for a re-inspection, submit your own estimate, or use your policy's dispute options.
