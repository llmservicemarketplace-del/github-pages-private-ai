Do I have to give the insurance company a recorded statement?

Leer en español

An adjuster may ask to record a phone interview about your claim. A recorded statement is usually informal: no court reporter, often no oath.

Your policy requires you to cooperate with the investigation. Whether that includes a recorded statement depends on the policy wording. A sworn examination under oath is a separate, more formal requirement in many policies.

The short answer. Read your policy's duties-after-loss section first. If you agree, schedule it for a time you're prepared, stick to facts you know, say "I don't know" when you don't, and ask for a copy of the recording or transcript. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • What your policy says about statements and cooperation.
  • Whether the request is an informal recorded statement or a formal EUO.
  • Whether you have a public adjuster or attorney.

How to prepare

  • Review your timeline, photos, and claim log before the call.
  • Answer only the question asked. Don't guess at dates, costs, or causes.
  • It's fine to say, "I'd need to check my records."
  • Ask for a copy of the recording or transcript afterward.
  • You can ask to reschedule if you aren't ready, or have your representative present.

Why it matters

What you say becomes part of the claim file. Inconsistent dates or guesses can be used to question the claim. Accurate, careful answers protect you.

What your file needs

  • Your timeline of events.
  • Your claim log.
  • Photos and receipts you may refer to.
  • Your policy's duties-after-loss section.

Quick answers

Is a recorded statement the same as an examination under oath?

No. A recorded statement is usually an informal recorded interview. An examination under oath is a formal, sworn proceeding that many policies specifically require.

Can I have someone with me during a recorded statement?

Generally yes. You can ask to have a public adjuster or attorney present, or reschedule to arrange it.
