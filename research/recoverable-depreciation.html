How do I get the recoverable depreciation the insurer held back?

With replacement cost coverage, many insurers pay in two steps. First they pay actual cash value (replacement cost minus depreciation). Then, once you repair or replace, they pay the depreciation they held back. That second payment is "recoverable depreciation."

The short answer. Find the deadline in your policy, finish the repair, and send the insurer proof of what you spent: the final invoice, proof of payment and photos. Ask in writing for the held-back depreciation. Keep copies.

Step by step

  1. Read the estimate summary. Find the line for recoverable depreciation. If depreciation is marked non-recoverable, ask why in writing.
  2. Find your deadline. Look in the replacement cost loss settlement section of your policy. Some forms give a set number of days after the loss to tell the insurer you intend to claim replacement cost. State law can extend it.
  3. Repair or replace. Keep every invoice and receipt.
  4. Send proof. Final contractor invoice, proof of payment, completion photos, and a short letter with the claim number asking for the held-back amount.
  5. Follow up in writing and log every contact in your claim log.

Deadlines and state rules

  • Policy time limits. Many homeowners forms set a deadline to notify the insurer that you'll claim replacement cost. Read yours; if you can't finish in time, write to the insurer before the deadline asking for an extension.
  • California. Insurers can't set a time limit shorter than 12 months from the first actual cash value payment to collect full replacement cost, or 36 months for a loss related to a declared state of emergency, with six-month extensions for good cause (Cal. Ins. Code 2051.5).
  • Florida. If a roof deductible applies, the insurer may limit the roof payment to actual cash value until it receives reasonable proof that you paid the roof deductible (Fla. Stat. 627.7011(3)(a)).

Common problems

  • The repair cost less than the estimate. Insurers usually pay what you actually spent, up to the estimate.
  • You did the work yourself. Document materials and your own time; rules on paying for your labor vary.
  • You chose not to rebuild. Many policies then pay only actual cash value.

Quick answers

What is recoverable depreciation?

It's the part of the replacement cost the insurer holds back until you repair or replace. Once you show the work is done and what it cost, it's paid up to the amount actually spent.

Is there a deadline to claim recoverable depreciation?

Usually yes. Check the replacement cost section of your policy; state law can lengthen it. California requires at least 12 months, or 36 months after a declared state of emergency.

What if I don't rebuild?

Many policies then limit payment to actual cash value, so the held-back depreciation isn't paid.
