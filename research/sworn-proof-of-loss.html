What is a sworn proof of loss, and what's the deadline?

A proof of loss is a signed, sworn statement of how much you are claiming and why. Many standard homeowners policies require you to send one within 60 days after the insurer requests it. Check your policy: some have different wording or deadlines.

Flood insurance under the NFIP has its own, stricter rules. See NFIP proof of loss.

The short answer. If the insurer requests a proof of loss, calendar the deadline, fill it out completely and truthfully, attach your estimate and inventory, and send it in a trackable way. If you can't finish in time, ask for an extension in writing before the deadline. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • Whether the insurer requested it, and when.
  • Your policy's proof-of-loss wording and deadline.
  • Whether you can support each amount claimed.

What a proof of loss usually includes

  • Date, time, and cause of loss.
  • Your interest in the property and any other insurance.
  • Any mortgage holder or liens.
  • Amount claimed for the building, contents, and extra living expenses.
  • Supporting estimates, inventories, bills, and receipts.

Common mistakes

  • Missing the deadline.
  • Signing the insurer's pre-filled form with their lower number without understanding it. Ask whether you can submit your own figures.
  • Guessing at amounts. Every number should have support.
  • Not keeping a copy and proof of delivery.

What your file needs

  • The insurer's written request and the form.
  • Your contractor's or your own estimate.
  • Contents inventory.
  • Receipts for living expenses and repairs.

Quick answers

When is a proof of loss due?

Many standard homeowners policies require it within 60 days after the insurer requests it. Check your policy, and ask in writing for an extension if needed.

Is a homeowners proof of loss the same as an NFIP proof of loss?

No. NFIP flood policies have their own strict proof-of-loss rules and deadlines.
