How to File an Insurance Complaint in Texas

Last reviewed November 14, 2026. Written by The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit educational resource. General information only, not legal advice. We are not a government agency and we charge nothing for these guides.

Short answer: In Texas, insurance complaints go to the Texas Department of Insurance. Filing is free and you do not need a lawyer. The consumer line is (800) 252-3439.

Start here: Texas Department of Insurance: get help with a complaint.

If your insurance company denied a claim, paid too little, or stopped answering, you can ask the state regulator to step in. This page covers who takes the complaint in Texas, what to send, what the regulator can and cannot do, and what to try next if the complaint does not fix the problem.

What the regulator can and cannot do

It can:

  • Send your complaint to the company and require a written answer.
  • Check whether the company followed Texas insurance law and the policy language.
  • Push the company to fix a clear violation, such as ignoring a claim or missing a legal deadline.

It cannot:

  • Act as your lawyer or give legal advice.
  • Decide disputed facts, such as what caused the damage or how much a repair should cost.
  • Order payment when the company followed the law and the policy.
A complaint does not stop the clock. Deadlines in your policy and the deadline to sue keep running while the regulator reviews your complaint. See lawsuit deadlines.

What to gather before you file

  • Your policy number and claim number
  • The company's name exactly as it appears on the policy
  • The denial letter, payment letter, or estimate you disagree with
  • Your own estimate, photos, or repair invoices
  • A short timeline: dates you reported the loss, dates of inspections, calls, and letters
  • One or two sentences on what you want: a written explanation, a reinspection, payment of a specific amount

If you do not have the company's full file, you can ask for it. See how to request your claim file and policy and keeping a claim communication log.

Step by step: filing an insurance complaint in Texas

  1. Ask the company first, in writing. Request the reason for the decision and the policy language it relied on. A regulator will usually ask whether you tried this.
  2. Open the official complaint page. Use Texas Department of Insurance: get help with a complaint. Do not use a paid site that offers to file for you.
  3. State the facts in date order. Keep it short. Say what happened, what the company did, and what you are asking for.
  4. Attach copies, not originals. Include the letters and estimates listed above.
  5. Save your confirmation or file number. You will need it to check status or add documents.
  6. Read the company's response. The regulator forwards your complaint and the company must answer. You normally get a copy or a summary.
  7. Decide your next step. If the answer does not resolve it, look at the options below.

If the complaint does not fix it

  • Ask for a reinspection or file a supplemental claim with new evidence.
  • Use the policy's appraisal clause when the fight is about the dollar amount, or ask about mediation.
  • Compare a public adjuster and an attorney.
  • For a smaller amount, see small claims court in Texas.
  • If you cannot afford a lawyer, see free legal aid in Texas.

Complaints that go somewhere else

  • Flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program: these policies are federal. Appeals go to FEMA, not the state. See flood insurance basics.
  • Health coverage from a large employer: many employer plans are self-funded and regulated by the U.S. Department of Labor, not the state. Call the Employee Benefits Security Administration at 1-866-444-3272.
  • Medicare: call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227). See Medicare appeals.
  • Scams and business practices outside insurance: use the Attorney General consumer complaint portal.

Texas insurance rules worth knowing

Insurance: prompt payment and 18% interest

Texas law sets deadlines for insurers to acknowledge, decide, and pay claims, and late payment can add 18% annual interest. See Texas claim delay.

Common questions

Who do I complain to about an insurance company in Texas?

The Texas Department of Insurance. It regulates insurers that sell policies in Texas and takes complaints from policyholders.

Does it cost anything to file an insurance complaint in Texas?

No. Filing a complaint with the state insurance regulator is free, and you do not need a lawyer to do it.

How do I file an insurance complaint in Texas?

Use the complaint page of the Texas Department of Insurance. The consumer line is (800) 252-3439. Have your policy number, claim number, the company's letters, and a short dated timeline ready.

Will a complaint get my claim paid?

Sometimes. The regulator can make the company answer in writing and can act when the company broke state insurance law or the policy. It cannot decide disputed facts, such as what caused the damage or what a repair should cost, and it is not your lawyer.
