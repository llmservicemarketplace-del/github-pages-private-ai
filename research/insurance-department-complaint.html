How do I file a complaint against my insurance company?

Every state has a department of insurance that regulates insurers and takes consumer complaints. Filing is free. The department usually sends your complaint to the insurer and requires a written response.

A complaint is often the fastest way to get a stalled claim moving.

The short answer. Find your state's department through the NAIC website, file online, and attach a short timeline, your claim log, and key letters. Be specific about what you want: a decision, a payment, a re-inspection, or an explanation. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • Which state's department regulates your policy.
  • Whether the problem is delay, nonresponse, or a disputed decision.
  • How well your timeline is documented.

What to include

  • Your name, policy number, claim number, and insurer.
  • A short timeline of what happened and when.
  • What you want the insurer to do.
  • Copies of key letters, emails, estimates, and your claim log.

What a complaint can and can't do

  • Can: get a written response, prompt action on a stalled claim, and flag rule violations.
  • Usually can't: order the insurer to pay a disputed amount or act as your lawyer.
  • Filing a complaint doesn't stop lawsuit deadlines. See deadlines to sue.

Some states also offer mediation programs.

What your file needs

  • Claim and policy numbers.
  • Timeline.
  • Claim log.
  • Key correspondence.

Quick answers

Does it cost anything to file an insurance complaint?

No. Filing with your state insurance department is free.

Can the insurance department make my insurer pay?

Usually not on a disputed amount, but it can require a response, push a stalled claim, and act on rule violations.
