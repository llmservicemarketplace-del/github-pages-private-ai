How do I challenge an engineer's report the insurer used to deny my claim?

In roof, foundation, and water claims, insurers often hire an engineer to decide what caused the damage, for example hail versus wear and tear, or wind versus long-term deterioration. That report is often the reason for a denial.

An engineer's report is an opinion. You can ask for it, check it, and answer it with your own evidence.

The short answer. Ask the insurer in writing for a complete copy of the report, photos, and notes. Check what the engineer inspected and how long they were there. Answer with your own evidence: dated photos, weather data, a contractor's or independent engineer's opinion. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • Whether the engineer inspected the areas that matter.
  • Whether the report addresses the date and event of the loss.
  • Whether you have independent evidence of cause.

What to check in the report

  • What was inspected: did they go on the roof or into the attic, or only look from the ground?
  • Time on site and the date of inspection compared to the date of loss.
  • Weather data: did they check hail or wind reports for your address and date?
  • Photos: do their photos match their conclusions?
  • Credentials: license, specialty, and who hired them.

How to respond

  1. Write a point-by-point response to each conclusion you dispute.
  2. Attach your evidence: photos from right after the loss, weather reports, contractor statements.
  3. Consider an independent engineer if the amount justifies it.
  4. Ask the insurer to reconsider in writing, and note your policy's appraisal or other dispute options.

What your file needs

  • The full engineer report with photos and field notes.
  • Weather data for your address and date of loss.
  • Photos from immediately after the event.
  • A contractor's or independent engineer's written opinion.

Quick answers

Can I get a copy of the engineer's report?

Ask the insurer in writing. Many states and policies make it available, and insurers often provide it on request. Keep a record of the request.

Is the insurer's engineer report final?

No. It is an opinion. You can respond with your own evidence and use your policy's dispute options.
