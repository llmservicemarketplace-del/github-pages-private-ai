How do I claim my damaged belongings?

Your contents (personal property) coverage pays for furniture, clothes, electronics, and other belongings. Most policies require you to give the insurer an inventory of damaged property showing quantity, description, value, and amount of loss.

Contents claims are often underpaid simply because the list is incomplete.

The short answer. Make a room-by-room inventory with photos, the age and price of each item, and receipts where you have them. Keep damaged items until the insurer has seen or released them. If your policy pays replacement cost, keep receipts for what you replace. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • Whether your contents coverage is replacement cost or actual cash value.
  • Your contents limit and special limits on jewelry, cash, firearms, and similar items.
  • How complete and supported your inventory is.

Build the inventory

  1. Go room by room, including closets, drawers, garage, and attic.
  2. List each item: description, brand/model, quantity, age, and replacement price.
  3. Attach photos, receipts, bank or card statements, and old photos showing the items.
  4. Use online listings for current replacement prices.

Depreciation on belongings

Many policies first pay actual cash value (replacement cost minus depreciation), then pay the depreciation back after you replace the item. Keep replacement receipts and send them in before your policy's deadline. See ACV vs. replacement cost.

What your file needs

  • Room-by-room inventory.
  • Photos and receipts.
  • Replacement price sources.
  • Policy contents limit and special limits.

Quick answers

Do I need receipts for a contents claim?

Receipts help but aren't always required. Photos, bank or card statements, and current replacement prices can support your inventory.

Why did my insurer pay less than the cost to replace my belongings?

Many policies first pay actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation, then pay the rest after you replace the items and send receipts.
