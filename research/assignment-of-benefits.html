Should I sign an assignment of benefits with a contractor?

An assignment of benefits (AOB) transfers some of your insurance claim rights to a contractor, such as a water mitigation or roofing company, so they can deal with your insurer and be paid directly.

In Florida, assignments of post-loss benefits are void for residential and commercial property policies issued on or after January 1, 2023. Other states allow AOBs, sometimes with limits.

The short answer. Read anything a contractor asks you to sign after a loss. An AOB can give up control of your claim. Ask whether it's an assignment or a direction to pay, what the contractor will charge, and whether you can cancel. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • Your state's AOB law and your policy's assignment terms.
  • What exactly the document assigns.
  • Whether you can cancel it, and how.

Risks of an AOB

  • You may lose control over negotiation of that part of the claim.
  • Disputes between the contractor and insurer can delay your repairs.
  • Some contracts let the contractor sue the insurer in your claim's name.

Alternatives

  • A standard repair contract where you stay in charge of the claim.
  • A direction to pay, which lets the insurer pay the contractor without assigning your rights. Read it carefully; terms vary.

Before you sign anything

  • Never sign during the emergency without reading it. Ask for a copy to review.
  • Check the contractor's license and reviews.
  • Look for cancellation rights, and use them in writing if you change your mind.

What your file needs

  • A copy of every document you signed.
  • The contractor's license number.
  • Your policy's assignment clause.

Quick answers

Are assignments of benefits allowed in Florida?

Not for residential or commercial property policies issued on or after January 1, 2023. Assignments of post-loss benefits under those policies are void.

What is the difference between an AOB and a direction to pay?

An AOB transfers claim rights to the contractor. A direction to pay generally lets the insurer pay the contractor directly without transferring your rights. Read the actual document.
