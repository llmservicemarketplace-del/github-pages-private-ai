My claim was denied for wear and tear. What can I do?

Homeowners policies don't cover damage caused by wear and tear, deterioration, or lack of maintenance. Insurers often cite these exclusions on older roofs and plumbing.

But an old roof can still be damaged by a storm. The question is what actually caused the damage you're claiming.

The short answer. Ask for the exact exclusion and the evidence behind the denial. Then show the storm or sudden event caused the damage: dated photos, weather data for your address, and a contractor's or engineer's opinion that separates storm damage from age. Rules differ by state and by policy wording.

What this turns on

  • What caused the specific damage claimed.
  • Evidence tying damage to the date of loss.
  • Whether covered and excluded causes combined, and how your policy and state treat that.

Signs the damage may be storm-related

  • Damage appeared right after a storm with reported hail or high wind.
  • Damage is on one side of the house, matching wind direction.
  • Fresh breaks, missing shingles, or hail bruising, rather than general granule loss.
  • Neighbors filed claims for the same event.

How to respond

  1. Request the full claim file, including photos and any engineer report.
  2. Gather weather data for your address and date.
  3. Get a written opinion from a licensed roofer, plumber, or engineer.
  4. Send a written response asking the insurer to reconsider, and keep a copy.

See also how to read a denial letter.

What your file needs

  • The denial letter and exclusion cited.
  • Weather data for your date of loss.
  • Photos before and after the event.
  • Maintenance records or receipts.

Quick answers

Does homeowners insurance cover an old roof?

It generally covers sudden damage from a covered event, like wind or hail, even on an older roof, but not damage caused by age, deterioration, or lack of maintenance. Policies and roof endorsements vary.

How do I prove storm damage instead of wear and tear?

Dated photos, weather data for your address and date, and a written opinion from a roofer or engineer tying the damage to the event.
