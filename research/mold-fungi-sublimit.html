Does homeowners insurance cover mold, and what is the mold limit?

Mold is usually covered only when it results from a covered loss, such as a sudden pipe burst or storm water coming through a damaged roof, and even then often only up to a separate, smaller limit. That separate cap is the mold "sublimit."

The short answer. Find the fungi, mold or bacteria endorsement in your policy and its dollar limit on the declarations page. Separate the cost of mold remediation from the cost of repairing the water damage itself; the water repair may not count against the mold limit.

How the limit usually works

  • Many insurers use a "limited fungi, wet or dry rot, or bacteria" endorsement. In the standard industry version introduced in the early 2000s, the basic property limit was $10,000 per policy year, covering removal of the mold, tearing out and replacing property to reach it, and testing. Higher limits are sometimes available for a premium.
  • Your insurer may use its own form and a different amount. The declarations page shows your limit.
  • The limit is often an annual aggregate: one cap for all mold losses in the policy year.
  • Mold from long-term leaks, humidity or poor maintenance is commonly excluded. See sudden vs. gradual leaks.

Keep mold costs separate from water damage costs

The disputed question is often which costs count against the mold limit. Replacing drywall that was soaked by a burst pipe is water damage repair. Remediating the mold growing on it is a mold cost. Ask the insurer to show which line items it is charging against the mold sublimit, and why.

Documenting a mold claim

  1. Report the water loss promptly; delay is the most common reason mold claims are denied.
  2. Photograph the source, the wet areas and the growth.
  3. Dry the area and limit spread. Most policies require you to prevent further damage. See duty to mitigate.
  4. Get a written remediation protocol and estimate from a qualified remediation company.
  5. Keep clearance testing results after remediation.
  6. Track mold costs and water repair costs in separate columns.

For safety and cleanup basics, see mold after a flood.

Quick answers

Is mold covered by homeowners insurance?

Usually only when it results from a covered loss, and often only up to a separate mold sublimit shown on your declarations. Mold from long-term leaks or maintenance problems is commonly excluded.

What is a typical mold sublimit?

The standard industry endorsement introduced a basic $10,000 annual limit; insurers vary and higher limits may be bought. Check your declarations page.

Does repairing water damage count against the mold limit?

It shouldn't automatically. Repairing water-damaged materials is often a separate cost from mold remediation. Ask the insurer which items it is charging against the mold limit.
