Does an insurer have to replace undamaged material so the repair matches?

When hail dents half a roof slope or a flood ruins part of a continuous floor, the question is whether the carrier pays only for the damaged portion or for enough of the undamaged material that the finished repair matches.

Some states require a reasonably uniform appearance by statute or regulation; others leave it entirely to the policy language. A few limit the obligation to the affected line of sight rather than the whole structure.

It is one of the largest dollar swings in a residential claim: replacing one slope versus an entire roof, or one room of flooring versus the continuous run.

Rules differ by state and by policy wording. Use the searches below to read what courts in your own state have said, then check that a case is still good law before relying on it.
