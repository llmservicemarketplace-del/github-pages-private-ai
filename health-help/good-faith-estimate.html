Good Faith Estimates for Uninsured and Self-Pay Patients

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or medical advice. Rules can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

If you don’t have health insurance, or you choose not to use it, federal law gives you the right to a Good Faith Estimate: a written estimate of what your care is expected to cost, before you get it.

Your rights

  • You can ask any provider or facility for a Good Faith Estimate, even before you schedule.
  • When you schedule care, the provider must give you one without being asked.
  • It should list the expected charges for the items and services in your care.

Tip: Tell the scheduler up front that you are uninsured or self-pay, and ask for the estimate in writing. Keep it.

If your bill is much higher than the estimate

If a provider or facility bills you at least $400 more than its Good Faith Estimate, you can use the federal patient-provider dispute resolution process. An independent reviewer decides what you owe.

DeadlineStart within 120 calendar days of the date on your first bill.
FeeA $25 non-refundable fee. If the reviewer decides in your favor, that amount comes off your bill.
During the disputeThe provider can’t send your bill to collections, must pause collections already started, and can’t charge late fees on the disputed amount.

Start on the CMS website or call the No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059.

Have insurance? You have different protections. See surprise medical bills.

Frequently asked questions

Who can get a Good Faith Estimate?

People who are uninsured or who choose not to use their insurance (self-pay). You can ask any provider for one, and providers must give one when you schedule care.

What if my bill is higher than my Good Faith Estimate?

If the bill is at least $400 more than the estimate, you can start the federal patient-provider dispute resolution process within 120 days of the first bill. The fee is $25.

Can a provider send my bill to collections during a dispute?

No. While the dispute is underway, the provider can't send the bill to collections and must pause collections already started.
