Prior Authorization Explained

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or medical advice. Rules can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Prior authorization (also called pre-approval or pre-certification) means your health plan must agree to cover certain care before you get it. Common examples include MRIs, some surgeries, specialty drugs, and medical equipment. If you skip it, the plan may refuse to pay.

How it usually works

  1. Your doctor orders the care. Ask: “Does this need prior authorization?”
  2. The doctor’s office sends the request with medical records to your plan.
  3. The plan approves or denies it. Get the approval number, what it covers, and how long it lasts.
  4. Schedule the care within the approval dates.

How long plans have to decide

Starting in 2026, federal rules require Medicare Advantage plans, Medicaid and CHIP, and plans sold on HealthCare.gov to decide most prior authorization requests within 7 calendar days, and urgent requests within 72 hours. They must also give a specific reason when they deny. Other plans follow state rules or their own contracts. Ask your plan for its timeline.

If prior authorization is denied

  • Read the reason. Sometimes the office left out a record or used the wrong code. Your doctor can resubmit.
  • Ask for a peer-to-peer review. Your doctor can talk directly with the plan’s medical reviewer.
  • Appeal. You have the right to appeal. See how to appeal a health insurance denial.
  • If it’s urgent, ask for an expedited decision.

Official sources

  • HealthCare.gov: Internal appeals

Frequently asked questions

What is prior authorization?

Approval from your health plan before you get certain care, such as an MRI, surgery, or specialty drug. Without it, the plan may not pay.

How long does prior authorization take?

It depends on the plan. Starting in 2026, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, CHIP, and HealthCare.gov plans must decide most requests within 7 calendar days and urgent requests within 72 hours.

What can I do if prior authorization is denied?

Your doctor can resubmit with more information or ask for a peer-to-peer review, and you can file an appeal.
