Medical Debt and Your Credit Report

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or medical advice. Rules can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Medical debt can hurt your credit, but there are now limits on what the three big credit bureaus, Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, report.

What the credit bureaus say they don’t report

  • Paid medical collections are removed from credit reports.
  • Medical collections under $500 are not reported.
  • New medical collections don’t appear until they are at least one year old, giving you time to sort out insurance or financial assistance.
Important: these are voluntary policies of the credit bureaus, not a federal law. A federal rule that would have removed most medical debt from credit reports was struck down by a court in July 2025. Some states have their own laws that limit medical debt reporting. Check your state’s rules.

Check and dispute your reports

  1. Get your free reports from all three bureaus at AnnualCreditReport.com, the official free site.
  2. Look for medical debt that is paid, under $500, less than a year old, not yours, or the wrong amount.
  3. Dispute errors with each bureau that lists them, online or in writing. Include copies of your proof.
  4. Dispute with the collector too. If a debt collector contacts you, you can ask them in writing to verify the debt.

Before you pay a collector

  • Make sure the bill is correct and that insurance was billed. See disputing billing errors.
  • Ask the original hospital about financial assistance, even if the debt was sent to collections.
  • Get any payment agreement in writing before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

Does paid medical debt show up on my credit report?

The three major credit bureaus say they remove paid medical collection debt from credit reports. This is a voluntary bureau policy, not a federal law.

Is medical debt under $500 reported to credit bureaus?

Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion say they don't report medical collection debt under $500.

Was medical debt banned from credit reports?

A federal rule that would have removed most medical debt from credit reports was vacated by a court in July 2025, so it did not take effect. Some states have their own limits.
