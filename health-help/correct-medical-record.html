How to Correct an Error in Your Medical Record

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or medical advice. Rules can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Mistakes in medical records happen: a wrong allergy, a medicine you never took, a diagnosis you don’t have, or the wrong date. These errors can affect your care, your insurance, and claims. Under HIPAA, you have the right to ask a provider or health plan to amend (correct) your record.

How to request a correction

  1. Get a copy of your record first. See how to request your medical records.
  2. Identify each error and what the correct information is.
  3. Put your request in writing. Providers may require a written request and give a reason. Many have a form.
  4. Include proof if you have it, such as a pharmacy record or another provider’s note.
  5. Keep a copy and note the date you sent it.

How long they have

A provider generally must act on your request within 60 days. It can extend that once, by up to 30 days, if it tells you in writing why.

If they say no

A provider can deny a correction if it believes the record is already accurate and complete, or if it didn’t create that information. If it denies your request, it must tell you why in writing. You then have the right to:

  • Submit a statement of disagreement, which the provider must keep with your record and include when it shares that information.
  • Ask that your request and the denial be included with your record.
  • File a complaint with the provider or with the HHS Office for Civil Rights.

Note: a provider usually won’t delete a doctor’s medical opinion, but it can add a correction or your statement.

Official sources

  • HHS.gov: HIPAA for individuals
  • HHS Office for Civil Rights: File a complaint

Frequently asked questions

Can I make my doctor change my medical record?

You have the right to request a correction under HIPAA. The provider can agree, or deny if it believes the record is accurate. If it denies, you can add a statement of disagreement.

How long does a provider have to respond to a correction request?

Generally 60 days, with one possible extension of up to 30 days if the provider tells you in writing why.

What is a statement of disagreement?

Your written explanation of why you think the record is wrong. The provider must keep it with your record and include it when it shares that information.
