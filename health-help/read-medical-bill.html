How to Read a Medical Bill

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal or medical advice. Rules can change, so confirm with the official sources below.

Medical bills can be hard to read, with codes and abbreviations everywhere. But almost every bill has the same parts. Once you know them, you can spot mistakes and know what you really owe. Don’t pay a bill you don’t understand. You can ask for time to review it.

The parts of a medical bill

SectionWhat it means
Patient and account numberUse this number every time you call or write.
Date of serviceThe day you got the care. Check it against your calendar.
Description and codeWhat was done. Doctors’ services use 5-character CPT or HCPCS codes. Hospitals may also list revenue codes.
ChargesThe provider’s full list price, before any discount.
Adjustments or discountsAmounts removed because of your insurance contract, a cash discount, or financial assistance.
Insurance paymentsWhat your plan already paid.
Patient responsibility / balance dueWhat the provider says you owe. Compare this with your insurer’s Explanation of Benefits.

Five things to check

  1. Is it really your care? Check the name, dates, and provider.
  2. Was insurance billed? If the bill shows no insurance payment, call the provider and give them your plan information.
  3. Does it match your Explanation of Benefits? The amount you owe on the bill should match the “what you owe” amount on your insurer’s EOB. See how to read an EOB.
  4. Is it a summary bill? If it only shows totals, ask for an itemized bill.
  5. Are there duplicate or unfamiliar charges? Circle anything you don’t recognize and ask about it.

When you call the billing office

  • Have the bill, your insurance card, and your EOB in front of you.
  • Write down the date, the name of the person you talk to, and what they said.
  • Ask them to put the account on hold while you review it, so it isn’t sent to collections.
  • Ask whether you qualify for financial assistance or a payment plan.

Frequently asked questions

What is a CPT code on a medical bill?

A 5-character code that describes a medical service, such as an office visit or a test. It tells your insurer exactly what was billed.

Should I pay a medical bill right away?

Not before you check it. Compare it with your insurer's Explanation of Benefits and ask for an itemized bill if anything is unclear. Ask the provider to hold the account while you review it.

What does patient responsibility mean?

The amount the provider says you owe after insurance payments and adjustments. It should match the amount your insurer says you owe.
