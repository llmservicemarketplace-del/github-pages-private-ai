How to Prepare for Mediation in a Civil Case

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and change over time, so confirm with the official sources below.

Many civil cases are sent to mediation before trial. A neutral mediator helps both sides try to reach a settlement. The mediator doesn't decide who wins. Nothing is final unless both sides agree and sign. Good preparation can make the difference between a fair settlement and a wasted day.

What usually happens

  1. Opening session: the mediator explains the process. Each side may give a short summary of its view. Some mediators skip the joint session.
  2. Caucuses: each side goes to a separate room, and the mediator goes back and forth carrying offers and asking questions.
  3. Offers and counteroffers until there's a deal or an impasse.
  4. Settlement agreement: if you agree, the terms are usually written and signed before you leave.

Confidentiality

What's said in mediation is generally confidential and usually can't be used as evidence later. Many states, including Georgia and Florida, have rules on this. Ask the mediator what's confidential, including whether what you tell them in caucus can be shared with the other side.

How to prepare

  • Know your best and worst outcomes at trial, and your costs and time to get there.
  • Decide your goal, your opening number, and your walk-away point in advance.
  • Bring copies of key documents: the contract or policy, photos, estimates, letters, and a damages summary.
  • Prepare a 2 to 3 minute summary of your side, calm and factual.
  • Bring anyone who must approve a deal. The other side should send someone with authority to settle.
  • Plan for a long day. Bring food, water, medication, and phone chargers.

Negotiating tips

  • Listen to the other side's concerns. The mediator often shares risks you hadn't considered.
  • Make moves in reasonable steps and explain why.
  • Think beyond money: payment timing, repairs, apologies, or non-monetary terms can help close a deal.
  • It's okay to take a break or say no.

Frequently asked questions

Is mediation binding?

The process isn't binding, but a settlement agreement both sides sign usually is.

Is what I say in mediation confidential?

Generally yes, under court rules and state law, with some exceptions. Ask the mediator to explain the rules at the start.

Do I have to settle at mediation?

No. You can reach an impasse and the case continues. But you usually must attend and participate in good faith if the court ordered mediation.
