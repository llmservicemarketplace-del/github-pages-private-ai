Fence and Property Line Disputes After a Storm

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and change over time, so confirm with the official sources below.

When a storm knocks down a fence on or near a property line, neighbors often disagree about who owns it, who pays, and where the new one goes. Start with facts: where the line is and what your insurance covers.

1. Check insurance first

  • Many homeowners policies cover fences under "other structures" for causes like wind or a falling tree, subject to your deductible.
  • If a neighbor's tree fell on your fence, see tree damage: who pays.

2. Confirm the property line

  • Look at your deed, plat, and any survey from your closing.
  • If the line is unclear or disputed, hire a licensed land surveyor. A survey is the best evidence.
  • Your county GIS map is a starting point, but it isn't exact enough to settle a dispute.

3. Who has to pay?

It depends on your state and local rules. Some states, such as California, have laws presuming neighbors share the cost of a boundary fence. Many states have no such rule, so a neighbor may not have to pay unless you agree. Check your state law, city or county code, and any HOA rules.

4. Talk, then put it in writing

  • Share the survey and your insurance information.
  • Agree on location, height, material, cost split, and who maintains it.
  • Sign a short written agreement. A recorded agreement can bind future owners.

Encroachments

If a fence or structure crosses the line, address it in writing. Over time, some states let a long, open use of land create rights for the user, so don't ignore an encroachment for years. A real estate lawyer can explain your state's rules.

Permits and codes

Many cities require a permit for a new fence and limit height and placement. See permits after a disaster.

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover a fence destroyed by a storm?

Often yes, under other structures coverage for causes like wind, subject to your deductible.

Does my neighbor have to pay half of a new fence?

Only in some states or if you agree. Many states have no law requiring neighbors to share fence costs.

How do I prove where my property line is?

Hire a licensed land surveyor. A survey is the strongest evidence of the boundary.
