How to Break a Lease Early

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General legal information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Laws vary by state; this is not legal advice.

Leaving before your lease ends can mean owing rent for the rest of the term. But you may have options, depending on your lease and your state's law.

Check these first

  • Your lease: look for an early termination clause or fee.
  • Military service: the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act lets active-duty servicemembers end a lease after receiving qualifying orders.
  • Domestic violence: many states let survivors end a lease early with notice and documentation.
  • Unsafe or unlivable housing: serious habitability problems may allow you to end the lease. See when a landlord won't make repairs.
  • Disaster damage: if the home is destroyed or unlivable, many leases and state laws allow termination.

Reduce what you owe

  • Many states require landlords to make reasonable efforts to re-rent the unit, which limits what you owe.
  • Offer to find a replacement tenant or sublet, if the lease allows.
  • Negotiate a buyout in writing.
  • Give written notice, take move-out photos, and request your deposit back.

Free help

Contact legal aid as early as you can, especially if there is a court deadline. Find your local program through our legal aid guide, LawHelp.org, or by calling 211.

Questions this guide answers

Can I break my lease without paying the rest of the rent?

Sometimes. It depends on your lease and state law. Many states require landlords to try to re-rent, and some protect servicemembers, domestic violence survivors, and tenants in unlivable housing.

Can military members break a lease?

Yes. The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act lets active-duty servicemembers end a lease after qualifying orders, with proper notice.
