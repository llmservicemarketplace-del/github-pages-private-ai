How to File a Consumer Complaint

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General legal information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Laws vary by state; this is not legal advice.

When a business won't fix a problem, a complaint to the right agency can get a response, and it creates a record that helps others.

Where to complain

ProblemWhere
Most businesses and scamsYour state attorney general's consumer protection office; FTC
Banks, credit cards, loans, mortgages, debt collectors, credit reportsConsumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)
Insurance companiesYour state insurance department
Contractors and licensed tradesState licensing board. See contractor disputes

Make it effective

  • Complain to the business first, in writing.
  • Give a short timeline with dates, amounts, and names.
  • Say exactly what you want.
  • Attach copies of receipts, contracts, and messages.

The FTC usually doesn't resolve individual complaints, but uses them in investigations. The CFPB forwards complaints to companies and tracks their responses. State attorneys general often mediate.

Free help

Contact legal aid as early as you can, especially if there is a court deadline. Find your local program through our legal aid guide, LawHelp.org, or by calling 211.

Questions this guide answers

Where do I file a complaint against a company?

Start with the company, then your state attorney general. Use the CFPB for financial products, your state insurance department for insurers, and the FTC for fraud.

Will the FTC resolve my complaint?

Usually not individually. The FTC uses complaints to investigate and bring cases. The CFPB and state attorneys general are more likely to get you a direct response.
