Deadlines to File a Civil Lawsuit (Statutes of Limitations)

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General legal information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Laws vary by state; this is not legal advice.

A statute of limitations is the deadline to file a lawsuit. If you miss it, the court will usually dismiss your case, no matter how strong it is. Limits are set by state law and depend on the type of claim.

Common categories

  • Written contracts, oral contracts, property damage, personal injury, and debt each have their own limits, often from one to several years.
  • Insurance policies often have their own shorter deadline in the policy. See deadlines to sue your insurer.
  • Claims against a government agency often require a written notice within a short time, sometimes months, before you can sue.

When the clock starts

Usually when the harm happened or when you discovered it, but rules vary. Some events can pause the clock.

What to do

  1. Write down the date the problem happened and the date you discovered it.
  2. Look up your state's limit for your type of claim, or ask legal aid.
  3. Don't wait for negotiations to finish if the deadline is close.

Free help

Contact legal aid as early as you can, especially if there is a court deadline. Find your local program through our legal aid guide, LawHelp.org, or by calling 211.

Questions this guide answers

What is a statute of limitations?

The legal deadline to file a lawsuit. Missing it usually means the court will dismiss the case.

Do negotiations stop the deadline to sue?

Usually not. Unless there's a written agreement or law that pauses it, the deadline keeps running.
