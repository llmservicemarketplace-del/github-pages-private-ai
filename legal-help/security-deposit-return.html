How to Get Your Security Deposit Back

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General legal information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Laws vary by state; this is not legal advice.

Most states require a landlord to return your security deposit, or send an itemized list of deductions, within a set time after you move out, commonly 14 to 30 days. Landlords generally can't deduct for normal wear and tear.

When you move out

  • Clean, and take dated photos and video of every room.
  • Return all keys and get a receipt.
  • Give your forwarding address in writing. Some states start the deadline from that date.
  • Ask for a walk-through if your state allows one.

If it isn't returned

  1. Look up your state's deadline and rules. Some states add penalties, such as double the deposit, for bad-faith withholding.
  2. Send a demand letter citing the law and your photos.
  3. File in small claims court if they still don't pay.

Wear and tear vs. damage

Faded paint, small nail holes, and worn carpet are usually wear and tear. Holes in walls, broken fixtures, and pet damage usually aren't. Your move-in photos are your best evidence.

Free help

Contact legal aid as early as you can, especially if there is a court deadline. Find your local program through our legal aid guide, LawHelp.org, or by calling 211.

Questions this guide answers

How long does a landlord have to return a security deposit?

It depends on your state, commonly 14 to 30 days after you move out. Check your state's tenant law.

Can a landlord keep my deposit for normal wear and tear?

Generally no. Deductions are usually limited to unpaid rent and damage beyond normal wear and tear.
