What to Do When Your Landlord Won't Make Repairs

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General legal information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Laws vary by state; this is not legal advice.

In most states, landlords must keep rental homes safe and livable: working heat, water, electricity, plumbing, and a weather-tight roof, free of serious hazards. Remedies vary by state, and using the wrong one can put you at risk of eviction.

Steps

  1. Request the repair in writing and keep a copy. Describe the problem and date it.
  2. Document it: photos, video, and any health effects such as mold or no heat.
  3. Give a reasonable time to fix it. Emergencies like no heat in winter call for a fast response.
  4. Call local code enforcement or the housing inspector if it isn't fixed.
  5. Talk to legal aid before withholding rent or making repairs and deducting the cost. Some states allow this only under strict rules.
Retaliation: many states protect tenants from eviction or rent increases in retaliation for asking for repairs or reporting code violations. Keep records of the dates.

See also what to do if you get an eviction notice.

Free help

Contact legal aid as early as you can, especially if there is a court deadline. Find your local program through our legal aid guide, LawHelp.org, or by calling 211.

Questions this guide answers

Can I stop paying rent if my landlord won't fix things?

Only in some states, and only by following strict steps. Withholding rent incorrectly can lead to eviction. Talk to legal aid first.

Who do I call if my landlord won't fix a dangerous problem?

Your local code enforcement or housing inspection office, and legal aid for advice on your rights.
