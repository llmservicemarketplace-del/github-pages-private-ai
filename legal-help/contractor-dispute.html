What to Do When a Contractor Takes Your Money or Does Bad Work

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General legal information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Laws vary by state; this is not legal advice.

After storms, contractor problems are common: deposits taken with no work done, jobs abandoned halfway, or work that fails inspection. You have options, and acting quickly helps.

Steps

  1. Gather records: contract, estimates, receipts, payment records, texts, and dated photos of the work.
  2. Send a demand letter giving a deadline to finish, fix, or refund.
  3. Check the license with your state's contractor licensing board, and file a complaint. Some states have a recovery fund that repays homeowners harmed by licensed contractors.
  4. Report fraud to your state attorney general and the FTC, and to local police if the contractor took money and disappeared.
  5. Sue in small claims or regular court.
Watch for liens. An unpaid subcontractor or supplier may be able to put a lien on your home even if you paid the contractor. See mechanic's liens.

Insurance claim involved? Tell your insurer and your mortgage company. See assignment of benefits.

Free help

Contact legal aid as early as you can, especially if there is a court deadline. Find your local program through our legal aid guide, LawHelp.org, or by calling 211.

Questions this guide answers

Can I get my deposit back from a contractor who never started?

Send a written demand, file a complaint with the licensing board, check whether your state has a recovery fund, and consider small claims court or a police report for fraud.

Where do I report a bad contractor?

Your state contractor licensing board, your state attorney general, and the FTC.
