Settling a Small Estate Without Full Probate

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General legal information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Laws vary by state; this is not legal advice.

When someone dies, their property often goes through probate. Most states offer a faster, cheaper process for small estates, often a sworn small estate affidavit or a simplified court procedure. Each state sets its own dollar limit and waiting period.

What usually doesn't count toward the limit

  • Property that passes outside probate: jointly owned property with survivorship, accounts with a named beneficiary or payable-on-death designation, life insurance with a named beneficiary.

Typical steps

  1. Get a certified death certificate.
  2. List the deceased person's assets and debts.
  3. Check your state's small estate limit and waiting period.
  4. Complete the affidavit or simplified probate form, sign it under oath, and present it to the bank or other holder of the property.
  5. Pay valid debts as the law requires and distribute property to the rightful heirs.
Real estate often has different rules and may require court involvement. Heirs' property, where a home passes without a clear title, is common after disasters and can block FEMA and repair aid. Legal aid can help clear title.

See also property and probate.

Questions this guide answers

What is a small estate affidavit?

A sworn form many states allow heirs to use to collect a deceased person's property without full probate, if the estate is below a set value.

Does a small estate affidavit work for a house?

Sometimes, but real estate often has different rules and may need a court process. Check your state's law.
