How to Prepare for a Court Hearing

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General legal information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Laws vary by state; this is not legal advice.

Being organized is the biggest advantage a self-represented person can have. Judges hear many cases a day. A short, clear presentation with organized evidence helps.

What to bring

  • All court papers you filed and received.
  • Three copies of every document you'll show: one for the judge, one for the other side, one for you.
  • A one-page timeline of key dates.
  • Photos printed, and texts or emails printed with dates showing.
  • Witnesses, if you need them. Some courts require advance notice or a subpoena.

In the courtroom

  • Arrive early, check the posted docket, and turn off your phone.
  • Address the judge as "Your Honor." Don't interrupt.
  • Stick to the facts that matter: what happened, what you want, and your proof.
  • Ask for an interpreter or accommodation ahead of time if you need one.

Practice

Write a 2-minute summary and practice it out loud. Many courts allow you to watch other hearings beforehand.

Free help

Contact legal aid as early as you can, especially if there is a court deadline. Find your local program through our legal aid guide, LawHelp.org, or by calling 211.

Questions this guide answers

What should I bring to court?

Your court papers, three copies of every document you'll use, a timeline, printed photos and messages, and any witnesses.

Can I get an interpreter in court?

Many courts provide interpreters. Ask the clerk ahead of your hearing.
