Default Judgments: How to Avoid One, and What to Do After

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General legal information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Laws vary by state; this is not legal advice.

If you're sued and don't respond by the deadline, the court can enter a default judgment: the other side wins without a hearing on the facts. A default judgment can lead to wage garnishment, bank levies, or eviction.

How to avoid one

  • Read the summons the day you get it and calendar the deadline.
  • File a written answer or appearance by the deadline. The court's self-help center can help.
  • Keep your address current with the court.
  • Go to every hearing.

If a default judgment was entered

  1. Act fast. Courts have short deadlines for asking to set aside a default.
  2. Get a copy of the court file to see what was filed and when.
  3. File a motion to set aside (or vacate) the judgment. Common reasons: you were never properly served, a mistake or excusable neglect, or fraud.
  4. Show you have a real defense if the court requires it.
  5. Talk to legal aid. This is a common problem they help with.

See serving court papers and how to file a motion.

Free help

Contact legal aid as early as you can, especially if there is a court deadline. Find your local program through our legal aid guide, LawHelp.org, or by calling 211.

Questions this guide answers

What is a default judgment?

A judgment entered against you because you didn't respond to a lawsuit or appear by the deadline.

Can a default judgment be reversed?

Sometimes. You can ask the court to set it aside, often for improper service, mistake, or excusable neglect, but deadlines are short.
