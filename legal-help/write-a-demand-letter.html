How to Write a Demand Letter

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General legal information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Laws vary by state; this is not legal advice.

A demand letter tells the other side what they owe or must do, why, and by when. It often settles disputes without court, and if not, it shows the judge you tried.

What to include

  • A short, factual summary of what happened, with dates.
  • Exactly what you want: an amount, a repair, a refund.
  • A clear deadline, such as 10 or 14 days.
  • What you'll do if they don't respond, such as file in small claims court.
  • Copies (not originals) of key proof.

Template

[Date] [Their name and address] Re: [Short description, e.g., "Unreturned security deposit, 12 Oak St."] On [date], [what happened]. [Why they are responsible: contract, receipt, law.] I am requesting [amount or action] by [deadline date]. If I don't receive [payment/response] by then, I intend to [file in small claims court / file a complaint with ___]. Enclosed: [list of copies]. [Your name, address, phone, email]

Send it with proof

Use certified mail with a return receipt, or email plus mail. Keep a copy.

Free help

Contact legal aid as early as you can, especially if there is a court deadline. Find your local program through our legal aid guide, LawHelp.org, or by calling 211.

Questions this guide answers

Do I need a lawyer to send a demand letter?

No. Anyone can send a demand letter. Keep it factual and professional.

How long should I give in a demand letter?

A specific deadline, often 10 to 14 days, is common. Some laws set specific notice periods for certain claims.
