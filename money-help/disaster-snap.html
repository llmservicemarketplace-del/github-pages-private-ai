Disaster SNAP (D-SNAP): Food Help After a Disaster

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Program rules and amounts change and vary by state; check the official source before you apply.

D-SNAP provides one month of food benefits to households affected by a major disaster, including many that wouldn't normally qualify for SNAP. It runs only when a state requests it and USDA approves it, usually after a presidential disaster declaration, and the application window is short, often about 7 days.

Who may qualify

  • You lived or worked in the disaster area.
  • You had disaster losses: damage to your home or business, lost income, disaster expenses, or lost food.
  • Your income and resources after disaster expenses are under the D-SNAP limit.

How to apply

  1. Watch for your state's D-SNAP announcement on local news, the state SNAP agency website, or 211.
  2. Apply during your county's window, in person or by phone depending on the state.
  3. Bring ID and, if you have them, proof of address and disaster expenses.

Already on SNAP?

You may get a supplement, and you can ask for replacement benefits for food lost in a power outage or flood. Report the loss quickly, generally within 10 days.

Questions this guide answers

What is D-SNAP?

Disaster SNAP gives one month of food benefits to eligible households affected by a major disaster, including many who don't normally get SNAP.

Can I replace SNAP food lost in a power outage?

Usually yes. Report the loss to your state SNAP office quickly, generally within 10 days.
