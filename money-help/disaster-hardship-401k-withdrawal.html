Using Retirement Savings After a Disaster: Qualified Disaster Distributions

Last reviewed November 10, 2026. Free public guidance from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. This is general information, not tax or financial advice. Rules differ by state and change over time, so confirm with the official sources below.

Federal law lets people affected by a federally declared major disaster take a special withdrawal from retirement accounts with tax breaks. It can help in an emergency, but it reduces your retirement savings, so consider other help first.

The rules at a glance

RuleQualified disaster recovery distribution
Maximum$22,000 per disaster, across all your plans and IRAs
10% early withdrawal penaltyWaived, even if you're under 59½
Income taxStill owed, but spread evenly over 3 years by default (or all in one year if you choose)
RepaymentYou can repay within 3 years and treat it as a rollover, which can undo the tax
TimingGenerally must be taken within 180 days after the later of the disaster's start or its declaration

Who qualifies

  • Your main home was in the declared major disaster area during the incident period.
  • You had an economic loss from the disaster, such as damage to property, displacement from your home, or lost income from a layoff.

Important details

  • IRAs: you can generally take a qualified disaster distribution.
  • Employer plans (401(k), 403(b)): your employer's plan must offer it. Ask your plan administrator. Some plans also offer larger disaster loans.
  • Report it to the IRS on Form 8915-F with your tax return.
  • Keep records showing your address and your loss.

Before you withdraw

  • Apply for FEMA, insurance, and SBA disaster loans first. SBA loans for homeowners often have low interest rates.
  • Ask whether your state taxes the distribution.
  • Money taken out stops growing. Consider repaying if you can.
  • Talk to a tax professional. Free tax help (VITA) may be available. See disaster tax relief.

Frequently asked questions

Can I take money out of my 401(k) after a disaster without a penalty?

Yes, if you qualify. Qualified disaster recovery distributions up to $22,000 aren't subject to the 10% early withdrawal penalty, if your plan offers them.

Do I have to pay taxes on a disaster distribution?

Yes, but by default the income is spread over three years, and you can repay within three years to reverse the tax.

How long do I have to take a disaster distribution?

Generally within 180 days after the later of the first day of the disaster or the date it was declared.
