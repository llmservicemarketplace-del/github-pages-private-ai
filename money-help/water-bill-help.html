How to Get Help Paying Your Water Bill

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Program rules and amounts change and vary by state; check the official source before you apply.

The temporary federal water assistance program (LIHWAP) has ended, so help with water and sewer bills now comes mostly from local sources.

Where to look

  • Your water utility: ask about low-income discounts, payment plans, leak adjustments, and customer assistance funds.
  • 211: local charities and community action agencies often have emergency funds.
  • Community action agency in your county.
  • Your city or county may run its own assistance program.

High bill? Check for a leak

  1. Turn off every faucet and appliance, then watch the meter. If it moves, you likely have a leak.
  2. Check toilets: a few drops of food coloring in the tank that show up in the bowl mean a leak.
  3. Fix it, keep the receipt, and ask the utility for a leak adjustment credit.

Shutoff notice? See utility shutoff protections.

Free local help

Call or text 211, or visit 211.org, to find local programs for bills, food, housing, and more. You can also browse our free resource directory.

Official and nonprofit sources

  • 211: Find local help
  • Community Action Partnership: Find your local agency

Need help sorting out bills or applications? Contact NFCcop for free guidance.

Questions this guide answers

Is there a federal program to help pay water bills?

The temporary federal Low Income Household Water Assistance Program has ended. Help now comes mostly from local utilities, cities, and charities. Call 211.

What is a leak adjustment?

A credit some water utilities give when a high bill was caused by a leak you've repaired. Ask your utility and keep the repair receipt.
