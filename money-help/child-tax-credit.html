Child Tax Credit: Who Qualifies and How to Claim It

Last reviewed November 15, 2026. General information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Program rules and amounts change and vary by state; check the official source before you apply.

The Child Tax Credit lowers the federal income tax of families with qualifying children. Part of it can come back as a refund even if you owe little or no tax. You must file a tax return to get it.

Check the current Child Tax Credit rules (IRS)

Which children qualify

  • The child is under 17 at the end of the tax year.
  • The child is your son, daughter, stepchild, foster child, sibling, or a descendant of one of them, such as a grandchild.
  • The child lived with you for more than half the year and did not provide more than half of their own support.
  • You claim the child as a dependent.
  • The child has a Social Security number that is valid for work.

How to claim it

  1. File a federal tax return, even if your income is low enough that you are not required to file.
  2. List each qualifying child and their Social Security number.
  3. Complete the Child Tax Credit schedule that your tax software or preparer provides.
  4. Choose direct deposit for a faster refund.

The refundable part

If the credit is more than the tax you owe, you may get part of the difference as a refund. This is called the Additional Child Tax Credit, and it generally requires some earned income. The credit amount and income limits are set by law and can change, so check the IRS page for the year you are filing.

What to have ready

  • Social Security numbers for you, your spouse, and each child.
  • W-2s, 1099s, and other income records.
  • Records showing the child lived with you, such as school or medical records, in case the IRS asks.
  • Last year's tax return, if you have it.

Questions this guide answers

Do I have to file taxes to get the Child Tax Credit?

Yes. The credit is claimed on a federal tax return, so file even if your income is below the filing requirement.

Can I get the Child Tax Credit if I owe no tax?

Possibly. Part of the credit is refundable for families with earned income. Check the IRS rules for the year you are filing.
