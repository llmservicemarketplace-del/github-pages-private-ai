How to Apply for Medicaid in New Jersey (NJ FamilyCare)

Last reviewed November 14, 2026. Written by The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit educational resource. General information only, not legal advice. We are not a government agency and we charge nothing for these guides.

Short answer: Apply through NJ FamilyCare. New Jersey's Medicaid program is called NJ FamilyCare. You can apply any time of year. There is no open enrollment period for Medicaid.

Medicaid is free or low-cost health coverage paid for by the federal government and the state together. New Jersey sets its own income limits within federal rules and runs its own application. This page covers who qualifies in New Jersey, where to apply, what to have ready, and what to do if you are denied.

Who qualifies in New Jersey

New Jersey has adopted the Medicaid expansion. That means most adults under 65 can qualify on income alone, up to about 138% of the federal poverty level, without having a child or a disability.

These groups can also qualify, often at higher income levels:

  • Children, through Medicaid or the Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP)
  • Pregnant people, including coverage after the birth
  • Parents and caretakers of minor children
  • Adults 65 and older and people with disabilities, including many who get SSI
  • People who need long-term care at home or in a nursing home, under separate rules

A federal law passed in 2025 adds work or community-activity rules for some adults, and states are putting them in place through 2027. The New Jersey agency will tell you whether they apply to you.

What to have ready

  • Social Security numbers and birth dates for the people applying
  • Proof of income: recent pay stubs, a benefit letter, or last year's tax return
  • Proof of citizenship or immigration status for the people applying
  • Information about any health insurance you have or can get through a job
  • For age or disability cases: bank statements and information about property

Step by step: applying for Medicaid in New Jersey

  1. Apply. Use NJ FamilyCare. If you start at HealthCare.gov instead, it sends your information to the state when you look eligible.
  2. Answer requests for proof. The state often checks income electronically and only asks for papers when something does not match. Watch your mail and your online account.
  3. Wait for the notice. Federal rules generally allow up to 45 days, or 90 days for disability-based cases.
  4. Pick a plan if asked. Many states use managed care plans. If you do not choose, one is assigned.
  5. Ask about recent bills. Medicaid can sometimes cover care you got shortly before you applied. Ask the agency what the current look-back period is.
  6. Renew every year. Keep your address current. Many people lose coverage only because a renewal letter went to an old address.

If you are denied or lose coverage

The notice must say why and how to ask for a fair hearing, with a deadline. Ask right away; in some cases coverage can continue while you appeal. See how to appeal a benefits denial and free legal aid in New Jersey.

Common questions

How do I apply for Medicaid in New Jersey?

Apply through the state's official page: NJ FamilyCare. You can also apply through HealthCare.gov, by phone, by mail, or in person. There is no enrollment period; you can apply any time of year.

Who qualifies for Medicaid in New Jersey?

New Jersey has adopted the Medicaid expansion, so most adults under 65 can qualify on income alone, up to about 138% of the federal poverty level. Children, pregnant people, adults 65 and older, and people with disabilities can also qualify under their own rules.

What is Medicaid called in New Jersey?

New Jersey's Medicaid program is called NJ FamilyCare.

How long does a Medicaid decision take?

Federal rules generally give the state up to 45 days, or up to 90 days when the application depends on a disability decision. Answer requests for documents quickly so the clock keeps moving.
