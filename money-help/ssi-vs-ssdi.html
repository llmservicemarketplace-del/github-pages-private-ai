SSI vs. SSDI: What's the Difference?

Last reviewed November 9, 2026. General information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Program rules and amounts change and vary by state; check the official source before you apply.

Social Security pays disability benefits through two programs. Some people qualify for both.

SSDISSI
Based onYour work history and Social Security taxes paidFinancial need (limited income and resources)
WhoWorkers with enough work credits who have a qualifying disabilityPeople 65+, blind, or disabled with low income and resources
AmountBased on your earnings recordFederal amount of $994/month for an individual in 2026 ($1,491 for a couple), plus some state supplements
Health coverageMedicare, generally after a waiting periodMedicaid in most states

Working while disabled (2026)

Earning more than $1,690 a month ($2,830 if blind) is generally considered substantial work, which can affect eligibility. SSDI's trial work period counts months you earn over $1,210.

Apply and appeal

  1. Apply online at SSA.gov, by phone, or at a local office.
  2. Give complete medical records and every doctor's contact information.
  3. Most first applications are denied. You generally have 60 days to appeal each decision. See appealing a benefits denial.

Call or text 211, or visit 211.org, to find local programs for bills, food, housing, and more. You can also browse our free resource directory.

Questions this guide answers

What is the difference between SSI and SSDI?

SSDI is based on your work history and Social Security taxes paid. SSI is based on financial need. Some people qualify for both.

How much is SSI in 2026?

The federal SSI payment is $994 a month for an individual and $1,491 for a couple in 2026. Some states add a supplement.

Is SSD the same as SSDI?

Yes. "SSD" and "Social Security disability" are common shorthand for SSDI. SSI is a different program, even though both are run by the Social Security Administration and use the same medical definition of disability.

Can you get SSI and SSDI at the same time?

Yes. If your SSDI payment is low and you meet SSI's income and resource limits, SSI can top it up. This is called receiving "concurrent" benefits. Social Security checks both when you apply.

Which should I apply for?

  • If you've worked most of the last 10 years and paid Social Security taxes, you may qualify for SSDI. Younger workers need fewer work credits.
  • If you have little or no work history and very limited income and savings, look at SSI.
  • You don't have to choose. One disability application lets Social Security consider you for both.

State differences (including California)

Many states add their own payment to SSI. In California, SSI recipients also get a State Supplementary Payment (SSP), so the combined monthly amount is higher than the federal maximum. SSDI is the same in every state.

Working while on benefits

For SSDI in 2026, earning more than $1,690 a month ($2,830 if blind) is generally considered "substantial gainful activity," though work incentive rules like the trial work period let you test working without losing benefits right away. SSI reduces your payment gradually as you earn more. Report all work to Social Security.

How to apply

Apply online at ssa.gov/disability, call Social Security at 1-800-772-1213, or visit a local office. If you're denied, you generally have 60 days from receiving the notice to appeal. Many people who are denied at first are later approved on appeal, so don't give up after one denial. Free legal help may be available; see free legal help.
