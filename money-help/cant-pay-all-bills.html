Can't Pay All Your Bills? What to Pay First

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Program rules and amounts change and vary by state; check the official source before you apply.

When you can't pay everything, pay the bills that protect your basic needs first. Then talk to everyone else before you fall further behind.

Pay these first

  1. Food and essential medicine.
  2. Housing: rent or mortgage.
  3. Utilities: heat, electricity, water.
  4. Transportation you need to get to work.
  5. Child support and taxes, which carry serious consequences.

Unsecured debts like credit cards and medical bills usually come after these. That doesn't mean ignore them; it means contact those creditors and ask for a plan.

Then

  • List every bill: amount, due date, and what happens if it's late.
  • Call creditors before you miss a payment. Ask about hardship plans.
  • Apply for help: SNAP, utility plans, rent help, hospital financial assistance.
  • Avoid payday and title loans.
  • Know your rights if a debt collector sues you.

Free local help

Call or text 211, or visit 211.org, to find local programs for bills, food, housing, and more. You can also browse our free resource directory.

Questions this guide answers

Which bills should I pay first when money is tight?

Food and essential medicine, housing, utilities, transportation to work, and child support or taxes generally come first. Then contact other creditors for hardship plans.

Should I stop paying credit cards to pay rent?

Protecting housing usually comes first. Contact the credit card company and ask about a hardship plan, and consider nonprofit credit counseling.
