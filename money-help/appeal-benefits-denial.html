How to Appeal a Benefits Denial

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Program rules and amounts change and vary by state; check the official source before you apply.

If you're denied, cut off, or your benefits are reduced, the notice must explain why and how to appeal. Appeals often succeed, especially when the problem was missing paperwork.

Deadlines and options

ProgramHow to appeal
SNAPAsk your state for a fair hearing. You generally have up to 90 days.
MedicaidAsk your state for a fair hearing within the time on your notice (often up to 90 days).
SSI / SSDIRequest reconsideration, then a hearing with a judge. You generally have 60 days from receiving each decision.
Keep your benefits during the appeal: if your benefits are being cut or stopped, appealing quickly, often within about 10 days of the notice, may let them continue until the decision. Read your notice for the exact deadline.

How to prepare

  • Read the notice for the exact reason.
  • Send any missing documents right away and keep copies.
  • Ask for your case file.
  • Bring witnesses or letters, such as from a doctor or employer.
  • Legal aid often helps with benefits appeals for free.

Free local help

Call or text 211, or visit 211.org, to find local programs for bills, food, housing, and more. You can also browse our free resource directory.

Questions this guide answers

How long do I have to appeal a Social Security disability denial?

Generally 60 days from when you receive the decision, for each level of appeal.

Can I keep my benefits while I appeal?

Often yes, if you appeal quickly after a notice that cuts or stops benefits. Check the deadline on your notice.
