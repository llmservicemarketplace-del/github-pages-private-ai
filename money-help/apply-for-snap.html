How to Apply for SNAP (Food Stamps)

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Program rules and amounts change and vary by state; check the official source before you apply.

SNAP (food stamps) helps households buy groceries with an EBT card. You apply through your state's SNAP agency, and eligibility depends on income, household size, and some expenses.

Looking for your state? See Apply for SNAP (Food Stamps): All 50 States.

How to apply

  1. Find your state's SNAP office or online application.
  2. Apply as soon as you can. Benefits are generally counted from your application date.
  3. Complete an interview, usually by phone.
  4. Send proof of identity, income, and expenses such as rent and utilities.
  5. Ask about expedited SNAP if you have very little income or money. Some households get benefits within 7 days.

2026 work rule changes

Since early 2026, adults 18 to 64 without qualifying exemptions must generally work, train, or volunteer 80 hours a month, or they can only get SNAP for 3 months in a 3-year period. Parents are exempt only if their youngest child is under 14. Veterans, people experiencing homelessness, and former foster youth no longer get automatic exemptions. Rules for some non-citizens also changed. Ask your state office what applies to you.

After a disaster, see Disaster SNAP (D-SNAP). If you're denied, see appealing a benefits denial.

Questions this guide answers

Who has to meet SNAP work requirements in 2026?

Generally adults 18 to 64 without an exemption must work, train, or volunteer 80 hours a month. Parents are exempt only if their youngest child is under 14.

How fast can I get SNAP?

Households with very little income or money may qualify for expedited SNAP, often within 7 days. Otherwise states generally decide within 30 days.
