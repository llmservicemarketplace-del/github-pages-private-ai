Free and Reduced-Price School Meals

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Program rules and amounts change and vary by state; check the official source before you apply.

Through the National School Lunch and School Breakfast Programs, children can get free meals if household income is at or below 130% of the federal poverty guidelines, and reduced-price meals at or below 185%.

Who qualifies automatically

  • Children in households getting SNAP, TANF, or (in many states) Medicaid.
  • Children experiencing homelessness, foster children, runaway youth, and migrant children.
  • Students at schools using community eligibility, where all students eat free.

How to apply

  1. Get the application from your child's school or district website. You can apply any time during the school year.
  2. One application usually covers all children in the household at the same district.
  3. Report changes: if your income drops mid-year, apply again.

Many areas also run free summer meal sites, and some states give families summer grocery benefits for kids. Call 211 or ask your school.

Free local help

Call or text 211, or visit 211.org, to find local programs for bills, food, housing, and more. You can also browse our free resource directory.

Official and nonprofit sources

  • USDA: National School Lunch Program

Need help sorting out bills or applications? Contact NFCcop for free guidance.

Questions this guide answers

What income qualifies for free school lunch?

Household income at or below 130% of the federal poverty guidelines qualifies for free meals, and up to 185% for reduced-price meals.

Can I apply for free school meals in the middle of the year?

Yes. You can apply any time, for example if your income drops.
