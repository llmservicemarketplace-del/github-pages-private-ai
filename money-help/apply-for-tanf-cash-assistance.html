How to Apply for TANF Cash Assistance

Last reviewed November 15, 2026. General information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Program rules and amounts change and vary by state; check the official source before you apply.

TANF (Temporary Assistance for Needy Families) gives monthly cash help to low-income families with children. States run the program under their own names and set their own rules and payment amounts.

Find welfare and TANF benefits in your state (USA.gov)

Who may qualify

  • Families with a child under 18, and pregnant women in many states.
  • Households with low income and few resources, as your state defines them.
  • Residents of the state where you apply. Citizenship and immigration rules also apply.

What to expect

  • Most adults must take part in work, job search, or training activities.
  • Federal law limits most families to 60 months of TANF in a lifetime. Some states set a shorter limit.
  • You are usually asked to cooperate with child support enforcement.
  • TANF often connects you to other help, such as child care and job training.

How to apply

  1. Contact your state or county human services office. Many states take applications online.
  2. Complete the application and an interview.
  3. Turn in the documents the office asks for by the deadline it gives you.
  4. Ask about emergency or one-time cash help if you are facing eviction or a shutoff.

What to have ready

  • ID and Social Security numbers for household members.
  • Birth certificates or other proof of each child's age and relationship to you.
  • Proof of income and of any child support you receive.
  • Proof of address and of rent and utility costs.
  • Bank statements, if your state counts resources.

Free local help

Call or text 211, or visit 211.org, to find local programs for bills, food, housing, and more. You can also browse our free resource directory.

Official and nonprofit sources

Questions this guide answers

Is TANF the same as welfare?

TANF is the federal program most people mean by welfare or cash assistance. Each state gives its program its own name.

How long can I get TANF?

Federal law generally limits a family to 60 months in a lifetime, and some states allow less. Ask your state office how the limit applies to you.
