How to Apply for Medicaid

Last reviewed November 8, 2026. General information from The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Program rules and amounts change and vary by state; check the official source before you apply.

Medicaid is free or low-cost health coverage for people with low income, and for many children, pregnant women, older adults, and people with disabilities. Each state runs its own program, and rules vary. You can apply any time of year.

Looking for your state? See Apply for Medicaid: All 50 States.

How to apply

  1. Apply through your state Medicaid agency or HealthCare.gov.
  2. Have income, household, and ID information ready.
  3. Respond quickly to any request for documents.
  4. Children may qualify for CHIP even if parents don't qualify for Medicaid.

New work rules coming

Under federal law, many adults ages 19 to 64 covered through the Medicaid adult group will need to show 80 hours a month of work, community service, a work program, or half-time school, or monthly income of at least $580, starting no later than January 1, 2027. Some states may start earlier.

Exemptions include pregnant and postpartum people, parents and caregivers of a child 13 or younger or of a disabled person, medically frail people, veterans with a total disability rating, former foster youth, American Indians and Alaska Natives, and some others. Keep your contact information current with your state so you don't miss notices.

If you're denied or cut off, you can ask for a fair hearing. See appealing a benefits denial.

Call or text 211, or visit 211.org, to find local programs for bills, food, housing, and more. You can also browse our free resource directory.

Questions this guide answers

When do Medicaid work requirements start?

Generally no later than January 1, 2027, for many adults ages 19 to 64 in the Medicaid adult group. Some states may start earlier.

Can I apply for Medicaid any time?

Yes. Medicaid and CHIP accept applications all year.
