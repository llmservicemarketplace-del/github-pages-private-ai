Surplus Property for Nonprofits and Local Governments

Last reviewed November 14, 2026. Written by The New Faith Church and Co-op, Inc. (NFCcop), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit educational resource. General information only, not legal advice. We are not a government agency and we charge nothing for these guides.

Short answer: Eligible nonprofits and public agencies can receive federal surplus property through their state agency for surplus property, paying a service charge instead of the market price. Call your state's agency from the list below and ask for the eligibility application.

Before federal surplus is auctioned to the public, it is offered to state and local governments and to certain nonprofits. The program is called the Federal Surplus Personal Property Donation Program. Each state runs it through one office. Many of those offices also offer the state's own surplus to nonprofits and local governments before selling it to the public.

Who can qualify

  • State and local public agencies
  • Nonprofit education and public health organizations, including programs for people who are homeless or impoverished
  • Nonprofit and public programs for older adults
  • Public airports and certain other special categories

The state agency makes the eligibility decision. Expect to show your IRS determination letter, describe the program the property will be used for, and name the people allowed to pick up property for you.

How it works

  1. Call your state agency and ask for the eligibility application.
  2. Get approved and receive a donee account.
  3. Look for property. The agency has a warehouse or online inventory and can search federal listings for items you need.
  4. Pay the service charge and arrange pickup.
  5. Use it. Put the property into use within one year and keep using it for at least one year. Keep records.

Other ways nonprofits get equipment

  • State surplus: ask the same office whether nonprofits can buy state surplus before public sale.
  • Local governments: cities, counties, and school districts can often donate or sell surplus to nonprofits directly. Ask the purchasing office.
  • Public auctions: nonprofits can bid like anyone else. See federal surplus auctions and city and county surplus auctions.

Common questions

Can a nonprofit get federal surplus property?

Some can. Federal law lets eligible nonprofits, such as those running education or public health programs, programs for older adults, or services for people who are homeless or impoverished, receive federal surplus through their state agency for surplus property. The state agency decides eligibility.

Is federal surplus free for nonprofits?

The property is donated, but the state agency charges a service fee to cover its costs for handling and transport. The fee is usually well below market value.

Are there rules after we receive the property?

Yes. The organization generally must put the property into use within one year and keep using it for at least one year. Some items carry longer restrictions. Breaking the rules can mean returning the property or paying for it.
